### The ONE weakness this run attacks, and how

**Artwork craft and genuine detail.** It has been the weakest criterion in 7 of
the last 10 runs at a mean of 6.0, and it is still the top repeat offender even
though No.32 moved the MECHANICAL half of it decisively (drawn share 99 percent,
125 drawn marks against 1 blocky, median pairwise similarity 0.227). That is the
point worth being precise about. No.32 proved the deck was not a template. It
did not prove the deck was rendered. Its own artwork ledger entry says it fired
"no SDF and no GPU path at all", and No.30 and No.31 did not either. So three
consecutive decks have argued flat, and DESIGN_DOCTRINE section 4 says plainly
that a rendered hero with a graded finish is the craft floor for hero slides.

This run attacks it on the axis the last three left alone, and it does it with
two specific commitments made HERE, at planning altitude, rather than discovered
at Phase 9:

1. **THE HERO CLIMBS THE LADDER.** The hero slide renders on akthree (GPU PBR,
   technique 87) with real materials, soft shadow maps and an akpost film grade,
   or on aksdf (88) if the story's mass is organic rather than manufactured. It
   carries a designed Canvas fallback and checks the snapshot sentinel, per the
   contract. Ramps are built in OKLCH via akcolor (90). The known trap is logged
   at 0.95 in the instincts ledger and is planned around in advance: AKPOST.grade
   costs about 34 seconds on a 2D canvas that has already had GL pixels
   composited into it and about 0.9 seconds before, so the 2D atmosphere is
   graded FIRST and the GL frame lands on top of it.
2. **DETAIL IS A HIERARCHY.** Every dossier names TWO focal clusters that get
   the micro-detail pass, and declares the rest of the frame deliberately quiet.
   This is the correction written into FIELD_NOTES on 2026-08-13, that
   DESIGN_DOCTRINE section 5's zoom test is a FLOOR on every region and not a
   BUDGET for the run's drawing effort, and that evenly worked frames read noisy
   rather than detailed. Uniform coverage is exactly what a deck scoring 6.0 for
   six weeks looks like.

**The second weakness, carried as a floor rather than as the target.**
Legibility and platform fitness has been weakest in 3 of 10 and has NEVER been
worked on, and all three of the recurring machine defects are its symptoms:
outside safe zone, top-loaded composition, tiny text (latest 2026-08-13). It is
not this run's headline attack, because the headline attack has to be the top
offender, but it gets a hard floor written into every dossier: no on-slide label
below 28 px even where the engine allows 24, every art-band label on an opaque
knockout plate by default per the 0.98 instinct, and the lower-third treatment
field filled with something that has modeled tone in it rather than a plate.

## Top instincts injected into every subagent this run

1. A generated block pasted into a run record goes stale the moment another
   round runs. Regenerate gate_status at the LAST render, not the first. (0.99)
2. Never read a machine_qa PASS as composition approval. qa.py's text-collision
   check is DOM ONLY, so any label against Canvas or SVG geometry can collide
   freely and still return PASS with zero warns. Ship art-band labels on opaque
   knockout plates. (0.99 / 0.98)
3. There is no context budget, no token budget and no remaining-budget gate in
   this routine. A constraint you cannot point at is a constraint you invented.
   Draw the next slide. (0.99)
4. Never nest a colour helper. lerpHex returns an rgb() string, feeding it back
   into a hex parser yields NaN, and canvas silently keeps the previous
   fillStyle, so a whole region renders the wrong colour with a clean gate.
   (0.97)
5. Size every plate from the MEASURED string, never a guessed constant.
   JetBrains Mono at 24 px with 0.10em tracking advances 16.8 px per character
   and the eye estimates about 14. (0.95)
