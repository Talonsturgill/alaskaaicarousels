# STORYBOARD — Alaska.Ai carousel No. 60 — 2026-09-15

## THE DIRECTORS ROOM, AND THE SYNTHESIS

Three lenses pitched in parallel. **Data-journalist** brought THE MEASURED
PLAIN, a sunlit snow flat with a steel datum rail across every frame whose
solidity encodes whether an honest comparator exists, arguing that Alaska
lives in the tail of five separate distributions. **Cartographer** brought THE
MODEL AND THE PLATES, an overhead drawn relief model of Alaska on a dark
table where the land is engraved and every quantity is a cut plate, arguing
that the order's own phrase "a comprehensive single source" specifies a
CENTER, and a center behaves differently where nearly 82 percent of
communities have no road to it. **Editorial-essayist** brought A DOOR IS NOT
A FILE, a freestanding door on open flats with nothing behind it, arguing
that the order diagnoses a records problem and will be read as an AI order.

**THE CARTOGRAPHER WINS THE DECK.** Three reasons, in order.

Its thesis is a reading of the primary document rather than a metaphor. "a
comprehensive single source" is the order's own phrase, verbatim in C07, and
a single source is a center. THE CAPTION CRITIC CAUGHT THIS ROOM MISQUOTING IT
as "One comprehensive single source" in the cover headline and in this thesis,
which would have put the studio's own argument inside quotation marks and
attributed it to the President. The cover now reports the phrase rather than
quoting it, and the argument sits outside the quotation marks where it belongs. The essayist's door is a good image and the
director said so itself, honestly, in its own self-critique: if the door
renders smooth, the deck is a handsome idea with thin craft, which is exactly
the 6.8 the trend check predicts. The data-journalist's tail framing is the
sharpest ARGUMENT of the three and it is also five arguments, which is one
more than a nine-slide deck can carry to the end.

Its cover is the best image available anywhere in the room. A large, lit,
fully modelled Alaska carrying one gold circle and nothing else, where the
circle is a true 500 statute mile geodesic from Anchorage and visibly fails
to cover the state. That reads in half a second at 432px with zero words, and
it is the thesis.

Its light is the most rigorous answer to the standing weakness. Key to fill
at 2.2 to 1, with a hard floor at `#44534C` below which nothing on the model
may fall, gated rather than asserted. The last four decks ran 4:1 to 6:1 and
put their detail into shadows nobody could see.

**WHAT THE OTHER TWO CONTRIBUTED, AND IT IS NOT A COURTESY.**

FROM THE DATA-JOURNALIST, SLIDE 07 ENTIRELY. The cartographer's own weakest
frame was its 07, two smooth plates comparing the VetPop count to the Census
count, and its self-critique named it. That frame is replaced by the
data-journalist's ranked-states frame, six bars in parallel projection with
the national line through them, which is a better keepable artifact and puts
Alaska's first-of-fifty fact on a frame of its own. The VetPop-versus-Census
guard the cartographer wanted moves to mono furniture on 02 and 06, where
each count is actually used.

FROM THE DATA-JOURNALIST ALSO, THE FRAME ZONE TABLE. Its single best idea was
making the type band a NO-EMIT DOMAIN for every generator rather than a
region to mask. Adopted deck-wide and stated below.

FROM THE ESSAYIST, THE RESERVE DISCIPLINE. Its instruction that the reserve is
carved out of EVERY generator's domain, not one, is adopted verbatim. The
engraver, the stipple field and the plate tooth populations all take the same
measured rect list.

**WHAT WAS CUT FROM THE WINNER AND WHY.** The cartographer's frame 05
originally argued by comparing an azimuthal equidistant projection against a
registered conic ghost, and its own self-critique said the outlines may differ
by only 12px and the frame would then be pretty and ordinary. The projection
change stays, because the mono line has to be able to say DISTANCE TRUE FROM
ONE POINT honestly. The ARGUMENT moves onto the seven true rings, which are
unambiguous at any size. The ghost outline is cut.

### SLIDE COUNT RATIONALE

Nine. The default band is 8 to 10 and the argument has exactly nine moves in
it. This run's Phase 1 refresh measured that a 10-slide document carries about
22 percent more reach than a 3-slide one because the swipe itself is the dwell,
so trimming below nine would be a reach decision rather than an editing one.
Ten was considered and rejected: the tenth slide would be a second breather,
and the deck already has a genuine one.

### VARIETY LEDGER CHECK, AND THIS DECK'S REQUIRED DIVERGENCE

The last four heroes are a printed map sheet with art inside a neatline and
type in the margin (No.56), a continuous vertical water column descended
through (No.57), a night concrete pour with a bronze monument disc (No.58),
and a cargo hold at altitude loaded by a repeated unit crate (No.59).

**The nearest and most dangerous is No.56.** Its defining law was art inside a
neatline and ALL type in the margin outside it. This deck **has no neatline and
no margin**. The art runs to all four edges and every string of type sits on
the model or in the model's own sea, defended by a reserve carved out of each
generator's domain before a stroke exists. The composition law is inverted
rather than varied, and `aksheet.js` is not loaded anywhere in this deck.

No.57 is one continuous medium on one shared vertical mapping; this is nine
discrete overhead plan views on one shared AREAL mapping, with no depth axis
and no descent. No.58 is a near-ground object hero at about 400mm; this is
nadir on every frame. No.59 is an interior with a repeated unit; this is an
exterior copy stand and its repeated marks are ticks and dots, never crates.

**The binding read beyond the literal list was that three of the last four are
near-black interiors lit by one hard key, which is why artwork craft sits at
6.8.** This deck answers it with a number rather than a promise. Key to fill is
**2.2 to 1**, the lowest this page has shipped, and `#44534C` is a hard floor
below which nothing on the model may fall. A stroke inside a cast shadow on
this deck is still a visible stroke.

Atmospheres diverge: not first dark under new ice, not night on a fresh pour,
not a near-black hold. This is thin overcast on a copy stand, late morning.
Palette diverges: lichen plaster and open shadow, a green-grey mid-value
subject, against new ice over black water, night pour and struck bronze, and
the hold at altitude. Type diverges: Bricolage Grotesque over Fraunces over
JetBrains Mono, which is neither forbidden pairing and has never shipped here.
Hook archetype is NEW: THE SPECIFICATION, DRAWN, which is not the paired
readout, not the half vote and not the two sums.

### THE LIGHT, DECLARED ONCE FOR ALL NINE FRAMES

```
azimuth       305 degrees   (upper left)
elevation      28 degrees
key to fill   2.2 : 1
sky dome      12 percent from directly overhead
cast bearing  125 degrees (lower right), length 1.88 x object height
```

Elevation 28 sits inside `akengrave`'s legible swell band of 20 to 30, so
stroke thickness spreads across its full range and the lay models rather than
hatches. Azimuth 305 is the cartographic northwest convention, which is why the
relief reads as raised rather than sunken.

**REVISED AT THE SECOND RECONCILIATION, and the revision is the point.** This
section originally specified a SCREENED lit pool at every object's foot, in the
order pool, cast, seam, with the pool centre at `foot + 14` and its width 70 to
110px. That is what got built, and `qa.py` was right to call it: a screened
radial at a foot is a lamp with nothing in the picture emitting it, which is the
defect this studio's own ledger committed a fix for on No.59 and this deck
reopened on five frames. It survives here as a record of what was planned.

What shipped instead: every frame that stands something on the ground LAYS A
FLOOR, one ground plane per frame drawn as a lay of fine rules at a 1.15px pitch,
swelling and thinning along the declared light axis, feathered out before it
reaches any furniture row, and nothing is painted at any foot. The cast is an
ATTACHED subtraction from THAT floor, thrown down-light, with the hard narrow
seam drawn last at the object's own foot. Contact order is floor, cast, seam,
always, and the floor belongs to the frame rather than to the object.

**Honest expectation, stated before the build.** A 2.2 to 1 deck measures a
SMALLER contact dL than a 6:1 deck, by physics. Target dL 6 to 10 against the
4.0 floor, measured with `scripts/contact_probe.py`, never computed. If a probe
comes back at 4.5 the answer is a wider pool, not a darker deck.
