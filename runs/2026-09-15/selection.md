# SELECTION — Alaska.Ai carousel No. 60 — 2026-09-15

## THE STORY

**Executive Order 14426 of September 8th, "Accelerating Access to Veterans'
Benefits and Employment Opportunities," published in the Federal Register on
September 11th as document 2026-18738, orders the Secretaries of War and
Veterans Affairs to "create and deploy new digital tools using artificial
intelligence and other emerging capabilities to streamline veterans'
applications" within 180 days. Alaska has the highest veteran share of any
state.**

The order runs to four sections and mentions artificial intelligence once. It
carries three clocks: 30 days for prompt sharing of military personnel and
medical records, 120 days for the Secretaries of War and Veterans Affairs to
act on information technology, and 180 days to stand up updated IT systems,
policy guidance and the AI tools themselves. Signed September 8th, those land
on October 8th, January 6th and March 7th.

The Alaska argument is a per-capita one and it has to be written as such.
Nothing in the order names a state. What makes it an Alaska story is that the
population the order acts on is a larger share of Alaska than of anywhere
else, and that the alternative to a digital tool, in most of this state, is an
airplane. An AI-first benefits pipeline is not a uniform national change. It
lands hardest where the counter is furthest away.

3. **Tangibility.** A claim, a form, a clock.
4. **Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** A veteran, a VSO, a
   legislative staffer and anyone in the delegation's casework office, yes.

## THE OTHER THING THAT NEARLY WON, AND THE HONEST REASON IT DID NOT

**NMFS's draft 2027 Annual Deployment Plan for Observers and Electronic
Monitoring**, posted September 9th to the North Pacific Council's agenda as
item C1 for the October 5th to 13th Anchorage meeting. Inside it, one of five
electronic monitoring development projects NMFS intends to support in 2027 is
"Advancing Fisheries Monitoring: Scaling AI tools to Modernize Data
Collections and Analytical Practices of Electronic Monitoring Videos."

On the merits this was the best-looking deck on the board. The AI hook is
explicit, named, and inside a document that is entirely about Alaska. The
numbers are extraordinary and all primary: a $5.45 million preliminary budget,
$732,000 of it for trawl electronic monitoring and $4.72 million for the rest,
up to 181 vessels in the fixed-gear selection pool, 2,742 full-coverage trips
against 1,404 partial-coverage trips, and observer selection rates running
from 9 percent to 47 percent across four strata. It carries a genuine reader
deadline, November 1st, for vessels to opt in or out. And the picture writes
itself, because a coverage rate is a density of marks and a reader gets that
at thumb size with no words at all.

It lost on the gate, and the gate is right. `dedupe_check` returns a LIKELY
DUPLICATE against No.52 (2026-09-06, ten days) at jaccard 0.173, sharing four
entities and eight keywords. No.52 is titled "Two Instruments, One Line" and
its second instrument IS this Annual Deployment Plan; it pointed forward to
exactly this committee review. The 2027 draft landing three days later is a
real new fact, but it is a new fact inside a deck this page has already run.
Shipping it would also make four NOAA or NMFS decks in sixteen runs, after
No.44's Chukchi glider, No.52's survey framework and No.57's incidental
harassment authorization. The 30-day dedupe rule exists to force a genuinely
distinct story at daily cadence, and today has one.
