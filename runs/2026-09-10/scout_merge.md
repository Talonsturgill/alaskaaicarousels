## THE SELECTED CANDIDATE (see selection.md for the decision)

### Anchorage bought the room before it wrote the rules

WHAT IS ON THE RECORD
- $598,998.10 approved for a ThunderCat Technology contract to relocate and
  expand the Real Time Crime Center, paid from federal asset-forfeiture funds.
- The vote was 8 to 4. Sydney Scout, Keith McCormick, Daniel Volland and
  George Martinez opposed.
- $11.8 million was approved for the underlying system in 2025. Supporters of
  the ordinance put total Anchorage police surveillance spending at
  $12.4 million.
- The center is built to pull in as many as 750 camera feeds. 238 cameras were
  registered as of August 27th, 11 of them integrated for direct APD access,
  against about 15 operational at the September 2025 launch and 216 registered
  a week earlier on August 20th. More than 250 municipal traffic cameras feed
  it.
- Retention today is POLICY, not law. Automated license plate reader records
  are held 14 days. Traffic camera footage overwrites on a 3 to 5 day cycle.
- Alaska has ZERO Flock Safety cameras against about 120,000 nationwide, per
  DeFlock data cited by ADN. Anchorage's system is locally built rather than
  bought off the national shelf, which is why an Anchorage rule is the rule.
- AO 2026-108, sponsored by McCormick, Scout and Vice Chair Volland, would
  require Assembly pre-approval for surveillance technology contracts, bar
  adding AI with human-feature recognition without Assembly approval, require
  a case number before live-feed access, set two-week retention unless the
  footage is evidence, and require a warrant before footage goes to out-of-state
  or federal partners.
- Public testimony on AO 2026-108 is September 15th, 2026.

THE TWO SENTENCES THAT ARE THE ARGUMENT
- Police Chief Sean Case: "Code in and of itself is not going to be able to keep
  up with how agile a police department needs to be in a modern world."
- ACLU of Alaska policy director Mike Garvey: "Laws have more permanence and are
  more resistant to change."
- Assembly Vice Chair Daniel Volland, answering Case: "I find that disturbing.
  I don't think we need to be putting the cart before the horse."

THE SAME WEEK, THE SAME DEPARTMENT, THE OTHER DIRECTION (Beat F finding 2)
The Alaska Landmine posted video on August 28th of Chief Case addressing the
Assembly about video surveillance. On August 31st APD's director of community
relations filed a copyright claim with Facebook and the video came down after
passing 60,000 views. The Landmine appealed, got no answer, emailed four
officials on September 2nd with no reply, published on September 4th, and the
claim was withdrawn. APD's statement conceded the use "may be covered under a
valid copyright exception."

That is the thesis in one anecdote. An automated takedown moved in three days
on one municipal email. A rule governing the surveillance room has been pending
since August 20th and is still pending. The showrunner is handing this to the
directors room as an OPTIONAL slide, because it is a different subject matter
(copyright, press access) and could dilute a tight governance deck. The room
decides.

RISK, STATED PLAINLY
The ordinance's own text could not be fetched. muni.org and
anchorage.legistar.com both refuse automated retrieval and have no known route,
which is a standing entry in config/sources.yaml. AO 2026-108's number and its
operative provisions are corroborated by FOUR independent outlets (ADN twice,
Alaska Public Media twice, Alaska's News Source, Must Read Alaska) but not by
the primary municipal document. EVERY slide that states what the ordinance would
do must attribute it to the reporting rather than to the ordinance. The
fact-checker is told this explicitly.

Two sources disagree on the vote night because the Assembly meeting spanned
September 1st to 3rd. The honest form is "the September 1st meeting, decided
September 3rd."

Camera counts move week to week (216 on August 20th, 238 on August 27th). Any
count on a slide carries its own date.

## RUNNER-UP 1 — DeepGreen's underwater data center, FERC comment window

Found by Beat A, Beat D and Beat F, all three. FERC accepted DeepGreen Cook
Inlet SPV, LLC's preliminary permit application, notice at 91 FR 56879 on
September 4th, Project No. P-15423-000. Roughly 100 MW, 66 subsea compute hives,
330 to 350 tidal turbines, 1,650 acres of seabed west of Nikiski in 45 to 166
feet of water, comments due 5:00 p.m. Eastern November 2nd. Alaska Energy
Authority CEO Curtis Thayer says AEA was never contacted and that the filing
gets facts about AEA's own $400 million Cook Inlet PowerLink wrong. Kenai City
Manager Terry Eubank was not contacted either.

NOT SELECTED, for three reasons that compound. It is a federal-document story
and four of the last twelve decks were federal documents, which the plan named
as a variety problem. It is ALREADY an item on this publication's own docket,
updated today, so a deck would be re-telling a tracker entry. And its clock runs
53 days, against the selected story's 5, so it keeps.

## RUNNER-UP 2 — The September 1st NSF cohort

Beat B and Beat C both found it. Eight NSF awards to University of Alaska
campuses share a start date of September 1st, 2026, totalling $8,156,411, and
three of the eight are AI-tagged carrying $4,787,612, which is 58.7 percent of

the fiscal year closes on September 30th and the denominator stops moving.
