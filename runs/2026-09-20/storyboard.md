# DECK HEADER

## Thesis
Notice of the August 23rd cloud seeding over the southern Kenai went ten days
up to a federal desk and sideways to a state agency, and never down to the
borough underneath, so the borough answered with the only rule it can enforce,
a ten question form that reaches its own land and asks nothing about the model
that picked the cloud.

**PDF document title (34 chars):** `Every Notice Went Up`

## Arc, and where the temperature shifts
1. COVER, the direction claim. Cold, wide, nothing visibly wrong.
2. The three risers. Notice happened, and it went up. Register stays neutral.
3. Whose ground. The first structural turn, still cool.
4. The honest scale. BREATHER beat. Temperature deliberately DROPS here, and
   this is where DEC's no violation finding sits, so a reader's temperature
   can't climb on an unearned grievance.
5. The blind port. THE TURN, and the deck's emotional centre.
6. The ten questions. Keepable, dense, procedural.
7. The eleventh position. THE PAYOFF, the sharpest frame in the deck.
8. What anyone can reach. Synthesis, widest section, coolest again.
9. The close. One ask, one date, one room.

## Slide count rationale
Nine. The house cadence (hook, pay, point, breather, turn, data, point,
synthesis, close) maps onto this argument with nothing left over and nothing
forced. Eight would cost either the honest scale beat or the fairness beat,
and both are load bearing. Ten would split the form across two frames and
break the keepable slide, which is the thing a reader saves.

## Variety ledger check, and this deck's required divergence
Last four hero structures: No.60 nine nadir plan views on one shared areal
mapping with quantities as cut plates; No.61 a continuous receding snow fence
seen from nine places; No.62 a 9,720 px lateral apron panorama at constant
camera height; No.63 one machined instrument on a bench in parallel oblique.

**THIS DECK DIVERGES STRUCTURALLY, NOT MERELY IN SUBJECT.** All four of the
above are an object or a surface under a camera, lit by one key, seen from a
station, and the narrative advances because the STATION moves. This deck has
no station. An orthographic section is a projection rather than a view, the
reader is not standing anywhere, the frame behaves as a drawing instrument
with a printed datum and a printed scale, and the narrative advances because
the CUTTING SCALE changes and the ground moves against a datum that never
does. Against No.60 specifically, the only plan view in the deck is a 180 px
locator key that is annotation furniture, and the entire finding lives in the
vertical axis a plan view deletes.

Atmospheres of the last three, all avoided: lichen plaster and open shadow;

## THE KEY IS ON THE RIGHT, and every fitting now agrees (round two)
Azimuth 205 gives `lightX = -sin(205) = +0.42`, so the chassis throws every
cast to the LEFT and the key stands on the screen right. Every lathed brass
fitting in the first build carried its bright stop on the upper LEFT, which

## IGN dither is on, and the film grade is NOT (round two)
The risk register named IGN dither as this deck's mitigation for a 900 px sky
ramp, and it was running on no frame. `AKPOST.grade` now runs on all nine with
`dither: true, filmic: false`. The second half of that is load bearing: akpost
defaults `filmic` to true, so a call that asks only for dither silently runs an
ACES curve over the whole frame and moves every value the run has already
measured for contrast, contact and encoding. It cost slide 06 nine
label-crossing failures in a single render before the default was found. The
dossiers' `akpost` bullets are honoured as dither only, and the graded look
they describe is NOT claimed.
