# SELECTION — carousel No. 64, 2026-09-20

## THE DECISION

**The Kenai Peninsula Borough's answer to the Homer cloud seeding, and the
jurisdiction it does not reach.** Shipped as an EXPLICIT UPDATE to No.64's
predecessor No.42 (2026-08-27), stated on the cover.

On September 15th the Kenai Peninsula Borough Assembly took up two measures
from Mayor Peter Micciche, both written in response to Rainmaker Technology
Corporation's drone cloud seeding over the southern Kenai Peninsula in August.
The Assembly ADOPTED Resolution 2026-046, 7 Yes to 0 No with 2 absent, asking
state lawmakers and the governor to ban or regulate weather modification and
geoengineering, and asking Alaska's congressional delegation to strengthen
federal law. It INTRODUCED Ordinance 2026-35, enacting KPB 17.10.165, which
would create a borough permit whose application must carry ten named items.
Its public hearing and final vote are set for October 20th.

## WHY THIS ONE

**1. Concrete Alaska impact.** A borough is writing an enforceable rule with a
published hearing date a reader can attend. That is this publication's entire
product, a decision and whether you get a say, and most days it has to go
looking for one.

**2. The frame is structural rather than partisan, which is the house voice.**
The story is not that a company broke a law. It broke none, and it did give
notice to Alaska DNR, which answered with a letter of no objection, and
federal law requires a filing at least ten days ahead under 15 CFR 908.4.
CORRECTED IN PHASE 8: an earlier draft of this file said the notice went
ten days ahead TO NOAA. No claim in this run verifies that the filing was
made. C22 and C23 are the statute and the rule, which is a requirement; C20
is the DNR letter, which is a fact. The deck says the requirement and never
the filing, and a reply must do the same. Every one of those requirements
points UP. None of them points DOWN at the borough underneath. The drones flew
over STATE land, so no borough permission was needed. The Assembly's two
measures are the exact shape of that constraint, and the borough's own attorney
wrote why, that a second class borough has no authority to fully regulate this
or the airspace. The one it could pass by itself is a request that somebody
else act. The one it can enforce reaches only borough land. Nobody is a villain
and the geometry is the argument.

**3. The AI thread is real, primary on both sides, and the sharpest thing in
the story.** The state's own letter says Rainmaker's materials describe using
"drones, atmospheric observations, weather radar, and modeling to identify
clouds and atmospheric conditions that may be suitable for seeding" (C31). The
ordinance's own text lists ten required items, every one of them about
chemicals, flares, pyrotechnic mass, safety sheets, frequency, altitude,
duration or coordinates (C27). **Not one asks about the modeling that picks the
moment.** Both halves are primary documents, which is what makes this a finding
rather than a framing. Note the Stratus claim from No.42 is deliberately NOT
used: rainmaker.com no longer names it and it could not be re-verified today.

**4. Visual potential is unusually strong for the standing weakness.** The
geometry is a real land-ownership boundary, which is a MODELED SURFACE rather
than a chart, and it is exactly the kind of subject the run's artwork-craft
attack needs. The quantity contrast is vivid and honest, about 374 grams of
silver iodide against one hundredth of an inch of rain, both of them Rainmaker's
own modeled estimate and neither a measurement. There is a permit form with ten
numbered fields and an eleventh that nobody wrote, which is a keepable data
slide that draws itself.

## WHAT THE CLAIMS PHASE RESOLVED (written after, so the record is not left wrong)

All five went to the fact-checker. Three came back differently from the way
this section originally framed them, and the frame was mine, so it is corrected
here rather than quietly dropped.

1. **The federal law. THERE WAS NO CONFLICT BETWEEN THE TWO OUTLETS.** This
   section originally staged one, on the strength of a summary of the Alaska
   Public Media piece. The fact-checker fetched that page twice and it reads the
   same as KDLL, that the Weather Modification Reporting Act requires companies
   to inform NOAA BEFORE modifying the weather. The deck must not manufacture a
   dispute that is not on the page.
   What the statute and the rule actually say is better than the dispute would
   have been. **15 U.S.C. 330a** bars any person from engaging in weather
   modification without submitting reports to the Secretary of Commerce and lets
   the Secretary require them "before, during, and after". **15 CFR 908.4**
   fixes the lead time at **at least 10 days before** the activity begins, filed
   with the NOAA Administrator. DEC names the shape of the gap exactly, that the
   federal framework "is not a comprehensive federal permitting program". So
   notice is required, it is in advance, nobody has to approve it, and it goes
   UP to a federal agency rather than sideways to the borough. C22, C23, C24.

2. **The meeting date was September 15th**, not the 17th. The borough's own
   agenda is headed Tuesday, September 15th, Legistar returns no Assembly
   meeting on the 16th or 17th, and the signed resolution is dated the 15th.
   Neither outlet ever printed a wrong date; both printed no date at all. C01.

3. **The numbers exist and the ordinance text was reached.** Ordinance 2026-35
   enacting KPB 17.10.165, companion Resolution 2026-046. The ordinance's own
   .docx on the borough's Legistar record parses when unzipped, so the deck's
   central image is PRIMARY rather than single-sourced to coverage: **ten**
   required application items, not the four the stories summarise. C27 to C30,
   C38.

4. **October 20th is confirmed** and is carried inside the ordinance's own
   agenda title, "(Hearing on 10/20/26)". Three other ordinances on the same
   agenda read 10/06/26, so the borough distinguishes the two October dates
   deliberately. It stays a `deadline` on the docket because it is the point at
   which a reader can speak to it. C10.

5. **Whose land, and a nuance that improves the deck.** The drones flew over
   state land, so no borough permission was needed. C21. But Rainmaker **did**
   tell a government: DEC's letter records that it contacted Alaska DNR before
   operating and received a **letter of no objection**. C20. So the honest frame
   is not that nobody was told. It is that **every notice requirement pointed
   up, and none of them pointed down at the borough underneath.** The borough's
   own attorney wrote why, that as a second class borough it "does not have
   authority to fully regulate weather modification activities or airspace
   rights". C12.

**THE THESIS, AS CORRECTED BY THE EVIDENCE.** Notice was given. It went ten
days ahead to NOAA, and to DNR, and it never reached the borough. The borough's
answer is the two things a second class borough can do, a resolution asking
somebody else to act, and a rule on the only land it controls. And the rule's
ten questions are about chemicals, flares, pyrotechnics, altitude and
coordinates, while the state's own letter says it is **modeling** that picks
the clouds and the moment. C31. Nothing on the form asks about that.

Two things the deck must carry for fairness. DEC found no violation and no
adverse impact, C32. And Rainmaker told DEC that talking to state officials
beforehand would have been appropriate, C33, which is the company conceding the
point rather than the page scoring one.

One thing the deck will NOT use. The count of how many states have passed
weather modification laws. The company's chief executive says 31 considered and
two passed; the borough's own resolution cites Tennessee, which is not in that
two. They are counting different things and the deck has no need of either.

# PHASE 5 — THE DIRECTORS ROOM, AND WHAT THE SHOWRUNNER TOOK

Three lenses, chosen as a trio that had not run together: **cartographer**,
**systems-illustrator**, **field-documentarian**. All three returned complete
treatments with their own self-critiques, and all three critiques were
accurate, which made the synthesis easy to reason about.

## The three pitches, in one line each

- **CARTOGRAPHER, "THE JURISDICTION SECTION".** One vertical section cut
  along a Homer to Soldotna transect, a ground datum fixed at y 960 on all
  nine frames, modeled earth below and the flown air above, with the section's
  SPAN pulling back from about 4 km to about 350 km and then collapsing to one
  20 m room at the close. A gold notice path leaves through the top of every
  frame and only comes down on the last slide.
- **SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, "THE CUT BLOCK".** A sectioned sand-cast aluminium
  manifold seen at 1:1 with no camera, no ground and no silhouette. Three
  bores exit the top edge; one boss at the base is cast and never drilled.
- **FIELD-DOCUMENTARIAN, "THE UPWARD FRAME".** Nine zenith hemispheres, the
  camera pointing straight up from about five and a half feet, printed through
  an aperture cut in an engraved plate whose SHAPE changes with every beat.

## THE DECISION: the cartographer's section is the spine

It wins on four things the rubric actually weighs.

1. **The artwork IS the argument's geometry.** The finding is a DIRECTION,
   that notice went up and the borough's reach goes down and stops. A section
   is the only one of the three where the vertical axis carries that natively.
   The systems pitch says the same thing in metal, and the documentarian pitch
   says it by pointing at the sky, but only the section lets a reader measure
   it against a printed datum.
2. **Alaska authenticity is safe.** A true transect on committed geodata, the
   canonical conic equal area projection at parallels 55 and 65 rotated 154,
   a real locator key, real place names. The systems director named this as
   the fatal flaw in the strongest image in the room, and was right to. A cast
   manifold with no Kenai in it risks the rubric's "national story with an
   Alaska sticker", and a stranger guessing the story from the art alone would
   guess hydraulics.
3. **It makes this run's standing weakness geometrically impossible.** The
   earth slab occupies the bottom band of every frame by construction, so the
   dead lower third that has been named in six scored runs cannot occur, and
   the `data-breather` escape is spent zero times.
4. **Nine frames that cannot read as one frame.** The earth mass swings from
   14 to 46 percent of frame height and the section span moves through four
   orders of magnitude, which is a filmstrip answer planned at treatment time
   rather than discovered at the contact sheet.

## THE ORGANS GRAFTED IN, and why each one beat the spine's own version

**From the SYSTEMS pitch, THE BLIND PORT.** The single best image produced in
the room, and the spine had nothing as good at the turn. A boss that was cast
and never drilled, centre punched, with a gold scribed circle marking a
through bore that does not exist and solid material behind it. It is grafted
into the cartographer's world rather than borrowed whole: on the turn slide
the notice path's downward stub ends in exactly that blind port, incised into
the borough's own ground at the borough's position on the transect. The
systems director's metaphor becomes the section's one modeled fitting.

**From the SYSTEMS pitch, THE ELEVENTH POSITION, SCRIBED AND NEVER CUT.** Two
directors arrived at this independently, which is the strongest signal the
room produced. Ten cut things and an eleventh outlined and never opened,
labelled with what it would have asked.

**From the SYSTEMS pitch, THE HAIRLINE AUDIT.** A build time enumeration of
every `stroke()`, `strokeRect()` and 1px SVG path on the art canvas, with the
hairline token permitted ONLY on extension lines, the locator outline and the
neatline. Anything else on a load bearing feature is a rebuild rather than a
note. This is the run's standing weakness turned into a check, and it is
better than the prose rule the plan started with.

**From the DOCUMENTARIAN pitch, THE FILM THAT WAS NEVER MEASURED.** The best
continuity device anyone proposed. One water mark per frame, never a chorus,
whose state carries C17, that every rainfall figure here is a modeled estimate
and not water measured at the ground. Present as a real film on the rail,
then redrawn as a dashed phantom tagged MODELED, NOT MEASURED, then absent.
It turns the deck's most important hedge from a footnote into a thing a reader
watches change. The spine's own palette arc was the weaker second device and
is demoted to a supporting note.

**From the DOCUMENTARIAN pitch, WHERE THE FAIRNESS SITS.** Its instruction to
place DEC's no violation finding (C32) and Rainmaker's own concession (C33)
INSIDE the beat where a reader's temperature would otherwise rise, rather than
in a footnote at the end. Adopted exactly.

## What was deliberately NOT taken
- The zenith hemispheres. Beautiful, genuinely novel, and in direct conflict
  with a section. One or the other.
- The systems pitch's rack in the lower band. The earth slab already solves
  that band, and a second device there would be clutter wearing a belt.
- The manifold metaphor as the deck's world, for the authenticity reason above.

## The cover, synthesized rather than picked
The cartographer offered "19 FLARES OVER THE KENAI. EVERY NOTICE POINTED UP."
The systems director offered the sharper verb, "EVERY NOTICE WENT UP. NONE
CAME DOWN." The documentarian offered the most human line but at 12 words.
The deck takes the systems director's two beat display, which is seven words
and a direction a thumb can check against the picture in one glance, and moves
the cartographer's concrete anchor into the sub-line where it does not cost
display size.

**HOOK ARCHETYPE, NEW: THE VECTOR.** The cover claims the DIRECTION of an
obligation rather than its size, its contradiction or its absence. It is not
the center and the remainder, because nothing is divided. It is not the
completed contradiction, because both halves are simply true and nothing
resolves. It is not the quoted absence, because nothing is quoted.
