Who has to be told before somebody changes the weather over the southern Kenai? The answer is not the borough underneath.

On August 23rd, 19 flares put about 374 grams of silver iodide into the clouds. Federal law takes a filing at least 10 days out. Rainmaker asked Alaska DNR first and got a letter of no objection. The borough found out from the press release.

The state reviewed it and found no violation. Alaska has no law of its own and no bill among this session's 856. The borough wrote the one rule it can enforce, ten questions about chemicals, flares and coordinates, on borough land only. None asks about the modeling that picks the cloud.

The ten items as the ordinance writes them, what the state found, and the blank in the form.

The hearing is October 20th. Who belongs on that address line before the next flight?
