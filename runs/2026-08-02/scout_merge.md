### THE ARGUMENT
A public notice is the only part of a procurement most Alaskans ever see, and it
is what tells a person whether to look. Three notices published in eight days
will decide who transcribes the Legislature, the courts, the university and
every tribe in the state, who automates a DMV compliance check, and who develops
3,967 acres at Eielson. None prints the word. The state's own governor's order
prints it plainly, which proves the vocabulary exists and is available. Naming
the technology is what pulls in the terms that govern it, accuracy floors, audit
rights, data residency, disclosure and public-records handling.

### PRECISION REQUIRED (carried into the fact-check)
The negative claim is about the PUBLIC NOTICE and the PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENTS, not
about the full solicitation packages, which are behind the state procurement
portal and SAM.gov and were NOT read. The deck must say exactly that. This is
the more defensible claim and also the better one, because the notice is the
public's only trigger to look.
