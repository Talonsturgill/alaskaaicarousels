"Leverage technology, such as artificial intelligence," says an order the governor signed last August.

Vendors have 22 days to bid on one statewide contract to convert speech, live or recorded, into written or electronic text. It closes 9:30 A.M. on 24 August. Four days earlier the DMV's window on "automated compliance and data integration systems" shuts. Neither notice prints artificial intelligence, machine learning or AI. Only the notice text was searched, not the packages behind the state portal.

Inside the deck, the notice's one sentence on the work, the eight kinds of public body it names, and the one order that prints the word.

Any of those eight can use whatever gets signed. Nobody has to ask first how the work gets done. What does a public body lose by not asking before bids close?
