# SELECTION — 2026-08-02 — Carousel No. 23

## THE STORY

**Alaska's public notices are buying machine learning without printing the word.**

Three procurement notices published in eight days, each with a deadline inside
the next three weeks, will decide who supplies machine-learning services to
Alaska institutions. None of the three public notices prints "artificial
intelligence". A fourth document, the governor's own Administrative Order 360,
prints it plainly, which proves the vocabulary exists and is available to the
state when it wants it.

- Eielson AFB, Enhanced Use Lease, four sites, 3,967 acres, proposals due
  2026-08-10. The Air Force notices say "mixed-use development".
- Statewide Transcription Services RFP 2026-0200-0064, issued 2026-07-31,
  proposals due 2026-08-24. It buys speech-to-text for the Legislature, the
  Court System, the University of Alaska, boards and commissions, and every
  city, borough, school district and federally recognized tribe in the state.
- DMV RFI 02-101-27, published 2026-07-30, responses due 2026-08-20, on
  "automated compliance and data integration systems".
- Administrative Order 360, signed 2025-08-04, tells agencies to "Leverage
  technology, such as artificial intelligence ("AI")" for permitting, with a 15
  percent regulatory cut due 2026-12-31. Its first agency plans were filed
  2026-07-27 and 2026-07-28.

## WHY THIS ONE

1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** The transcription contract alone reaches every
   branch of state government plus every borough, school district and federally
   recognized tribe. The Eielson lease puts a private developer on 3,967 acres
   of Interior ground. Both are decided this month.
2. **Visual potential.** The subject is documents, calendars, scope and a word
   that is present in one place and absent in three. That is countable geometry
   (word counts, acreage, institution fan-in, three deadlines on one August
   calendar) rather than a mood.
3. **Tangibility.** Three dates a reader can act on, all inside 22 days.
4. **Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and specifically to a
   borough clerk, a court administrator, a tribal administrator or a
   legislative staffer, all of whom are inside the transcription contract's
   scope and none of whom have been told so in AI terms.

The house voice's specialty is naming the structural problem and the missing
option. Here the structural problem is that the public notice is the only part
of a procurement most Alaskans ever see, so it is the notice, not the buried
solicitation package, that determines whether anyone who cares about machine
learning knows to look. The missing option is that naming the technology is what
pulls in the terms that govern it, accuracy floors, audit rights, data
residency, disclosure and public-records handling.

## A NOTE ON THE HOOK ARCHETYPE

The obvious hook here is an ABSENCE, the missing word. That is too close to
No.22's THE WITHHELD MAP ("an absence rendered at authoritative scale") and to
No.21's THE DEAD LETTER (two documents disagreeing). Three document-centric
absence decks in a row would be a variety failure.

So the cover leads on the CALENDAR and the REACH, which is the actionable half
and the docket's whole product, and the missing word becomes the deck's TURN in
the middle third rather than its opening move. The reader gets the thing they
can act on first and the analytical thing second.
