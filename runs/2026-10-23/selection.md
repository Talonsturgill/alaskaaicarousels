# SELECTION, 2026-10-23, No.94

## Decision

FAIRBANKS NORTH STAR BOROUGH ORDINANCE NO. 2026-42, the mayor's temporary moratorium on
land use permits for large-scale data centers, referred to the Planning Commission on
October 22nd. Framed explicitly as an UPDATE of No.76 (2026-10-03).

Why, by the four criteria:
1. Concrete Alaska impact. A binding local ordinance (not a request to Juneau) with a
   number in it: "large-scale" is 5 MW of peak demand, or more than 50,000 square feet,
   or 5 MW of onsite generation; no permits through December 31st, 2027; a six-month
   study of power, air, water and noise. In the Interior, whose utility issued its first
   Energy Emergency Alert eleven days earlier.
2. Visual potential, and it is unusually strong. The ordinance says a large data center
   is permitted today "only in the GU-1 and GU-5 General Use Districts". The borough's
   own zoning layer (gisportal.fnsb.gov, Zoning_Districts layer 1, ACRES field) puts
   those two districts at 4,617,772 of 4,763,599 zoned acres, 96.94 percent. "Only"
   means almost everywhere, while Heavy Industrial (3,555 acres) and Light Industrial
   (5,929 acres) are not on the list. That is a map the art can draw from real polygons.
3. Tangibility. Three tests, two dates, a 90-day clock, a 2005 plan.
4. Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker? The 97 percent figure is the line a Fairbanks
   planner, a GVEA engineer or an Assembly member forwards.
