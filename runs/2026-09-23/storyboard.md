# STORYBOARD — Carousel No. 66 — September 23rd

## THESIS

On September 15th the Matanuska-Susitna Borough wrote a limit on automated plate
readers into its own code, unanimously and effective the same day, for a machine the
borough has no police powers to operate. What it can actually stop is itself.

**Document title (PDF, 46 chars):** `What Mat-Su's Plate Reader Ban Actually Reaches`

(Corrected in the directors room. The earlier title asserted that the borough never
ran a plate reader, which no claim establishes. See selection.md.)

## CONTINUITY SYSTEM (three devices, >= 2 required)

**D1 THE READING FRAME.** A drawn rectangle with a chamfered lower-left corner, the shape
of a plate reader's own field of view, appears on every frame. Its STATE is what it
contains: on 01 it holds the vote count, on 02 the pipeline, on 03 it is quartered, on 04
it is empty and open to the right, on 05 it is drawn in outline only with nothing inside,
on 06 it holds five agency marks with the borough's own slot struck, on 07 it holds eight
signature rules, on 08 it is doubled and the second one is dashed, on 09 it closes to a
solid. Full state table below.

**D2 THE ENGRAVED GROUND, and it carries the argument.** Every frame's tone is built with
`AKENGRAVE` (technique 93) and each frame's `form` IS THAT FRAME'S REACH, so the lay of
the line literally wraps what the ordinance can touch. Where the ban reaches, the surface
is modelled and the crossline and interdot channels open. Where it does not, the form
falls to zero, `tone` rises past 0.45, and the surface thins to bare mainline. The reader
never has to be told; the ground gets emptier as the argument moves outward.

**D3 THE STRUCK SLOT.** One small mono field, bottom left, printing the reach in force on
that frame (`BOROUGH DEPARTMENT`, `BOROUGH CONTRACT`, `BOROUGH FUNDS`, `ANY OPERATOR`,
`NOT A BOROUGH POWER`, and so on). On the two frames where the reach fails, the slot is
struck through with a single rule. It doubles as the progress indicator's neighbour.

Edge tease is DELIBERATELY NOT USED. It has run as a continuity device on three
consecutive decks (No.62, No.63, No.65) and plan.md declares it burned.

## VARIETY LEDGER CHECK, and this deck's required divergence

Last four hero structures were the returned apron panorama (No.62), the season disc
(No.63), the jurisdiction section (No.64) and the waterline hold (No.65). This deck's
hero structure is **NEW: THE READING FRAME**, a flat-on, camera-less, engraved plate seen
square to the picture plane, with no station, no horizon and no perspective anywhere in
the deck. No.64 was also camera-less but it was a SECTION with a ground datum and a
vertical axis of jurisdiction; this is a FRONTAL PLATE whose only axis is reach outward
from the borough's own desk. Nothing in the deck is seen from anywhere.

Atmospheres of the last three were nilas studio, Kachemak stratus and flood tide overcast,
all of them WEATHER. This deck has no weather and no daylight at all: it is
**INTERIOR PLATE LIGHT**, one raking key at azimuth 118, elevation 24, chosen low because
the engraver's swell is only legible across a wide `ndotl` range and a high key gives back
a uniform hatch.

Hook archetypes of the last three were the quoted absence, the vector and the state of
matter. This deck's is **NEW: THE ENACTED LIMIT** (a rule that is already law when the
reader meets it, so the tension is not whether it passes but what it covers).

Palette families of the last three are all excluded; this one is ink and plate, below.
Type pairings of the last two are excluded; this one is below and uses neither.

## THE STANDING WEAKNESS, and what is different here

Artwork craft has been the weakest criterion in 7 of the last 10 runs, mean 6.9. The three
concrete changes, decided here and not at Phase 9:

1. The shading idiom is `AKENGRAVE` (technique 93), whose own library note records that
   artwork craft was weakest in four of the five consecutive decks that failed to use it.
   Prototyped and rendered before any slide was committed: the bench loads, `reserve`
   works, the three channels gate correctly, 1.5 s per frame.
2. `form` is a STORY QUANTITY on every frame, which the library names as the strongest use
   of this bench. Each frame prints its `SURFACE =` line with the claim id.
3. The noise term in `form` stays small. The prototype's noise dominated and the surface
   read as a contour map, which is the failure the library warns about. On the shipped
   frames the modelled solid leads and noise is texture only.

## PROTOTYPE FINDINGS, before any slide was committed

Two prototype frames were built and rendered BEFORE the directors room reported, to
de-risk the bench rather than discover it at Phase 8. Both rendered clean, zero warnings,
zero errors, about 1.5 s each.

**Proto 1, the bench alone.** `AKENGRAVE` loads, `reserve()` works against
`[data-reserve]` boxes, and the three detail channels gate exactly as documented: dense
crossline and interdots at low `tone`, bare mainline at high `tone`. The swell is legible
at elevation 24. Confirmed the idiom is worth the deck.

**Proto 2, the bench clipped to real borough geometry.** `d3.geoConicEqualArea`
`.parallels([55,65]).rotate([154,0]).fitExtent([[110,430],[970,1230]])` over a two-feature
collection puts Matanuska-Susitna and Anchorage in frame at a usable size. Engraving
inside a `Path2D` clip of each borough works, and the two read as materially different
surfaces, which is the encoding the deck needs.

**TWO DEFECTS FOUND, and both are now design rules rather than Phase 8 repairs:**

1. **A RADIALLY SYMMETRIC `form` PRODUCES A CONVERGENCE SINGULARITY.** The proto used a
   single Gaussian dome and every iso-line is a concentric ring, so the lay collapsed into
   a knot at the centre with a dark hole in it and strokes radiating out of it. This is the
   same family as the library's own one-axis warning at technique 93. **RULE: no `form` in
   this deck is radially symmetric about a point inside its own region.** Forms are
   directional, or multi-centre, or monotone across the region.
2. **A TOPOGRAPHIC-LOOKING `form` MAKES A FALSE CLAIM.** The dome read as a hill with a
   summit, which asserts elevation on a map where elevation means nothing and the borough
   has real mountains. **RULE: where the engraved surface sits inside real geography, the
   `form` must not read as terrain.** Use a form whose gradient runs across the polygon in
   a direction no reader will mistake for a landform, and say in the dossier what the
   surface means.

Both rules go into every dossier's risk flags.

## FLAT IS THE ARGUMENT, and this is the dossier arguing it

The rubric gives artwork craft a 10 only when the hero climbs the rendered ladder, GPU
PBR through akthree or an aksdf raymarched hero with a film grade, **or argues flat as a
deliberate choice in the dossier**. This deck argues flat, and the argument is not
budgetary.

The subject is a document. Specifically it is a piece of enacted code whose whole content
is a set of REACHES stated in words, adopted by seven people in a room, and printed on
paper that eight officials had already signed. There is no object, no site, no machine in
a landscape, no thing that sits on a surface. **Nothing in this story is seen from
anywhere**, and a perspective camera would have to invent a vantage the record does not
contain. The previous four decks all had one (an apron from a kneeling camera, a disc on
a bench under a key, a section through the ground, a survey station at a waterline), and
this deck's divergence is precisely that it refuses one.

So the deck is a FRONTAL PLATE: everything is square to the picture plane, at one scale,
under one raking key that exists to make the burin's swell legible rather than to place a
sun in a sky. That is the engraver's own convention and it is the right convention for a
document, because an engraving does not pretend you are standing somewhere.

**Flat does not mean unmodelled, and that distinction is the whole craft bet.** The
surface is modelled everywhere, continuously, by `AKENGRAVE`, whose lay wraps a real
`form(x,y)` and whose stroke thickness is set per stroke by the light. The depth cues
present are occlusion, the modelled surface itself, line-weight hierarchy across five
tokens, and the detail-budget gradient (crossline and interdots open only where the
surface is dark and near, and close as it recedes from the ordinance's reach). Four cues,
none of them a camera.

A consequence worth stating because it removes a whole recurring defect class: **there
are no contact shadows in this deck and every dossier says so by name**, because nothing
in it sits on anything. The two-run warn history on contact shadows is designed out rather
than repaired.

## HERO PROTOTYPE, three more defects found before the deck was coded

The room's agreed risk was that nine frames' tone comes from one bench whose documented
failure mode is a lay that collapses. So the hero's region was built and rendered THREE
TIMES before any dossier was finalized.

**Round 1 defect, NOISE IN `form` DRIVES THE LAY.** A 0.055-amplitude fbm term inside
`form` has a gradient comparable to the primary terms at these frequencies, so the
direction field followed the noise and the borough printed as a meandering river system.
It looked like terrain, which is the false claim this deck must not make.
**FIX, now a deck-wide rule: there is no noise term in any `form` in this deck. Texture
lives in `tone` only.**

**Round 2 defect, A GAUSSIAN CONE IS A RADIAL FORM.** Replacing the noise with two city
cones produced a bullseye with a dark void at its centre, the same convergence
singularity the first prototype found. Cones are radial by construction, so no amount of
`seedDeg` saves them.
**FIX, and it is better editorially than the thing it replaced: city police authority is
BOUNDED, not a hill.** Each city is a clipped chip with a LINEAR form inside it, engraved
at its own angle, which produces a dense parallel-and-crossed knot with no rings and no
void. It is also the more honest picture, because a city police department's authority
does not fall off smoothly with distance, it stops at a line.

**Round 3, the encoding now reads.** One broad, almost uniform shallow plane across the
whole borough at mainline only, and two small densely worked chips where city police
actually are. A huge boundary around an almost empty field, with the worked material in
two spots. That is the wordless claim, and it arrived from the drawing rather than from a
label.

**Three carried corrections for the build:**
1. Lean the plane a few degrees off horizontal. Dead-horizontal ruling reads as a printed
   screen rather than as a drafted plane, and it risks moire at feed scale.
2. Drop the chip radius from 54 to about 42. Wasilla and Palmer are close enough at this
   register that 54 px chips overlap and merge into one mark, which loses the count.
3. **The rail sat on bare plate below the map window, which is the six-run dead-lower-zone
   defect about to happen again.** The engraved ground must continue under the rail. Fixed
   by running a second surface pass across the bottom band rather than by moving the rail.
