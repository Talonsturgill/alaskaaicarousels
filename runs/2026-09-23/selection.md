# SELECTION — Carousel No. 66 — September 23rd

## THE STORY

**On September 15th the Matanuska-Susitna Borough Assembly adopted Ordinance Serial
No. 26-106, creating MSB 23.30, which prohibits the borough from acquiring,
installing, operating, funding or receiving data from automated license plate reader
systems. It passed unanimously, seven members, the same night as its public hearing,
and took effect on adoption. The Matanuska-Susitna Borough does not have police
powers.**

That last sentence is not a gotcha. It is the borough's own, on its own public safety
page, and it is what makes the ordinance interesting rather than routine.

## WHY THIS ONE

**(1) Concrete Alaska impact.** A named borough, a named sponsor, seven named members,
a date, an effective date, and four sections of code that now bind every borough
department. Not a proposal. Not a resolution asking someone else to act. Enacted law
in Alaska's fastest-growing borough.

**(2) Visual potential.** The ordinance is unusually drawable. An ALPR is defined in
the code text itself as a pipeline, "one or more high-speed camera units alone or
combined with computer algorithms and optical character recognition to convert images
of license plates, vehicle makes, models, colors, and unique vehicle identifiers into
computer-readable data," which is a machine with named stages the art can draw and
then cut. The four code sections are four distinct reaches. The seven-member unanimous
roll is a countable object. The jurisdiction question is a map.

**(3) Tangibility.** Everyone who drives the Parks Highway understands a camera that
reads plates.

**(4) Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to an assembly member. The
structural question at the center is one a Palmer resident can act on and one an
Anchorage resident has to act on by October 20th.

## THE ARGUMENT, and the honesty discipline around it

The deck's argument is about REACH, and it is not an accusation.

MSB 23.30 binds "any Borough department, division, employee, or agent" and any
"third-party contractor acting under contract with the Borough." It voids offending
agreements "ab initio." It forbids the borough from "accessing, querying, uploading
data to, or receiving data from any ALPR operator, network, database, or private
surveillance network."

What it does not reach is everyone who actually polices the borough. The borough's own
public safety page says it plainly: "The borough does not have police powers, however
some of our incorporated cities do, and the state troopers also patrol in our borough."
Wasilla Police, Palmer Police, Chickaloon Tribal Police, the State Troopers and the
State Wildlife Troopers are all named there and none of them is a borough department.

So the ordinance's operative reach is the borough's own machinery: the IT, Land
Management, Public Works, Emergency Services, Purchasing and Finance directors whose
signatures are on the routing slip of Information Memorandum 26-181, dated August 19th
and 20th, weeks before the vote. The strongest clause is the one nobody would have
predicted, 23.30.030, which stops the borough RECEIVING plate data it never had to
collect itself.

**THE DECK DOES NOT SAY THE ORDINANCE IS EMPTY, AND IT DOES NOT SAY IT IS A
BREAKTHROUGH.** Both readings are available and the record supports neither. What the
record supports is that a government wrote down, in advance and unanimously, a limit
on a capability it does not have, and the deck lets the reader decide what that is
worth. Fair is the house register here. Mat-Su went first on an instrument, and going
first on an instrument you do not operate is either foresight or theatre depending on
what happens next, and the deck says so without picking.

# DIRECTORS ROOM — the synthesis, and the reasoning

Three treatments came back, all complete, all specific enough to build from. The winner
is the **CARTOGRAPHER**, strengthened with named organs from the other two.

## THE TITLE WAS WRONG AND THE ROOM CAUGHT IT

Two of the three directors independently refused the title this showrunner had fixed in
the header, `The Borough That Banned a Machine It Never Ran`, on the same ground. It
asserts a negative the record does not establish. Nothing in the 38 claims says the
borough never operated a plate reader. The essayist put it best, that it is exactly the
kind of sentence the fact-checker would kill if a slide said it, so it has no business on
the cover of the PDF either.

They are right and it is corrected. **The deck title is now
`What Mat-Su's Plate Reader Ban Actually Reaches`** (46 chars). It names the subject and
states the argument, and it asserts nothing the claims do not carry.

That is what a directors room is for, and it is worth recording that the defect was the
showrunner's and the room found it.

## WHY THE CARTOGRAPHER WON

Its central device is the most original and the most honest thing anyone proposed, and it
converts the story's hardest problem into the design rather than working around it.

1. **The polygon is never filled, on any frame, at any zoom.** A choropleth would say
   "this area is banned", which is false. A stroke says "this is a boundary", which is all
   it is. The same decision doubles as the render-safety rule against a zoomed land
   polygon printing as a solid disc. One decision retires one lie and one crash.
2. **Tone is bought by REACH, not by territory.** The engraved surface exists only where
   borough machinery is, so ink density on the sheet IS the institutional quantity while
   the outline is the territorial one, and the two visibly disagree. A reader meets a very
   large line around an almost empty field before a single word explains it.
3. **The scale ladder prints a reason when there is no extent.** Every frame carries
   either a computed scale bar or a printed statement of why it can't have one.
   `NOT A PLACE` under the funds clause. `NO EXTENT` under the data clause. `1 SHEET`
   under the routing slip. `NOT ON THIS SHEET` beside the three agencies whose geometry is
   tribal or statewide and whose coordinates are not in the committed gazetteer.
4. **The thickest line in the deck is the one that binds nothing.** The borough boundary
   takes the 5.5 px hero weight and nothing else in the deck ever does. That is the
   argument stated in the weight system, and it is the kind of thing this studio's
   artwork-craft score has been missing.
5. **Slide 05 is the same projection, the same extent, the same framing as slide 01, with
   everything taken out of it.** A turn that costs nothing extra and pays enormously,
   because the reader recognizes the shape before they read the sentence.
