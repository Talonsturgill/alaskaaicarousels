## CANDIDATE 1 (LEAD) — The $272 million technology fund that bought portable X-ray machines

Beat E, confidence 0.92, primary-sourced to an Alaska state agency page.

On Friday August 7th, 2026 the Alaska Department of Health published its first
Rural Health Transformation Program funding decisions, awarding just over
$4.5 million across 19 projects out of a Year 1 federal allocation of
$272,174,856. That is under 2 percent of the money. The department says decisions
will now be published every Friday until the money is obligated, against a federal
obligation deadline in October 2026.

The program was designed by CMS around technology. Alaska's own plan names unmanned
drones, remote pharmacy dispensing units and portable diagnostic tools as things it
wants to pilot in roadless communities. Round one instead funded portable X-ray
machines, a scheduling system, remote monitoring, automated medication kiosks, a
data analytics system and EHR upgrades. No drone project and no project labeled AI
appears on the first list. The Tanana Chiefs Conference village drone pharmaceutical
delivery concept reported in June is not among the 19.

Numbers on the table: $272,174,856 Year 1 allocation; $4.5 million awarded; 19
projects; average award about $241,000; more than $267 million still undecided;
roughly 1,800 letters of interest from 864 organizations, of which 403 advanced to
full application; federal caps of 10 percent administrative, 15 percent provider
payments, 20 percent capital. Named awards include Alaska Primary Care Association
$650,200, Chugachmiut $627,200, Alaska Behavioral Health $593,100, Norton Sound
Health Corporation $292,100 and Nenana Volunteer Fire Department $50,000. Zero
projects in round one for Kodiak, the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta or the North Slope.

Quotes on the record: Mirna Estrada of the Department of Health, "That anticipated
timeline has been extended to mid-August due to emerging factors." Jared Kosin of
the Alaska Hospital and Healthcare Association, "The fundamental flaw continues to
come from the design of the program...I really do think in many ways they're being
set up to fail." Spencer Perry, a UAA economist, "The deep focus on technology
might kind of miss some of the lower-hanging fruit and more bread-and-butter public
health healthcare access policy." And from June 9th, so background rather than
in-window, Rep. Genevieve Mina, an advisory council member, "You name a program
'transformation,' but the way that the feds designed it is almost directing us to
AI."

Why it leads. The Alaska impact is concrete and enormous, the clock is live and
weekly, the AI content is a documented ABSENCE rather than an assertion, and no
deck in the 30-day window is near it. It also moves the page off a month of land
and power stories without leaving the beat.

What the record does NOT say, and the deck may not either: the published round-one
list contains no project explicitly labeled AI or drone delivery, which is not the
same as the state rejecting one; the state has not published a dollar figure for
the rural technology catalyst fund (federal rules allow up to 10 percent, roughly
$27 million for Alaska); no vendor names or model providers are disclosed; and DOH
has not published its promised comprehensive award dashboard.

## CANDIDATE 2 — NSF puts $4.74 million into Alaska campuses for AI over critical minerals

Beat C, confidence 0.90, primary-sourced to the NSF award API, and apparently
unreported anywhere else.

On August 5th, 2026 NSF recorded two linked EPSCoR Focused Collaborative awards
under a program it labels "Artificial Intelligence, Critical Minerals & Materials,
Biotechnology". $3,824,575 to the University of Alaska Anchorage (award 2614749, PI
Brandon R. Briggs) and $913,037 to the University of Alaska Fairbanks (award
2614751, PI Srijan Aggarwal), $4,737,612 combined. The project pairs microbial
leaching of rare earth elements out of coal mining waste with a reinforcement
learning bioprocess digital twin and a predictive virtual assay. Partners are
Montana Technological University and the University of Wyoming. Performance period
September 1st, 2026 to August 31st, 2030. A separate UAF award, 2626692 for
$324,995 dated August 10th, funds data-driven tuning of hybrid diesel and battery
microgrids validated on a 500 kW test bed.

Caveat that matters for the window. The two big awards are dated August 5th, one
day before the 10-day window opens. The $324,995 microgrid award on August 10th is
inside it.

What the record does not say: the abstracts name coal mining waste generically and
identify no Alaska mine or tailings pile, so nothing commits the project to Alaska
rock rather than Montana or Wyoming rock. No recovery rates, no tonnages, no
accuracy target for the digital twin, no named industry partner, no jobs number.

## CANDIDATE 3 — The land clock runs out before the rulebook exists

Beats A, D and F all landed on this independently, which is itself a salience
signal. Confidence 0.90 on the policy framing.

DNR's 41-page preliminary decision of July 10th, 2026 (ADL 234762) proposes
conveying about 19,950 acres two miles north of Houston to AIDEA at no cost under
the AS 38.05.810 public and charitable use statute, with AIDEA paying survey costs
only. AIDEA's stated vision includes "development of scalable data centers that
leverage existing and planned utility corridors". About 15,100 acres would be
reclassified to Settlement; 7,385 acres, 37 percent, are wetlands. Written comments
close 5 p.m. August 19th, and the notice states that only persons from whom the
division receives timely written comment during the period will be eligible to
appeal the final finding and decision. This showrunner re-verified that deadline and
that sentence against the state notice during Phase 3.5 today.

The policy vacuum beside it, from Beat D: SB 250, which would have required
commission-approved contracts so data center infrastructure costs were "borne by the
data center operators rather than being shifted to other customers", passed the
Senate May 16th and died in House Community and Regional Affairs, recorded dead
May 20th, 2026. Alaska has no large-load tariff. Chugach Electric's own published
pending-filings page with the RCA is empty. The only binding Alaska rule is
Anchorage's AO 2026-27, adopted 10 to 2 on March 27th, defining a data center at
20 MW peak demand and routing it through conditional use review. Beat D reports 23
states had approved at least one large load tariff as of May 2026 with 7 more
pending, and Alaska none.

DEDUPE PROBLEM, and it is why this is candidate 3 rather than candidate 1. No.27
(2026-08-06) covered the AIDEA Houston parcel and the Air Force acreage directly,
under the thesis "the land is on offer, the power is not". A deck built on the same
parcel ten days later needs a materially different thesis and would have to ship as
an explicit UPDATE. The SB 250 vacuum is a genuinely different argument, but the
overlap in entities is heavy and the dedupe gate has to rule on it.

## CANDIDATE 4 — Alaskans are certifying that their public comments are human

Beat F, confidence 0.85. The sharpest idea in the sweep and the thinnest window
position.

When more than 500 comments landed on the STAK Energy North Slope proposal, at
least one Alaskan wrote into the comment itself that it was "written with my own
brain and fingers typing". STAK's chief strategy officer John Boyle then dismissed
the bulk of the record as "form letters lacking substance". Other filed subject
lines include "HELL NO! To ANY DATA CENTERS", "NO AI", and a commenter asking
decision-makers to use "Alaskan intelligence".

Why it is not the lead. The comment record it rests on closed July 17th and the
reporting is from early July, so the in-window hook would have to be the August 19th
Houston deadline, which drags it back into candidate 3's dedupe problem.

Beat F also supplies the single best belief-versus-record correction in the sweep,
which any deck on this subject should carry: a number of commenters argued the North
Slope project would raise electricity prices for other Alaskans, and Northern Journal
states plainly that North Slope fields operate independently from the state grid, so
that claim is not supported.

## CANDIDATE 5 — The utility that says it has no gas went to emergency arbitration over 8.1 MMcf/d

Beat A, confidence 0.88, and the freshest item in the sweep. Petroleum News published
the fullest account on August 16th, the run date itself.

Alaska Pipeline Company, Enstar's procurement arm, filed an emergency arbitration
claim on July 30th against Cook Inlet producer HEX Operating, alleging HEX sold gas
subject to APC's optional right of first refusal to a third party and then stopped
discretionary deliveries entirely on July 26th. Enstar told the RCA on August 4th
that the interruption risks entering winter with insufficient storage inventory.
HEX says APC is conflating two contractually distinct products, and is offering the
gas at $17.50 per Mcf against the $13.50 Enstar says a March 1st side letter locked
in. John Sims of Enstar, on the record, "Three BCF is equivalent to 18 days in the
middle of winter where I cannot serve any customers."

Why it is not the lead, stated plainly. None of the four sources mentions data
centers, AI or large industrial loads anywhere. The connection to Alaska's compute
build-out is inference, and this page does not ship inference. The scale contrast is
extraordinary, roughly 8,100 to 15,000 Mcf per day contested here against STAK's
proposed 350,000 to 500,000 Mcf per day, but a deck built on it would be asserting a
link the record does not make.

## NEW SOURCES TO CONSIDER (converged across scouts)

Three scouts independently nominated keyless JSON APIs as the fix for the Alaska
primary-source 403 problem, and this is the most useful operational finding of the
sweep:

- `api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?awardeeStateCode=AK&dateStart=...` returns
  every Alaska NSF award in a date range in one call. It surfaced candidate 2, which
  nothing else had reported. The HTML award pages render only in JavaScript and come
  back empty; the JSON endpoint returns the full abstract.
- `federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[term]=...` answers "is there
  any federal action on Alaska AI this week", including the negative, in one call.
- `health.alaska.gov` RHTP page and its weekly award spreadsheet, which is the
  primary record behind candidate 1 and updates every Friday.

Plus: petroleumnews.com (the only outlet reliably covering RCA and Railbelt filings,
and it fetches cleanly), matsusentinel.com, northern.org/news (mirrors the DNR PDFs
the state's own portal refuses), avo.alaska.edu/news, ahab.aoos.org/ifcb, and
houstonak.us agendas and minutes.
