## THE PICK

## HOW IT WAS CHOSEN, INCLUDING THE PART WHERE THE FACT-CHECKER WAS RIGHT AND I WAS NOT

Six scouts returned five strong candidates. Three went to the fact-checker as an
adversarial validator with instructions to disprove rather than confirm. It
ranked them nsf-minerals, rural-health, anlc, and its reasoning changed this
run's deck twice.

**It killed my intended headline.** The rural health story was briefed as "the
$272 million technology fund that bought no autonomy in round one", and the
fact-checker refused it, correctly. The only published list of the nineteen
funded projects is a spreadsheet that would not open to it, and the PDF path
404s, so five of nineteen awards are itemised and fourteen are not. Its sentence
is the right one, "an absence I cannot read is not an absence I can publish."
Running that headline would have put an unverifiable negative on slide one, which
is the one place a deck cannot afford one.

**It killed the story I most wanted to make.** The Alaska Native Language Center
piece was the most affecting thing any scout brought back, and the AI link is
manufactured. The August 11th staffing article never mentions AI. The claim that
a ChatGPT DEI screen killed an ANLC grant rests on one student newspaper article
from April 6th, four months outside the window, and the plaintiffs' own discovery
release names no Alaska grant and no Alaska organisation. It also quietly
disproved four of the scout's supporting facts, including a fall from ten
research faculty to five that is nowhere in the cited article, and a second half
of a Sharon Hargus quotation that only ever appeared paraphrased. The instinct at
0.85 says that when two independent writers invent the same bridge, the shape of
the record is pulling them toward the inference a reader wants. That is exactly
what happened here, to a scout and then to me. The bridge is not in the record
and this page does not build bridges.

**It ranked NSF critical minerals first and I overruled that, on story rather
than on evidence.** The NSF award is genuinely bulletproof, 17 verified claims,
every figure from NSF's own award API, and its strongest single claim is NSF's
own program element string beginning "Artificial Intelligence", which is a
taxonomy rather than a reporter's characterisation. Three reasons it is the
runner-up and not the pick. Its own verdict says it is "an award announcement,
not an event with consequences yet", and nothing happens until September 1st.
The feedstock in the abstract is domestic coal refuse and ash, not Alaska ore, so
the deck could not honestly draw Alaska rock, which guts Alaska authenticity at a
0.12 weight. And No.33, two runs ago on 2026-08-14, was already a deck about an
NSF programme and the design of a federal AI allocation. It clears the dedupe
gate on entities and it would not clear the eye on variety.

The rural health story, reframed the way the fact-checker proposed, is the better
deck. It has $272 million of Alaska stakes, named Alaska organisations in named
Alaska places, a live weekly clock, a budget period closing October 30th, an
on-record critique of the technology emphasis from a UAA economist, and an AI
hook that is the state speaking in its own document about what the money may buy.

## THE REFRAME, precisely

Out: "the fund bought no autonomy." Unverifiable.

In: **the state published a menu and the first Friday produced an order, and they
are not the same document.** The menu is verified primary, in the state's own
words, and it names drones, kiosks, remote dispensing equipment, and AI-enabled
tools and wearables. The order is verified: $4.5 million, nineteen projects,
under 2 percent, five of them itemised as an X-ray machine, a scheduling system,
a hydroponic farm, a Medicare improvement and a plan. The gap between the two is
the deck, and the fourteen unitemised awards are drawn as a void rather than
argued about.

## SYNTHESIS — what won, what was grafted, what was refused

All three treatments cleared the forbidden-four test and all three named their own
two weakest points honestly. The winner is the EDITORIAL ESSAYIST's chassis with
two organs grafted in, one from each of the others.

### THE WINNER, and why

**THE WELDED RAIL (editorial essayist).** A horizontal dollar measure, 920 px
wide, welded so that 920 px is $272,174,856 and never rescaled once across nine
slides. At that scale the first Friday's $4.5 million is **15.2 px**. That is the
deck's whole argument performed by the reader's own eye, with no adjective and no
accusation, and it is the honest projection, because one linear axis is the one
axis that structurally can't lie about a quantity.

It beat the DATA JOURNALIST's welded dot denominator (1 dot = $5,000, 54,435 dots
on the cover) on a measurement rather than on taste. **That director named the
risk itself and the prototype confirmed it.** Cover prototype P6 was built at the
full 54,435 dots and downsampled to the 432 px feed width, and the field resolves
to grey tone. It is not a countable population at the size LinkedIn actually
judges a cover. A 15.2 px notch in a 920 px bar scales to about 6 px in a 368 px
bar and stays visible. The rail survives the feed test and the dot field does not.

### GRAFT ONE, from the FIELD DOCUMENTARIAN

**THE CONTACT RULE.** A thing the record says happened is solid, sits in a lit
pool and carries a declared `data-contacts` two-part shadow. A thing that is
merely eligible is a phantom outline with nothing under it darkening. This is the
deck's thesis rendered as physics rather than as a caption, eligibility versus
expenditure encoded as whether an object has weight, and it is falsifiable by
measurement. It is also the rule the prototypes already proved twice, once by
failing it at dL -0.9 and once by fixing it.

### GRAFT TWO, from the EDITORIAL ESSAYIST's own second device

**THE EDGELESS MASS.** The fourteen awards that are not itemised in the record
get drawn at full weight, in the same material and at the same scale as the five
that are, with **no edge anywhere**, no top face and no countable division. This
is the best answer any of the three found to the deck's hardest honesty problem,
and it is the thing that makes the deck trustworthy rather than merely careful.
The essayist's refusal is kept with it, that the five readable awards sum to
$2,212,600 and that subtracting a precise sum from C06's rounded $4.5 million
would manufacture a precision the record does not carry. Neither figure is
printed. The mass's right terminus feathers out instead, which draws the
approximation rather than asserting it.

### REFUSED, with reasons

- **The dot denominator as the deck's spine.** Measured and failed at feed size.
  Weighted stipple stays as the SUBSTRATE on every slide, which is what the
  prototypes validated it for, and it is no longer asked to carry the count.
- **The distance rail collapsing Alaska to one axis** (field documentarian). Its
  own self-critique is right that a reader who knows Kodiak and Nenana lie in
  opposite directions will read it as a route. The map slide, prototyped as P5 on
  the canonical equal-area projection, restores the second dimension properly.
- **Gold as "the state wrote it down itself"** (data journalist). Auditable and
  good, but gold as WHAT IS STILL OPEN is the better fit, because the deck's
  close is the remaining Fridays and the single ask, so the gold budget should
  peak where the ask lives.
- **The number-led cover**, which two of three proposed. The deck sits on an AI
  page and the number cover names no AI anywhere. The cover carries the
  list-versus-order tension in words and the allocation as geometry, which is
  both hooks in one frame. Note that the field documentarian and this showrunner
  drafted a nearly identical cover line independently, before seeing each other,
  which is the signal that it is the right one.

### THE COMPOSITION RISK ALL THREE NAMED, and the answer

Every director flagged the same defect in their own pitch, that nine frames on
one chassis read as one layout run nine times. The essayist put it hardest, eight
of nine are "type block above, welded rail below", and named No.28 as the
precedent. The answer is structural rather than typographic, and it is already
prototyped. Three slides break the skeleton outright, and each was built and
gated before this synthesis was written. Slide 05 is the aksdf raymarched hero
(P2). Slide 07 is the equal-area Alaska map with three regions drawn as absences
(P5). Slide 03 is the density funnel with no rail at all (P3). That is a third of
the deck on three genuinely different constructions, plus a cover and a close
that share no interior geometry with the body slides.
