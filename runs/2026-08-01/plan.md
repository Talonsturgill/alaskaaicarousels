**THE ONE WEAKNESS THIS RUN ATTACKS: artwork craft, and specifically the
missing literal anchor — a real Alaska geography, modelled, load-bearing.**

The reasoning, from the record rather than from taste. Artwork craft has been
weakest in 8 of the last 10 runs and the three most recent diagnoses each
found a real mechanism and each left a residue:

- No. 19 concentrated all craft in one modelled object, so the frame had one
  value group and flattened.
- No. 20 answered that with nine distributed fields and learned that
  "distribute the detail" is a plan, not a technique. What shipped was one
  background reused nine times. Its own retro prescribed the fix: build ONE
  surface properly, then reuse it.
- No. 21 built exactly that (akengrave, generative reservation) and it worked
  structurally, and then its own retro named the deck's real weakness in one
  sentence: **"There is no map and no geographic anchor anywhere in ten
  slides."** A director flagged it against its own pitch before a line was
  drawn and the showrunner overrode it, then cut the deck's only coastline to
  solve a layout collision.

DESIGN_DOCTRINE 6.4 is unambiguous that zero anchors is a fail, and 6.6 names
Alaska geography as the brand's recurring hero. Four consecutive decks (18
through 21) have been objects, columns, valleys and sheets. So this run's
attack is not another new surface technique. It is: **the story is chosen with
geographic legibility as a first-order selection criterion, the deck carries a
real projected Alaska geography as its hero or its spine, and that geography
is MODELLED (three value bands, per-region detail budget) rather than drawn as
a flat silhouette.** The three value bands come from No. 20's craft refresh
(background light values / midground medium / foreground full range and
sharpest edges); the per-region budget comes from No. 21's engraving find
(mainline, crossline, interdot as three separately controllable parts).

Committed shape of the attack, to be honoured in the Phase 5 dossiers:
1. Story selection weights "does this story have a PLACE the art can encode"
   above visual abstraction. A candidate with coordinates beats a candidate
   with a metaphor, all else equal.
2. The deck carries a true-projection Alaska geography (assets geodata,
   parallels 55/65, rotate 154) that is load-bearing, not a locator inset.
3. That geography is built ONCE as a modelled surface with a stated
   three-band value plan and a per-region ink budget, then re-used across
   slides at different camera stations or crops. One surface, built properly.
4. Every dossier's field 4a names what the lower band carries, and the answer
   is the terrain, the water or the ground plane extended into it, not a plate.

## THE RECURRING HARD FAIL, and the structural answer

`text against geometry` has hard-failed 3 of the last 10 runs, and contrast
once. No. 21 found the instrument bug (contrast_estimate measured a MEAN
against a rule about a MINIMUM) and Phase 12 patched it with a worst-cell walk.
That patch is in place for this run. The remaining exposure is layout, and the
2026-07-31 lesson is explicit about the answer:

- **Decide the text ground before the composition.** Body copy goes on dark
  ground or in a reserved field, never on the lit half of a lit surface.
- **Generative reservation over knockout plates** wherever the art is
  procedural: generate the art AFTER text layout, from MEASURED boxes in
  render_report.json, so a stroke under a glyph cannot exist. Measure the
  padding against a render; 14px was not enough on 07-31, 22px was.
- **Re-derive every layout position from the measured boxes on the second
  pass.** Every absolute position guessed from a word count produced a
  collision on 07-31; every position re-derived from render_report.json held.
