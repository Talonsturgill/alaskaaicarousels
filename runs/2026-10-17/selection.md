# SELECTION, 2026-10-17 (No.89)

## Decision
HALONG, ONE YEAR ON: the forecasts are now AI, and the observations under them have holes.
One year after ex-typhoon Halong inundated the Kuskokwim Delta coast (October 12th, 2025; more than 1,500 people forced from their homes; Kipnuk 90 percent of buildings damaged or destroyed), NOAA runs AI global weather models operationally (AIGFS, a 16-day forecast in about 40 minutes on 0.3 percent of the GFS's computing, fine-tuned on NOAA's own Global Data Assimilation System analyses, which put surface, balloon, aircraft, buoy, radar and satellite observations onto a grid). On the ground, every airport weather station in the Y-K Delta region has gone days or weeks without observations since April (Thoman via KYUK), a lack of balloon launches across Western Alaska may have played a role, Kotzebue's balloons have been suspended since February 2025, and the models got Halong's track and missed its eastward shift. Eos (October 9th): AI sea-ice systems now number in the dozens, and the observations to build and test them remain sparse across both poles.
Claims: C01 to C56 (C57 to C70 runner-up).

## Why it wins
1. Concrete Alaska impact: named villages, a named climatologist, a dated anniversary, FEMA numbers, the region's own stations.
2. Visual potential: a storm track over the Bering Sea; an observing network whose points go dark; the AI model's mesh over the sea; the tundra inundated 15 miles inland. Geometry, quantity, place all present.
3. Tangible and structural: it takes a position on the AI boom (a better model can't forecast what nobody measured) with a mechanism (GDAS analyses are observations on a grid).
4. An Alaskan would send it to a coworker: every Western Alaska emergency manager, pilot and legislator who has heard "AI weather" pitched.
