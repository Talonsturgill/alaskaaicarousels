## Decision

**Eielson: the one door open this week is 5 megawatts on wetlands.** The Air Force asked the public on
September 20th for comment on the preferred site for Oklo's 5 megawatt microreactor at Eielson Air Force
Base, near Spruce Lake east of the south end of the main runway, next to wetlands, with comments due to the
Eielson Public Affairs Office by September 30th (C01 to C07). On the same base, and on the same Interior
grid question, the Air Force's AI data center enhanced use lease solicitation AFCEC-26-R-0006 took proposals
from developers until June 29th and has no award (C28 to C41). The deck's job is scale and access: what the
public is being asked about (5 MW, backup electricity and heat, a reactor the Air Force hopes runs in 2030)
against the order of load the neighbouring parcels were offered for (a qualifying data center under EO 14318
is more than 100 MW of new load, C49), with the utilities saying they can't supply large data centers (C47)
and Murkowski saying the Interior's energy base is insecure (C44).

Criteria: (1) concrete Alaska impact, a named base, a named lake, named parcels near North Pole Middle School
and Salcha; (2) visual potential, real ground (NWI wetlands, NHD rivers, the 14/32 runway, DEM) and a scale
comparison in megawatts; (3) tangibility, an email address and a date two days after the run; (4) an
Alaskan would forward the address to a neighbour before Wednesday.

## Directors room synthesis

Three pitches: data-journalist ("Eielson, Measured in Megawatts", a light vellum page with one rendered
counter per megawatt walking five swipes to 100), systems-illustrator ("Eielson, Layer by Layer", a light
orthographic anatomy of five registered GIS plates and two process machines), cinematographer ("The One Door
Open at Eielson", a night location shoot, nine distinct setups under one off-camera strobe in first snow).

WINNER: the cinematographer's NIGHT STROBE SHOT LIST, because it is the only pitch that answers the standing
weakness at its root. The scorer's repeated art complaint is one drawing reused across frames; the last four
decks were each one object or terrain re-shot. Nine distinct subjects (wetland, runway end, map insert, the
empty mark, fence, highway posts, load blocks, the line, the mailbox) under one light and one weather is
cohesion by physics rather than by repeated geometry, and inverse-square falloff lights the NEAR ground
brightest, which puts modelled tone in every lower third by construction. It keeps the brand's dark register
where the two light pitches both gave it up on a white LinkedIn feed.
