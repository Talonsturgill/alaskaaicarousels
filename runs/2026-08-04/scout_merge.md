## Candidate ranking

### 1. THE DIXON DIVERSION (Beat A) - LEAD CANDIDATE

FERC accepted AEA's final license amendment application for the Bradley Lake
Hydroelectric Project, P-8221-124, and published the notice in the Federal
Register on 2026-08-03, one day before this run. The comment and intervention
window closes 2026-08-28 at 5:00 p.m. Eastern.

Verified by the showrunner directly against the primary document
(govinfo FR-2026-08-03, 2026-15691):
- applicant Alaska Energy Authority, filed 2026-06-30
- divert Dixon Glacier meltwater into Bradley Lake via a new diversion dam
- raise the normal maximum operating pool by approximately 16 feet
- install a 0.3 MW micro-hydro turbine in a fishwater release valve
- installed capacity 119.7 MW to 120.0 MW
- average annual generation up "approximately 38 percent"
- comments due 2026-08-28, 5:00 p.m. ET, docket P-8221-124

Also verified directly against AEA's own project page: cost "approximately
$400 million", schedule "estimated three years of study and permitting
followed by three years of construction", sited 5 miles southwest of Bradley
Lake and 27 miles northeast of Homer, final amendment application filed
2026-06-30. NOTE FOR THE FACT-CHECKER: AEA's page does NOT carry the tunnel
length, the homes figure, the gas displacement figure or any absolute
generation number. The scout attributed a 4.6-mile tunnel to AEA and that
attribution does not hold on the page fetched. Every one of those numbers
needs its own source or it does not ship.

WHY IT LEADS. It is the only candidate that is simultaneously (a) anchored in
a primary federal document published the day before the run, (b) carrying a
live deadline a reader can act on, (c) rich in real drawable geometry, a
glacier, a tunnel, a lake, a 16-foot pool band, a Railbelt spine, and (d)
pointed straight at the AI question from an angle the series has never used.

THE THESIS, and it is a scale check rather than an accusation. Every deck this
series has run on AI infrastructure has been about demand arriving. This one is
about what supply actually costs here. Diverting a glacier through a mountain
buys Alaska roughly 38 percent more energy from its largest hydro plant, takes
six years, costs about $400 million, and moves installed capacity from 119.7 MW
to 120.0 MW. A single Air Force enhanced-use-lease data centre is specified at a
minimum of 100 MW of NEW load, and the North Slope proposal is discussed in
gigawatts. Supply moves in decades and megawatts; demand arrives in months and
gigawatts. The one place a citizen gets a say closes 2026-08-28.

HONESTY FIREWALL, to be enforced in every dossier. Nothing in the record says
the Dixon Diversion is for data centres, and the deck must never imply it. AEA
has not said that and it is not true. The deck's comparison is a magnitude
comparison between two separately sourced facts, and the slide that makes it
must say so in its own words.

DEDUPE: ran dedupe_check. One LIKELY DUPLICATE, No.10 (2026-07-18), jaccard
0.076, read in full. No.10 is cost allocation and who pays, decided at the RCA,
the Assembly and the Legislature. This is generation supply, decided at FERC.
No shared named project, decider or document. NOT a duplicate. Cleared.

### 2. NTSB BERING AIR 445 AND PART 135 FLIGHT DATA MONITORING (Beat C)

NTSB adopted its final report 2026-07-30 on the 2025-02-06 crash near Nome
that killed 10, and asked the FAA for expanded flight data monitoring,
certificated dispatchers, broader load manifests and crash-resistant
recorders. Paired with carriers telling a 2026-07-31 roundtable that up to 20
Alaska aviation weather stations are dark on a given day.

Strong Alaska authenticity, strong primary sourcing, real stakes. Held as
runner-up on two grounds. The automation here is exceedance analytics rather
than AI, so the beat fit is a stretch the deck would have to argue rather than
inherit. And a deck built on ten named deaths needs an editorial posture this
run has not established; done carelessly it reads as using a fatal accident as
a hook for a technology argument. Worth a dedicated future run, planned
properly.

### 3. THE KENAI PENINSULA SCHOOL BOARD'S AI AND SCREEN-TIME RULEBOOK (Beat F)

Reviewed 2026-08-03. Proposed instructional screen caps of 15 minutes in
kindergarten and first grade rising to 90 minutes in fourth and fifth, no
per-grade cap in 6 to 12. Rides alongside AI policies reviewed 2026-07-07
barring uploads of student data to unapproved AI platforms. The district had
already bought a three-year MagicSchool AI subscription for $8,300.

Genuinely net-new territory for this series, genuinely unclaimed statewide,
and the buy-the-tool-then-write-the-policy sequence is a real argument. Held
back because nothing is adopted, no vote date is set, and a deck of proposed
numbers is a weaker artifact than a deck of decided ones. Queue candidate.
