# Selection, run 2026-10-21 (No.92)

## Decision

STORY: Geomorphic AI, an AI-driven minerals prospect generator, announced on October 20th a wholly
owned Alaska subsidiary, Golden Dyke Exploration LLC, naming no Alaska property yet (C01 to C08).
In 2026 the Alaska Division of Geological & Geophysical Surveys published five USGS Earth MRI
funded airborne surveys over the Kuskokwim region: Ganes Creek (25,731 line km), McGrath (86,319),
Aniak and the Little Taylor Mountains (20,090.5), Candle Hills EM (1,060) and Granite Mountain EM
(2,516), flown 400 m apart at about 100 m above the ground, released free under an open licence
(C13 to C42, C63 to C74). The company's own release says the new state airborne surveys extend
decades of public Alaska geology (C08), and its business is to generate and package targets and
partner on them while keeping an interest (C04, C05).

ANGLE: the input is public and the output will be private. Federal money flew about 135,700 km of
lines over the middle Kuskokwim and the state gave the grids away. An AI prospector set up an
Alaska company the same autumn. What it finds becomes a packaged target with a retained interest,
on ground where Aniak and Chuathbaluk sit under the lines. The deck does not say this is wrong; it
shows the asymmetry and asks who should see the map the public's data produces.

Why it wins, against the four criteria:
1. Alaska impact: the largest public geophysical campaign in this region in years, a named new
   Alaska entity, named quadrangles and villages, open data any Alaskan can download.
2. Visual potential: the strongest of the day by far. The real flight lines (about 3,400 of them)
   and the real residual magnetic field (145,406 cells at 500 m) are in hand as data, so the art
   can draw the actual survey rather than a picture of one.
3. Tangible: a filing-equivalent (the company's own release), five state reports with DOIs,
   grant numbers, aircraft, dates.
4. Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker: anyone in mining, ANCSA lands, DNR or the delegation.

Dedupe: `dedupe_check.py --entities "Geomorphic AI,Golden Dyke Exploration,DGGS,USGS Earth MRI"`
returned CLEAR (no in-window overlap). No.82 (2026-10-09, NSF bio-refinery for critical minerals)
is a different story and a different object; read by eye, no shared event.

## Directors room synthesis

Three pitches: data-journalist THE FLOWN LINE (one real McGrath flight line as a panorama rail
under nine readings of one dataset), systems-illustrator THE FIFTH LAYER (an exploded axonometric
survey machine whose fifth layer is the private target), field-documentarian THE LOW PASS (shot
and reverse shot, air and ground, dated sun).

Winner: THE FLOWN LINE, because it is the only pitch whose every frame is drawn from the real
survey data, and it is the most buildable at density 2 (no procedural aircraft hero to fail as a
toy, which both other pitches named as their weakest point). Organs taken from the others: the
field-documentarian's survey-height terrain as the masterful depth frame (05) and the October sun
as the deck's one key; the systems-illustrator's "the state designed it this way" turn, which
08 states without accusing anyone (C68 says the data is meant to promote exploration).
Changed from the winner: no printed five-survey total (aggregate_check has no sum kind, and the
cover's 86,319 is McGrath alone, from one claim); 03's tile is labelled NOT A TARGET; 07 became
the bill (Earth MRI grants, free download, a three-screen print of the real Ganes Creek
radiometrics) because C76 to C78 verified the open license and free download that the pitches
flagged as missing; the null cell moved into 08's turn as a milled pocket inside the data.
