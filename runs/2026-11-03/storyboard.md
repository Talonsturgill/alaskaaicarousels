# STORYBOARD, run 2026-11-03, carousel No.104

## THESIS

On November 3rd Alaska picked a governor from four candidates with three different answers on data centers, but the state is already moving land toward data centers without a data center law, so whoever takes office at noon December 7th inherits decisions that ran ahead of the statute.

Document title (PDF): `Four candidates, three answers. No law yet.` (43 characters).

## ARC, nine slides

01 COVER, hook: the four answers, one or two words each, in the candidates' own words. Plants: who said which?
02 PAYS: the four quotes in full, with names and parties; three positions, four people. Plants: who is ahead?
03 The count, honestly: an early first-round count at 8:51 p.m., 11 percent of precincts, ranked choice November 18th if needed. Plants: whoever wins, what is already on the desk?
04 State land: the 20,000-acre Houston transfer to AIDEA, "certainly possible" before December 7th. Plants: is that the only land?
05 Military land: the Pentagon's offer of about 4,700 acres, up to a dozen data centers, none awarded as of September 15th. Temperature turns from record to people. Plants: who was asked?
06 BREATHER: "Our largest concern is that nobody asked us." Fairbanks, about 150 people. Plants: where would anyone be asked?
07 The law: even the pause waits on a framework; the Senate passed a data center bill 14 to 5 and the House never took it up. Plants: what is the calendar?
08 KEEPER: the desk at noon December 7th, three items and four dates on one sheet.
09 CLOSE, single ask: law first, or land first?

## SLIDE COUNT RATIONALE

Nine. Each content frame carries a different document of the record (the candidates' quotes, the election-night count, a DNR hearing, a Pentagon offer, a town hall, a Senate vote) and a different object on the desk. Merging 04 and 05 would put state and federal land in one frame and lose the difference in who decides; a tenth frame would only add the campaign money figures, which the room ruled out (see HONESTY GUARDS).

## HOOK ARCHETYPE

NEW: THE FOUR-WORD BALLOT. The cover is the four answers reduced to their own key words, in the order of their positions, so the frame reads as a ballot of stances rather than a question, a sentence or a number. Not the unfinished sentence, the reversed question or the partner as drawn.

## NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

Stances, then count, then inventory, then the missing law: what they said (01, 02), what the count can and can't say (03), what is already on the desk (04, 05), who was not asked (06), the law that isn't there (07), the desk with dates (08), the ask (09).

## THE STANDING WEAKNESS, and what is different here

Artwork craft has been the weakest criterion in 6 of the last 10 runs (mean 7.26). Three changes here. Density 2: one object per frame, so all modelling effort goes into the frame's largest thing (lit face from the lamp, lee toward the lower left, a contact seam and a cast onto a lit pool). No terrain anywhere, so the stair-step, cell-lattice and fingerprint texture artifacts the last six decks shipped can't occur. The type always sits in the dark room zone the lamp's pool does not reach (art routed around type, never a reserve after the fact). The masterful depth frame (04, the survey cap at macro with thin-lens focus) is built first.

## HONESTY GUARDS (bind every frame)

- The vote count is PARTIAL. Every number from it carries "early", its 8:51 p.m. stamp and 11 percent of precincts (C01 to C04), or 45 of 400 at 8:40 p.m. (C05). No frame calls it a result, sizes anything by vote share, or orders the candidates by it. Candidates appear in the order of their positions, restrictive to embrace.
- Wilson's position is against HYPERSCALE data centers (C14), never "against data centers". The cover says "Never hyperscale."
- The Houston transfer "could" include data centers and AIDEA "has maintained" they are unlikely (C28, C30). "Certainly possible" is the commissioner's answer to whether DNR could decide before December 7th (C27). No "no-cost" wording.
- The military offer is the Pentagon's (C22, C23), near Anchorage, Healy and Fairbanks. Never "Air Force", never Clear, never a solicitation number. "None awarded" is always "as of September 15th" (C24).
- The bill is "a data center bill" that passed the Senate 14 to 5 (C20) and that the House did not take up (C21). No bill number on any frame (BASIS was unreachable).
- The AI-company employee contributions (C33 to C39) are NOT on any slide or in the caption: the room judged that one flat figure beside a candidate frame implies a link the claims don't support, and the studio's production model is made by Anthropic. Disclosed to the maintainer in the editor notes.
- The tally card's 150 strokes draw "about 150" (C40) and are labelled as a drawing of the reported attendance, [design].
- The props (cap, keys, cards, rule) are a still life set by the studio and say so nowhere they would be taken as the record's own objects: no prop carries an agency seal, a document title or invented text.
