# STORYBOARD — Alaska.Ai Carousel No. 47 — 2026-09-01

# DECK HEADER

## THESIS

The Fairbanks North Star Borough printed one line twice, on its state list and
on its federal list both, because the pause it wants is not a pause it has the
power to impose, and the words it actually adopted reach state or municipal land
while the twelve parcels that prompted them sit on federal ground.

## DOCUMENT TITLE (PDF, 41 characters)

`Fairbanks Wrote One Line Twice`

## SLIDE COUNT RATIONALE

**Nine.** The house default band is 8 to 10 and the story has exactly nine
beats, no more. There is one cover, one immediate payoff, five content beats
(the condition, the trigger, the geography, the calendar, the boundary), one
breather that is also the geography register lift, and one close. Cutting to
eight would mean folding the calendar into the boundary slide, and those are two
different arguments (the borough was late, and the borough's words stop short)
that a reader would blur together. Going to ten would mean adding a beat about
what happens next, and nothing verified this run supports one.

## HERO STRUCTURE (for ledger/artwork.json)

**NEW: THE COUNT LINE AND ITS OWN SHADOW.** A run of thin galvanized survey
markers driven into open Interior flats, one per item on a single adopted
resolution, with the row set exactly PERPENDICULAR TO THE CAMERA'S OPTICAL AXIS
so that a straight line parallel to the image plane projects through the pinhole
at uniform scale and the quantity carries zero perspective error. One station
carries two markers. The sun is low and almost directly behind the row, so every
marker throws a long shadow toward the reader, and those shadows, fanning and
falling out of focus as they approach the lens, ARE the type reserve. The
subject casts the ground the type stands on. The ground itself is a deliberate
void, described only as far as a shadow needs something to fall on.

Divergence, stated against each of the last four:

- **vs No.43, the drain scar and datum board on walked terrain.** There the
  terrain was the subject and one plywood prop carried every string. Here the
  terrain is a shadow catcher with no described features, nothing is walked
  across, and no prop carries type at all. The type carrier is an absence of
  light.
- **vs No.44, the interior in plan view keyed from under the reading surface.**
  Exterior, natural key, one sun, camera at 0.72 m and never above the subject.
  Nothing in this deck is lit from beneath.
- **vs No.45, the cut rag sheet on a table with boroughs lifted.** No paper, no
  table, no cutting, no lifting. Geography appears exactly once, as a flat
  true-projection strip map with a scale bar, quarantined inside a labelled
  drafting overlay.
- **vs No.46, the wall of spines seen square on with no surface.** There is a
  surface, it is horizontal, and the camera grazes it at 5.3 degrees rather than
  facing a vertical plane at zero. Three of the last four decks looked DOWN at a
  surface. This one looks ALONG one.

## ATMOSPHERE (for ledger/artwork.json)

**NEW: FIRST HOUR, LATE AUGUST, UNDER A HIGH CLOUD LID.** Camera bearing 058.
Key is the sun at **azimuth 062, elevation 14**, four degrees off the optical
axis and behind the row, so the register is contre-jour and every marker reads
as a dark vertical with a hot rim on its far edge. Fill is the whole overcast
hemisphere at `#0E1A28`, an enormous soft source, so **key to fill is 3.5 to 1**
and the modelling is carried by the raking angle rather than by contrast. Ground
fog forms in the hollows on the shadowed near ground only. Dry, no specular
puddles anywhere.

Elevation 14 is a craft decision. It is low enough that a 0.45 m marker throws a
1.80 m shadow, which lands the shadow tips at y 1307 and gives the reserve a
real depth, and high enough that the lateral fan stays inside a frame the type
can live in.

Divergence: not No.44's underlit 02:00 interior, not No.45's high-key north
window, not No.46's single hard lamp above and in front with no daylight. All
three were interiors with no sky. The nearest neighbour in the whole ledger is
No.43, five runs back, and it is answered on five measurable axes. Azimuth 062
against 318, which is the opposite side of the sky. Elevation 14 against 26.
First hour against eleven p.m. Dry with forming fog against wet after rain with
mirror puddles. Contre-jour against side lit.

## THE CAMERA MATH, solved once and used everywhere

```
vertical fov 34 deg, canvas 1080 x 1350
f   = (1350/2) / tan(17 deg) = 675 / 0.305731 = 2207.8  -> 2208 design px
cx  = 540, cy = 675
pitch 5.3 deg down
horizonY = 675 - 2208 * tan(5.3 deg) = 675 - 204.9 = 470.1  -> 470
camera height h = 0.72 m
row distance   Z = 3.70 m
ground at the row:  y = 470 + 2208 * (0.72 / 3.70) = 470 + 429.7 = 899.7 -> 900
marker height 0.45 m, so the cap sits 0.27 m below the lens:
cap y = 470 + 2208 * (0.27 / 3.70) = 470 + 161.1 = 631.1 -> 631
marker drawn height = 900 - 631 = 269 px
rod width 0.014 m -> 2208 * 0.014 / 3.70 = 8.35 px -> 9 px drawn
tag 0.045 m wide, 0.05 m tall -> 26.9 px wide, 29.8 px tall -> 27 x 30 px
```

**Station layout, in screen space, and x is NOT a measured axis.** The row is a
counted population of objects, not a scale, so it carries `window.__akAssert`
with `points` and it does NOT carry `data-scale`. That is deliberate and it is
what keeps the No.35 defect structurally out of this deck. The only `data-scale`
in nine slides is S06's day axis.

```
state block:    21 stations, x = 96 + i*31.5 for i in 0..20   ->  96 .. 726
separator gap:  96 px of empty ground, three pitches wide
federal block:   4 stations, x = 822 + j*31.5 for j in 0..3   -> 822 .. 916.5
right margin:   163.5 px
ink fraction:   9 px rod on a 31.5 px pitch = 28.6 percent ink, 71.4 percent air
```

**The doubled station** is state station 12 (the 13th), at x 474. Its twin
stands 0.25 m further back at Z = 3.95 m and 0.030 m to the right.

```
twin base y = 470 + 2208 * (0.72 / 3.95) = 470 + 402.5 = 872.5 -> 872
twin cap  y = 470 + 2208 * (0.27 / 3.95) = 470 + 150.9 = 620.9 -> 621
twin drawn height = 251 px against the front marker's 269
X of station 12  = (474 - 540) / 596.76 = -0.1106 m
X of the twin    = -0.0806 m
twin screen x    = 540 + 2208 * (-0.0806 / 3.95) = 540 - 45.1 = 494.9 -> 495
```

So the twin reads up and to the right of its partner, smaller, nearer the
horizon. The station is visibly WIDER than the other twenty four and throws TWO
shadows. That, and not a countable row, is what has to survive 432 px.

**The shadow solve.** Sun azimuth 062, camera bearing 058, so the sun sits 4
degrees right of the optical axis. World axes are X right, Y up, Z forward.

```
sun direction (from ground toward the sun), rel. azimuth +4 deg, elevation 14:
  dx = sin(4)  * cos(14) = 0.06976 * 0.97030 = 0.06769
  dz = cos(4)  * cos(14) = 0.99756 * 0.97030 = 0.96793
  dy = sin(14)                                = 0.24192
a marker top at (X, -0.27, Z) casts to the ground plane Y = -0.72:
  t = 0.45 / 0.24192 = 1.8601 m
  tip = (X - 0.06769*1.8601, -0.72, Z - 0.96793*1.8601)
      = (X - 0.1259, -0.72, Z - 1.8004)
for the front row, Z = 3.70:  tip Z = 1.8996
  tip y = 470 + 2208 * (0.72 / 1.8996) = 470 + 836.9 = 1306.9 -> 1307
  tip x = 540 + 2208 * (X - 0.1259) / 1.8996 = 540 + 1162.4*X - 146.3
```

Every front-row shadow tip lands on y 1307. The tips fan, because a bundle of
parallel ground lines running toward the lens diverges in projection.

```
station  0 (x  96, X = -0.7440):  tip x = 540 - 864.8 - 146.3 = -471.1  off frame left
station 12 (x 474, X = -0.1106):  tip x = 540 - 128.6 - 146.3 =  265.1
station 20 (x 726, X =  0.3117):  tip x = 540 + 362.3 - 146.3 =  756.0
station 24 (x 916.5, X = 0.6309): tip x = 540 + 733.4 - 146.3 = 1127.1  off frame right
the twin (X = -0.0806, Z = 3.95): tip Z = 2.1496, tip y = 470 + 739.7 = 1209.7 -> 1210
                                  tip x = 540 + 2208*(-0.2065)/2.1496 = 540 - 212.1 = 327.9
```

The twin's shadow is SHORTER on screen and lands 63 px left of its partner's
tip, which is why the doubling reads as two shadows rather than one thick one.

**And this is where the reserve comes from.** The shadow field occupies y 900 to
1307. The focal plane is the row at 3.70 m. The ground under the type is at 1.90
m and nearer, which is genuinely outside the depth of field, so the bands soften
as they approach the reader and merge into one graded field. Circle of confusion
is ramped linearly from 0 px at y 900 to 26 px at y 1080 and held. Below y 1040
the field is smooth. **The reserve is not a plate, not a clip, and not an
erase. It is the deck's own depth of field.**

### C. ART DIRECTION

**7. Technique stack.** #87 GPU PBR via akthree for the marker row, one
instanced geometry, `AKT.objectHero(R, group, {toward:[0.068, 0.242, 0.968],
keyColor:'#F0C089', intensity:1.35})` so the contour is carved by a warm
backlight rather than reading as a flat blob. `setPixelRatio` before `setSize`.
`await AKT.snapshot(R)` and `.ok` checked. #89 akpost grade once on the art
canvas, `exposure: -0.08` STOPS not multiplier, saturation 1.02, contrast 1.06,
filmic true, bloom threshold 0.78 strength 0.10 radius 12, grain 0.05 size 2,
dither true, vignette 0.16. #90 akcolor OKLCH ramps for the steel and the sky.
#2 grain tile. Seed `AK.rng(20260901 + 1)` and `AK.reseed(20260901)`. The
shadow field is analytic 2D, drawn from the solved tip coordinates in the deck
header, NOT shadow-mapped, because a 1300 px shadow will not resolve in a 2048
map and because an analytic field survives a GPU failure intact.
