# SELECTION — Carousel No. 47 — 2026-09-01

## THE STORY

**The Fairbanks North Star Borough Assembly's 2027 legislative priorities carry
25 items. One of them appears twice, on the state list and on the federal list
both, and it is a request to stop permitting, leasing or building new commercial
data centers until standards exist for what they draw. The resolution as written
reaches state-owned or municipal land, in its own words. The thing that
triggered it is on federal land.**

That last sentence is the deck. A borough asked two governments at once for a
pause, because the pause it wants is not a pause it has the power to impose, and
the text it adopted does not reach the parcels that started the argument. The
presiding officer's own correction, made to a reporter by phone after the vote,
is that federal land was intended.

This is not a story about whether data centers are good. Nobody quoted on the
record in this window argued that. It is a story about a unit of government
locating its own authority and finding the edge of it.

## WHY THIS ONE, against the four criteria in order

**1. Concrete Alaska impact.** Eielson Air Force Base is in the Fairbanks North
Star Borough. The Air Force put roughly 4,700 acres across three Alaska
installations on offer for AI data center development, and the Interior's grid,
water and air are what a load like that would land on. The borough that would
host it has now said, in a formal document going to Juneau and to the
congressional delegation, that the standards for judging it do not exist yet.

**2. Visual potential, and this is the strongest of any candidate this run.** The
geometry is a LIST and a MAP that disagree with each other. Twenty five rows,
split into two columns, with exactly one row drawn in both. Three installations
across a state, with acreage. And a land-ownership boundary that the adopted
words stop at. Every one of those is a quantity or a place that bespoke artwork
can encode without a single decorative tick. The run's plan committed to a
modelled type void and to spending detail where the eye lands rather than
spreading it, and a list with a hole in it is exactly the subject that rewards
that discipline.

**3. Tangibility.** A reader in Fairbanks can name the base. A reader anywhere in
Alaska can read a 25 item wish list and understand instantly what it means that
one line had to be written twice.

**4. Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to two different kinds
of coworker. To someone at a utility, because of the electrical demand standard
that does not exist. To someone in borough or state government, because of the
authority gap, which is the same gap every Alaska local government is about to
find.

# THE DIRECTORS ROOM — the synthesis and the reasoning

Three lenses, rotated off No.46's trio (editorial-essayist, field-documentarian,
historian-of-the-future): **data-journalist, systems-illustrator,
cinematographer**.

## What each one brought

**Data-journalist, THE COUNT LINE.** A row of survey markers staked across
Interior flats, one per item on Resolution 2026-25, the row set PARALLEL TO THE
IMAGE PLANE so the quantity projects at uniform scale and carries zero
perspective error. Cover hook is an arithmetic error you can see. Its own
self-critique names the fatal risk honestly, that 26 near-identical verticals at
34px pitch become 15px at feed scale and read as a fence rather than a tally.

**Systems-illustrator, THE ROD RUN.** A cast-iron interlocking lever frame on a
concrete plinth, 21 slots and 4 slots, one gold lever thrown in each, driving a
push-rod that couples into a STATE take-off and a MUNICIPAL take-off and then
stops at an open flange with nothing beyond it. The wordless claim is the best
single frame any director proposed. Its self-critique is also right, that a
nine-slide invented machine is one careless label from asserting a procedure
that does not exist, and that three of its nine frames differ only by camera
distance.

**Cinematographer, THE BOUNDARY CUT AT LAST LIGHT.** A surveyed cutline through
black spruce, contre-jour, with the type reserve made of the far treeline's own
cast shadow. It found the one thing neither other lens did.

## The decision

**The count line is the spine. The shadow is the reserve.**

The cinematographer's single best idea is not its world, it is its mechanism,
and the mechanism is portable. A type reserve made of SHADOW is the only kind
that is physically incapable of having busy art under it, because shadow is not
a plate laid over art and not a clip applied after it, it is the absence of the
thing that draws art. That is a real answer to the standing weakness this run
named in plan.md, and it beats an apron, a plinth face or an evenodd clip.

Merged onto the count line it gets better still. The reserve stops being a
leftover band beneath the subject and becomes THE SUBJECT'S OWN SHADOW, thrown
toward the reader by a low morning sun behind the row. The data casts the ground
the type stands on. Nothing else in the room produced a reserve that is a
consequence of the story rather than a container for it.

Three organs are transplanted from the other two treatments:

- From the cinematographer, the shadow reserve, the falling-key arc, and the
  line-voice rule that INK MEANS A CLAIM AND LIGHT MEANS THE WORLD. Every
  drafted dashed line in this deck marks something somebody asked for and did
  not get. Nothing physically present is ever outlined.
- From the systems-illustrator, the strict gold budget (gold means the thing
  that was written twice, and appears nowhere else in nine frames) and the
  honesty legend that keeps a constructed diagram from posing as a photograph.
  All three directors converged on the gold rule independently, which is treated
  as a finding rather than a coincidence.
- From the data-journalist, everything structural, plus the register lift to a
  true-projection strip map for the geography and the re-staking of the same row
  as a day axis for the calendar.

## The fix the room did not make, and the showrunner did

The data-journalist's fence risk is real and its proposed rescue, a gold pair
and a doubled shadow, is not enough on its own. Three changes close it.

1. **The markers get thinner and shorter.** Marker height drops to 0.45 m, so
   the row stands 269 design px rather than 400, and the rod is 9 px wide at a
   31.5 px pitch. That is 29 percent ink and 71 percent air, which reads as a
   tally. A 24 px marker at 34 px pitch, which is what the treatment implied,
   is 71 percent ink and that is a fence.
2. **The count is never asked of a thumbnail.** What has to survive at 432 px is
   one station that is visibly WIDER than the other twenty four and throws two
   shadows instead of one. The numerals 21 and 4 are set in type at 72 px on the
   slide where the split is the point.
3. **The shadow field is defocused, and that is optics rather than a fix.** The
   focal plane is the row at 3.70 m. The near ground under the type is at 1.9 m
   and closer, so it is genuinely out of focus, and 25 sharp alternating bands
   soften into one graded field by y 1040. The reserve is therefore built out of
   the deck's own depth of field, which is the answer to "busy art under text"
   that costs nothing and is true.

## What was rejected and why

The cinematographer's world was the most beautiful pitch of the three and it
loses on variety, by its own honest admission. Its nearest neighbour is No.43,
five runs ago, an outdoor Interior deck at eleven p.m. with a low northwest sun.
Its divergences are real but they are differences of degree inside one register.
The count line answers that by making the ground a VOID rather than a subject.
Nothing in this deck's terrain is described, walked, or given texture beyond
what a shadow needs to fall on.

The lever frame loses on honesty economics. A reader can be trusted with a row
of staked markers, because a stake driven per item is how a tally has been drawn
since before there were charts. A 21-slot interlocking lever frame invites the
reader to believe a mechanism exists that does not.

The sun moved to the morning, deliberately. No.43's key sat at azimuth 318 and
elevation 26. This deck's sits at azimuth 062 and elevation 14, which is the
opposite side of the sky at roughly half the height, in the first hour rather
than the last, dry with forming ground fog rather than wet with specular
puddles, and contre-jour rather than side lit. Late August sunrise in Fairbanks
is near azimuth 060, so the light is also simply correct.
