## WINNER: "Made in Alaska's Weather"
Alaska Airlines, the first carrier to adopt Air Space Intelligence's "Flyways" machine-learning route-optimization tool (2021), became the demonstration case behind the FAA's $875 million contract with the same startup to build "SMART," a predictive system to modernize the entire national airspace (awarded June 2026, initial deployment targeted fall 2026). Alaska runs the tool across ~1,500 daily flights and attributes ~1 million gallons/year of fuel savings and tens of thousands of flight hours.

### Why it wins (criteria in order)
1. Concrete Alaska impact: an Alaska-named carrier flying the most weather-hostile, terrain-boxed, road-less-community-serving network in the country was the proving ground; Alaskans book the operational results (fuel, delays, reliability) today.
2. Visual potential: reactive vs predictive route fields, wind-optimized arcs over Alaska terrain, a tangled-legacy vs smoothed-AI path split, a 2021 -> 2026 -> fall-2026 adoption timeline, one plane scaling to ~1,500/day. Lets the art climb the rendered ladder in a NEW direction (aviation cartography + flow fields), directly attacking the standing artwork-craft weakness.
3. Tangibility: everyone in Alaska flies; fuel burn and delays are felt.
4. Send-to-a-coworker: the rare "Alaska built the thing everyone else now wants" story.

### The structural frame (house voice)
For a month this deck has shown Alaska as a SITE things are done to: data centers sited, land leased (No.16, 27), power drawn (No.18, 25), grids studied (No.20, 31). This is the inversion. Alaska's terrain and weather did not attract the AI, they PRODUCED it, and the FAA is now buying the rest of the country a version of what Alaska's conditions forced into being. The thesis is about DIRECTION of causation, not about being a target.
