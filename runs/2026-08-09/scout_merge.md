## Candidate ranking

### 1. SELECTED — Kenai Peninsula Borough School District's AI rulebook (Beat E)

The district's Policy Review Committee met at 10 a.m. on August 3rd with
AR 5131.9 Academic Honesty (rewritten by a district AI committee to define
acceptable student AI use) and BP/AR 6161.5 High-Quality Instruction and
Purposeful Use of Technology among eight items. The same day the board
reviewed daily screen-time caps for routine instructional use, 15 minutes in
kindergarten and first, 20 in second, 60 in third, 90 in fourth and fifth,
and no numeric cap in grades 6 through 12. The district has already spent
$8,300 on a three-year MagicSchool AI subscription. Upstream document is
Alaska DEED's state AI framework, which means this draft previews what other
Alaska districts adopt next.

Why it wins: primary district record with hard numbers on both sides of it,
zero dedupe against thirty prior decks (the series has never run a schools
deck), and Beat F independently confirmed that classroom AI is genuinely
QUIET in Alaska press right now, which makes this a find rather than a
rewrite. It is also the most human story in the window and the one an
Alaskan is most likely to send to a coworker, because every district in the
state is about to face the same two documents.

### 2. RUNNER-UP — NOAA's ten-year drone permit over 20 whale species (Beat C)

Federal Register, August 6th, File No. 29817. The Alaska Fisheries Science
Center wants a ten-year scientific research permit to study 20 cetacean
species using uncrewed aircraft, with nine pinniped species possibly harassed
incidentally and ten of the target species ESA-listed. Comments close
September 8th. Fully primary, clean dedupe against No.13 (that deck was
acoustic detection of belugas, this is aerial survey permitting), open public
access, and the ten-year-term against a 33-day comment window is the art.

Held as the fallback if the claims gate starves the selection.

### 3. NSF 26-513, one AI compute hub per state, closes November 4th (Beat D)

Published July 31st. About 10 cooperative agreements at $4,000,000 to
$12,000,000 over five years, and the solicitation states that only one award
per state or multi-state region will be made. No Alaska institution operates
a hub-scale AI compute facility and no Alaska bid is public. Actionable, AI
central, and completely clean on dedupe. Too thin to carry a deck alone;
strong as a closing move inside a larger one, or as a future deck if a bid
becomes public.
