# SELECTION — 2026-08-09 — Carousel No. 30

## THE STORY

**The Kenai Peninsula Borough School District's AI rulebook, in committee days
before the school year, with the AI tool already bought.**

Working thesis, to be sharpened by the directors room and constrained by what
the fact-checker actually verifies: **the tool arrived before the rule.** The
district put money on a classroom AI product and is still drafting the
document that says what students may do with one.

## WHY THIS ONE, against the criteria in order

1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** A named borough, a named district, a dated
   committee meeting, and a school year that starts in days. Every family on
   the Kenai is inside it, and Alaska DEED's state framework is the upstream
   document, so this draft previews what other districts adopt next. Most AI
   stories in Alaska this month are about land nobody has broken ground on.
   This one is about a rule that binds a child's homework in a fortnight.
2. **Visual potential.** The screen-time staircase is drawn geometry before
   anyone writes a caption. 15, 20, 60, 90, then a bar that refuses to close.
   Against it, a purchase already made with a term already running. Two
   clocks, one document, one deadline. It also frees the deck from four
   consecutive night scenes, because this story's material world is paper,
   daylight, a classroom schedule and a purchase order.
3. **Tangibility.** Minutes, dollars, grade levels and a first day of school.
   Nothing here needs a reader to imagine a gigawatt.
4. **Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** This is the criterion it wins
   hardest. Every parent, teacher and administrator in the state is about to
   meet the same two documents, and Beat F established that Alaska press is
   NOT covering classroom AI right now. The deck arrives before the argument
   instead of after it.

## WHAT THE DECK MUST NOT DO

- It must not blur the two documents. The screen-time caps and the AI rules
  are separate items that moved on the same day, and the deck's whole
  credibility rests on keeping them distinct. If the fact-checker cannot
  settle which document carries which provision, the deck says so on the
  slide rather than choosing.
- It must not say the district did something wrong. Buying a tool before
  finishing a policy is ordinary institutional sequencing and the district has
  been open about the drafts. The deck's argument is about the ORDER and the
  CLOCK, not about bad faith.
- It must not assert what the policies say. It quotes them or it describes
  them as proposed, and it never converts a draft into a rule.
- Nothing on any slide comes from a source the fact-checker did not fetch.

### THE DECISION

The rule allows two answers inside thirty days, a different story or an
explicit UPDATE with material new developments stated on the cover. This ships
as an **EXPLICIT UPDATE**, and the material new development is real rather than
cosmetic.

**AR 6161.5 and BP 6161.5 did not exist on the public record on July 20th.**
They came to committee as NEW BUSINESS on August 3rd, and KDLL reported that
day was the board's first look at the pair (C55). The published minute ladder,
15 to 20 to 60 to 90 and then a stated absence for secondary schools, is this
deck's entire spine and it is sixteen days younger than No.12. Also new since
July 20th are the two competing drafts of AR 5131.9 (C06), the July 6th
postponement and August 3rd return (C07, C04), the four evening first readings
(C27 to C30), and a first student day that is now ten days out rather than
thirty.

**The thesis changes, and this is the part that matters.** No.12 argued the
ORDER, that the tool came before the rule. This deck argues the PRECISION, that
one document measures a five year old to the minute and can only describe a
seventeen year old with an adjective, while the AI regulation beside it draws
no grade line at all. That argument was not available in July because half its
evidence did not exist.

### WHAT CHANGES IN THE BUILD, AS A RESULT

1. **The cover states the update.** The kicker and the datum chip both say the
   minute ladder is new since July, so a reader who saw No.12 is told
   immediately what is different rather than left to notice.
2. **Slide 07 is rebuilt.** It was "The tool was bought while the rules were
   drafts", which is No.12's headline wearing new type. The $8,300 drops from
   being a beat to a single attributed line, and the slide becomes the state
   framework as a benchmark rather than a floor.
3. **The caption does not lead on the purchase.** Both directors independently
   reached this conclusion, one of them for exactly this reason.
4. **The topics ledger entry records the UPDATE relationship to No.12
   explicitly**, so the next run inherits the fact rather than rediscovering it.

### THE PROCESS FIX, handed to Phase 12

`dedupe_check.py` is advisory and stateless, and nothing tied its output to the
story actually selected. A run that changes its candidate after the pre-flight
silently keeps a stale pass. The upgrade engineer is asked to make the gate
read the selected story rather than a hand-typed fingerprint, or to fail the
ship gate when the recorded dedupe fingerprint does not match the shipped
topic. A gate that can be answered about the wrong question is not a gate.
