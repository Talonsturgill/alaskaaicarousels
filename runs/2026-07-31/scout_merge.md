## THE SHAPE THAT EMERGED, AND IT IS NOT THE SHAPE THE BRIEF PREDICTED

The queued brief recommended the spine "the record filled itself in": No. 17
said the money was on file and the motive was not, and four days later the
donor put his motive on the record. That is true, and it is a real update.

But two scouts independently found something better, and they found it from
opposite directions.

**S2, from the primary documents:** the reason a $100,000 personal check is
legal is that Alaska's contribution limit was struck down in 2021 and never
replaced. The statute still says $500. It has never been repealed. APOC's own
published table, date-stamped 2026-07-29, says Unlimited. The book and the
enforced rule are two different numbers, and on 2026-08-18, on the SAME BALLOT
as the governor's race, Alaskans vote on a third number, $2,000.

**S6, from the community:** the AI framing of this story is the NATIONAL frame.
S6 read every pro-reform letter and op-ed in the window (ADN letter 07-25, ADN
op-ed by the League of Women Voters 07-27, Fairbanks News-Miner letter 07-08,
Dermot Cole 07-13) and NONE of them mentions AI at all. Four independent reform
advocates, zero AI mentions. Its conclusion, which the directors room should
treat as a design constraint:

> "If the deck opens on 'AI money enters Alaska politics' it is opening on the
> national frame. If it opens on 'a fifth of the front-runner's money came from
> six people, and you vote on whether that stays legal in eighteen days' it is
> opening on the Alaskan one."

So the deck's subject is THE RULEBOOK, and the donation is the exhibit. That
reframe also solves the dedupe problem more cleanly than the brief's own
recommendation, because No. 17 was a deck about a RECORD and this is a deck
about a RULE that is itself on a ballot with a countdown.

## THE FIVE FACTS THE DECK IS BUILT ON

1. **The statute still says $500.** AS 15.13.070(b), never repealed,
   unenforceable. (S2. Sourcing weakness, see below.)
2. **APOC enforces no number.** Its own limits table says Unlimited for an
   Alaska resident and a non-resident alike. Out-of-state GROUPS are prohibited;
   out-of-state PEOPLE have no ceiling. Bethel and Berkeley are treated
   identically. (S2, primary, dated 2026-07-29.)
3. **Ballot Measure 1 is on the August 18 primary ballot** and would write
   $2,000 back in. Verbatim text captured off the official Division of Elections
   sample ballot. (S2, primary.)
4. **Even if it passes it can't touch this race.** An initiated law takes effect
   ninety days after certification (Alaska Constitution Article XI Section 6),
   and the act's own applicability clause excludes elections on or before its
   effective date. (S2, primary, but the CONSEQUENCE is an inference; see the
   caution.)
5. **Alaskans wrote the $500 cap themselves, on an August primary ballot, in
   2006, with 73.00 percent.** 113,130 to 41,836, official Division of Elections
   returns. The symmetry is exact and it is the deck's best structural gift.
   (S2, primary.)

## THE INVERSION (S6's best find, and a real deck beat)

Alaska Watchman, 2026-07-27, speculated: "It is a mystery why an AI company in
California would try to influence an election here in Alaska", guessing the
motive was "looking for political influence to help it build data centers here."

That guess is the exact inverse of the record. The donor says he wants AI
slowed. The candidate he funded is the only one in the field calling for a
data-center moratorium. The hostile local framing assumed pro-data-center
intent; the verified record says the opposite.

## THE HONEST TURNS THE DECK SHOULD NOT DUCK

- **Measure 1 does not re-erect the border.** The sponsors' earlier petition
  (23RCFL) was titled to re-establish limits on out-of-state contributions. The
  petition that reached the ballot (23RCF2) dropped that clause. A YES vote
  lowers the ceiling for everyone; it does not treat a Berkeley donor
  differently from an Anchorage one. (S2, medium confidence, verify in the
  pamphlet.)
- **Caps hit the most transparent channel.** Independent expenditure groups and
  self-funding candidates are untouched. Heilala is the live proof. (S6.)
- **The plank has four holes.** No duration, no megawatt threshold, no
  exceptions, no named legal instrument, against a New York EO that specifies
  50 MW and up to one year. State them as gaps, not as criticisms. (S4.)
- **Sequencing.** Even neutral coverage framed the moratorium statement as a
  response to the funding disclosure. Expect the objection. (S6.)

## THE STRONGEST OBJECTION TO THIS DECK, AND THE ANSWER

S6 wrote it at full strength and it deserves to be answered in the artwork, not
in a disclaimer: a deck organized around a sincere signed op-ed picks the one
artifact in the story that is pure self-report, and publishes it during early
voting. It functions as earned media whether or not that is the intent. And a
publication called Alaska.Ai amplifying an Anthropic employee's self-explanation
invites the reader to ask who the deck is for.

The answer, and it is the reason the rulebook spine is the right one: the deck's
subject is not the donor. It is the rule, the ballot and the ninety days. The
op-ed is quoted for exactly one line, the line where the donor argues against
the rule that let him give. The concentration ratio, the ballot math and the
counterweights ride on the same slides.
