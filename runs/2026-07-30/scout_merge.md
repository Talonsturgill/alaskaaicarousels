## CANDIDATE A — The rulebook for large new load, written in closed rooms

**One line.** Alaska has no data center statute, cannot pass one before the
August 18 primary, and the instrument that will actually govern what a large
new load pays to interconnect on the Railbelt is being drafted on August 5 and
6 in three subcommittee meetings whose own public notices say the public may
not attend.

Source strength: the three RTO notices are Alaska Online Public Notice records
published 2026-07-28, 07-29 and 07-30, the freshest primary documents any
scout returned, each with a live forward date.

- Notice 224637, Technical Subcommittee, Wed Aug 5, 8:00 a.m., published 07-28
- Notice 224638, Tariff Subcommittee, Thu Aug 6, 2:00 p.m., published 07-30
- Notice 224639, Finance Subcommittee, Thu Aug 6, 3:00 p.m., published 07-29
- All three: Teams only, Alaska Energy Authority, 813 W. Northern Lights Blvd,
  Anchorage. All three carry "Per the RTO's Bylaws, the RTO's open meeting
  provisions do not apply to subcommittee meetings."
- Subcommittees may only recommend to the Governance Committee, which does
  hold public meetings.

Supporting chain (needs verification):
- SB 250 passed the Senate 2026-05-16 and died in House Community and Regional
  Affairs. It would have required commission-approved contracts so data center
  costs are borne by the operators rather than shifted to other customers.
- HB 259 (state utility standards for large data centers) sits in committee.
- The third special session convened 2026-07-27 with three of 40 House members
  and one senator present, and its call is limited to Alaska LNG tax terms, so
  no data center bill can move before the primary.
- Enstar, at a July 28 news conference, said it is evaluating whether it is
  prudent to add new customers, naming rising data center interest.

**HONESTY FLAG, carried into the fact-check.** None of the three RTO notices
names data centers, AI or large loads. The connection is structural, that an
open-access transmission tariff is the instrument a large new load
interconnects under. That has to be VERIFIED, not assumed, and if it cannot be
verified this candidate loses its spine.

## CANDIDATE B — GAIA, the Alaska-led landslide digital twin, starts Aug 1

**One line.** NSF has funded a six-year, $1,772,170 UAF award to build an
AI-driven digital twin that forecasts Alaska landslides by fusing seismic,
satellite and weather data, and its clock starts August 1, two days after this
run.

Source strength: the NSF Award API is primary and fully machine-readable.
Award 2608510, awarded 2026-07-10, performance 2026-08-01 to 2032-07-31, full
FY2026 obligation booked. PI Carl Tape, co-PIs Michael West (Alaska Earthquake
Center), Franz Meyer (InSAR, Alaska Satellite Facility), Ronni Grapenthin.
Companion UW award 2608509, $2,209,093, PI Marine Denolle. Combined $3,981,263.
Filed under NSF's Artificial Intelligence program element, the only July 2026
Alaska award under it.

Mechanism worth drawing: fast surrogate models that approximate expensive
physics simulations, coupled to incoming observations through data
assimilation, producing a continuously updating twin.

**ADJACENCY FLAG.** No single ledger entry duplicates this, but the
NEIGHBORHOOD has been visited three times in twenty days: No.3 (07-10) UAF AI
volcano monitoring on seismic plus InSAR, No.8 (07-15) an NSF UAF award, No.11
(07-19) a geophysical digital twin whose angle was "a forecast the ground
itself keeps correcting." A fourth machine-reads-the-Alaska-ground deck in
three weeks is the editorial repetition the dedupe rule exists to prevent even
though no single entry trips it. If this wins, the angle must not be the
mechanism (already told twice) and must not reuse No.11's framing.

## CANDIDATE C — A third of Alaska's aviation weather stations went dark

**One line.** More than 50 automated weather stations, just under a third of
the 137-station network Alaska aviation depends on, went offline around 10 a.m.
Monday July 27.

Strong Alaska texture (about 23 percent of Alaskans live off the road system),
peak flying season, a primary UAA ISER dataset tracking all 137 stations, and
FAA weather-camera figures (85 percent reduction in weather-related accidents).

**TEETH FLAG.** This is an automation and sensing story, not an AI story. The
AI framing would have to be argued rather than sourced. Weakest of the three on
the Alaska+AI mandate, strongest on pure Alaska texture.
