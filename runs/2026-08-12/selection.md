# SELECTION — 2026-08-12 — Carousel No.31

## THE STORY

**Alaska's one federal AI grid award finally has a price and an address. It is
$725,000, and it plugs into Cordova, a roadless town of about 2,600 that has
been running a small data center inside its own hydroelectric powerhouse.**

On August 11th, 2026 the University of Alaska Fairbanks and the Alaska Center
for Energy and Power each published a release on AURORA-AI, the single Alaska
project on the U.S. Department of Energy's Genesis Mission award list. The
release carries the award amount, the mechanism (utility data plus virtual
replicas of the grid, forecasting demand and detecting abnormal operating
conditions), and the partner list. On that list is Cordova Electric
Cooperative. (The fact-checker later killed the inference that Cordova supplies
the project's operating data. Neither release says so. It is a named partner
and that is all the record supports.)

Cordova is not a random test bed. It already put roughly 170 kilowatts of
computing inside the Humpback Creek run-of-river hydro plant, running on the
20 to 25 percent of its annual hydro output that would otherwise be spilled,
and the configuration was validated against a digital twin of Cordova's
microgrid built by the National Laboratory of the Rockies.

## WHY THIS ONE

1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** More than 200 Alaska communities run an island
   grid on diesel and whatever water or wind they have. A forecasting error
   there is a fuel barge order or a village outage in January, not an
   abstraction.
2. **Visual potential is the highest of the six beats.** Two hundred plus
   closed loops with no wire between them is the anti-network, and it is a
   picture of Alaska nobody draws. The nesting is literal, a server inside a
   powerhouse inside a microgrid inside a roadless town. The digital twin is a
   mirrored pair. The scale contrast is a real ratio between two real numbers.
3. **Tangibility.** A named principal investigator, a named cooperative, a
   named creek, a dollar figure, and a distance of about twenty feet from rack
   to generator.
4. **Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to a different
   coworker than usual. Every Alaska AI deck for a month has been about
   somebody arriving to take power. This is the one where the machine is
   pointed at the grid itself, and the town that got picked is one most
   Alaskans can find on a map.

## THE ARGUMENT THE DECK MAKES

Alaska's AI power debate is conducted almost entirely in gigawatts that do not
exist yet. The compute that actually runs in Alaska today, on Alaska's own
surplus power, is measured in kilowatts, and it sits about twenty feet from a
creek turbine in a town with no road out. When the federal government finally
put money into an Alaska AI grid project, that is the town it picked, and the
reason it picked it is that Cordova is the only place that had already done the
thing.

**The honesty rule this deck must not break.** The North Slope gigawatt figures
and the Cordova kilowatt figure are not competitors and are not on the same
grid. One is proposed, self-generated, behind the meter and on a different
basin. The other is operating on a village hydro microgrid. The deck states
that distinction in its own words wherever the two appear together. If the
gigawatt figures do not survive the fact-checker against a non-advocacy source,
they come off the slide and the contrast is made against something else.

stay fair, and a comparison that needs a paragraph is not a comparison.

**THE THESIS, RESTATED, AND EVERY PIECE OF IT IS NOW VERIFIED.**

Cordova has no road out and no wire out. Its own utility says so in its own
words, that the cooperative is not connected by transmission line to other
communities (C29). Because no wire leaves town, the water that runs through
Power Creek and Humpback Creek in the months nobody needs it can't be sold to
anybody, and the co-op's CEO estimates a fifth to a quarter of the hydropower
goes unused every year (C20). You can't export the electricity. So Cordova
imported the load instead, and put 170 kilowatts of computing on the floor of
the powerhouse, about twenty feet from the generators (C18, C22).

**Stranded power can't be shipped. Computation can be moved to it.** That is the
argument, it is the reason a town of about 2,600 is interesting to the United
States Department of Energy, and it is why Alaska's single row on a 278 project,
342 institution federal AI list (C11, C13) is a $725,000 project aimed at over
200 island grids (C01, C06) with Cordova named on the partner list (C04).

The last turn is the one worth the swipe. **The AI that got funded is not the AI
in the data center.** The data center is a customer. AURORA-AI is the software
that would run the grid underneath it, forecasting demand and catching abnormal
operating conditions (C05).

## THE WINNER, AND WHY

**THE FALLING FRAME is the spine.** Three reasons, in order.

1. It breaks the 24-to-30 family hardest. Every one of those seven decks held
   SCALE CONSTANT under one camera or one traverse. Moving scale is the one
   variable no recent deck has touched, and it is a structural break rather
   than a new subject wearing the old shape.
2. It is the argument's own shape. The thesis descends from a statewide network
   to a distance of twenty feet and then climbs back out to a claim about
   software. The deck's geometry and its argument are the same movement.
3. **It protects the deck from its own weakest evidence.** This is the decisive
   reason and it is not aesthetic. The record gives us a great deal about
   Cordova's SYSTEM (18 MW split three ways, 1,566 customers, more than 83
   percent in 2025, 20 to 25 percent unused, 170 kW, no wire out) and almost
   nothing about the ROOM. The field-documentarian's own honesty list runs to
   nine reconstructions including the building, the generator form, the
   cabinet dimensions, the light and the whole watershed. A deck whose spine is
   that room is a deck built on inference. The falling frame visits the room
   ONCE, at the bottom of the descent, where its one measured fact lives.

## ORGANS GRAFTED, AND FROM WHOM

1. **THE TWENTY-FOOT LOCK (field-documentarian).** The single best idea in the
   room. Derive the deck's entire scale system from C22, the only measured
   length in the story. 1 ft = 42 px, so the dimension is exactly 840 px and

2. **POWER IS THICK, CONTROL IS THIN (systems).** A line-weight law where the
   thesis is legible from stroke weight alone at 432 px with the type turned

   once per slide. Every control path is 1.25 px and phantom dashed.
3. **THE FIFTH SHELL, AND ITS OWN PROPOSED FIX (systems).** All three directors

4. **THE CAPACITY LADDER WITH A DECLARED MAGNIFICATION (systems).** 170 kW

5. **THE ISOTYPE FIELD WITH AN OPEN RIGHT EDGE (field-documentarian).** The

6. **THE REMOTENESS SURFACE (cartographer).** Real cartographic craft driven by

7. **THE PRINTED SCALE ON EVERY FRAME (systems and field-documentarian, both).**

   non-geographic slides print NO SCALE, NO GEOGRAPHIC CLAIM. This turns the

8. **THE MID-KEY LIT GROUND (field-documentarian).** Eight of the last decks
   were near-black and the contact-shadow gate has failed repeatedly because a
   shadow on an unlit ground has nothing to subtract from. A mid-key lit band
