Brandon Briggs's UAA team holds $3,824,575 of a $6 million NSF bet on microbes and AI for rare earths in coal refuse and ash.

Montana Tech and UAF hold the rest. The AI is to screen feedstocks with a Virtual Assay. Its controllers are to learn on microgrid simulators and adjust the bioreactor as available power changes. Deposit hunting with AI is a different NSF effort (the UAF-led Critical Mineral Accelerator Engine).

The award calls the system energy-constrained. In Alaska that means a tank that has to adapt when the power dips. Srijan Aggarwal leads UAF's part. Nothing is built yet. The award ends August 31st, 2030. Grade it on that date.
