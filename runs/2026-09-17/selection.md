# SELECTION — 2026-09-17 — Carousel No. 61

## THE DECISION

**The University of Alaska's AI rulebook.** A mandatory Generative AI Security
Standard has bound every user of UA computing resources since January 2025, with
termination and expulsion named as consequences. The university's own policy page
says system regulations are still under development and was last updated in March
2025. About 85 percent of college students already use generative AI in
coursework, and the system's Chief Information Technology Officer told the Board
of Regents this month that the university can't control and track every usage. A
Board of Regents AI policy goes to a vote in November.

The deck's argument is the gap between the rule that already carries expulsion and
the rules nobody has written yet.

## WHY IT PASSES THE FOUR CRITERIA

1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** UA is the state's only public university system.
   The standard already binds every user of UA computing resources across UAA, UAF
   and UAS, and the November policy would require all three campuses to carry an
   AI plan for students, faculty and staff.
2. **Visual potential.** The story is documentary and countable. A standard with
   an effective date and a review date two years out, a guidance page frozen in
   March 2025, three data classifications with different permissions, three
   campuses, one vote in November, and a stated inability to see what is already
   happening. The art can be the rulebook itself.
3. **Tangibility.** Anyone who has typed into a chatbot on a UA login is inside
   this story, and the standard names expulsion and termination.
4. **Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker.** If the coworker works at UA, yes,
   because most of them have not read the standard that already binds them.

## LANE CHECK (added after the docket triage, and it changed the thesis)

The docket already tracks `kpbsd-ai-policy-package`, the Kenai Peninsula Borough
School District's AI rulebook. Checking that against the topic ledger turns up two
decks in the same family:

- No.12, July 20th, "AI is already in class. The rulebook is still in pencil."
  Alaska schools writing the rules for AI in the classroom, anchored on KPBSD's
  proposed policy.
- No.30, August 9th, KPBSD's AR 6161.5 screen time regulation.

Both are outside the 30 day window, at 59 and 39 days, so the dedupe gate has
nothing to say. The LANE test that No.47's note demands is the one that matters,
and it very nearly caught this deck, because the obvious thesis for a University of
Alaska AI policy story is "the rules are arriving after the behaviour is already
universal", which is No.12's headline almost word for word.

So that is NOT this deck's thesis. The distinction is real and it is the story:

**A rule already exists, it already bites, and it is not the one going to a vote.**
The UA System Generative AI Security Standard has been in force since January 2025.
It binds every user of UA computing resources. It names network disconnection,
termination for employees and expulsion for students. Its next scheduled review is
January 2027. The university's own GenAI guidance page, last updated in March 2025,
says system regulations are still under development, and delegates classroom rules
to individual campuses and departments and calls the research rules under
development. Meanwhile the Chief Information Technology Officer told the Board of
Regents this month that the university can't control and track every usage of AI.

That is not a rulebook in pencil. It is a rulebook in ink that almost nobody has
read, sitting over a practice the institution says it can't see, with a second
rulebook arriving in November. A K-12 district writing its first policy and a
university system enforcing an eighteen month old standard it can't observe
compliance with are different stories with different consequences.

## THE DIRECTORS ROOM — SYNTHESIS (Phase 5 step 2)

Three lenses pitched, rotated from the last two trios (No.60 ran cartographer,
data-journalist, editorial-essayist; No.58 ran field-documentarian,
systems-illustrator, historian-of-the-future).

**ARCHIVIST, "THE STAMP LADDER."** A cabinet-oblique accession rail, every
document in the UA AI record standing at its own last-touched stamp. Its best
idea is an honesty rule that is also a drawing rule, A DOCUMENT WITH A PUBLISHED
DAY GETS A HAIRLINE TICK, A DOCUMENT WITH ONLY A MONTH GETS A BAND. The
precision of the mark equals the precision of the record. Its own self-critique
is correct and disqualifying as a chassis: a rail with cards on it is a timeline,
which is the most worn armature in the infographic world, and the deck has no
Alaska in it at all.

**EDITORIAL ESSAYIST, "FORE-EDGE STRATIGRAPHY."** A bound instrument seen along
its fore-edge. This pitch has the sharpest THESIS and the only real TURN of the
three, and the turn is the thing the story actually needs: the binding rule
governs WHERE DATA GOES, every argument in the room is about HOW PEOPLE LEARN,
and only one of those has a clause with expulsion in it. Its gold law is the
argument encoded as colour, gold marks language that is in force and nothing
else. Same fatal weakness as the archivist, by its own admission, it is a deck
about paperwork with no Alaskan in it.

**CINEMATOGRAPHER, "THE DRIFT LINE."** A Wyoming-pattern snow fence on open
tundra at blue hour, bay index as the calendar, panel porosity as the data
classification, a gauged gate as the conditional regime. This one wins the
chassis, for three reasons that are not about it being the prettiest.

1. A snow fence is the ONLY metaphor on the table that can't be misread as a
   ban. It is roughly half open by design and what it actually does is decide
   where the drift lands. That IS C16, the correction the fact-checker forced on
   this run, built into the object rather than asserted beside it. The deck's
   worst accuracy risk is answered structurally.
2. It solves the run's standing weakness by construction rather than by patch.
   The camera is above the snow and pitched down on every frame, mean ground
   fraction 0.69, subject centroid below 0.55H on all of them, and the bottom
   band carries the fence's own barred shadow, which is modeled, directional and
   information-bearing, because the bar duty cycle IS the porosity.
3. It is the only one of the three an Alaskan recognises on sight.

## THE DECISION, AND WHAT EACH LENS CONTRIBUTES

**DRIFT LINE is the chassis. The ESSAYIST's thesis and turn are the spine. The
ARCHIVIST's precision rule governs every date mark in the deck.**

- Thesis and turn from the essayist, promoted to their own frame. The deck now
  turns on data versus learning rather than merely listing a contradiction.
- The archivist's tick-versus-band rule binds every stamp on the fence. C01,
  C03, C04 and C30 publish a month and no day, so they get a BAND. C17, C20,
  C21, C33 and C40 publish a day, so they get a TICK. Nothing in this deck
  invents a precision the record does not carry, and the legend says so on the
  section slide.
- The gold law is the essayist's, tightened to the cinematographer's single
  object. Gold means the conditional opening and the language in force, so the
  gate edge, the effective-date stamp, the word "may", and the Polaris at close.
  It never touches the November stake line, which is forget-me-not and dashed,
  because November is a plan.
