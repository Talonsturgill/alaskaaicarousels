## Decision
UPDATE of No.97 (October 27th) and No.88 (October 16th): Alaska DNR's Final Finding and Decision on ADL 234762,
signed by Commissioner John Crowther on November 10th and posted that afternoon. It conveys about 6,800 acres
two miles north of Houston to AIDEA at no cost, down from 19,500 acres in the July 10th preliminary decision.

Material new development, stated on the cover as an UPDATE: the decision No.97 said could come before
December 7th has now come, and it changed shape. The parcel is about a third of its July size, the
reversionary interest the preliminary decision waived is back, and a 10-year survey-or-void condition is new.
No.97's own topics note said the next Houston deck "needs a new decision on the record (the Final Finding ...)".
This is that decision.

dedupe_check: LIKELY DUPLICATE against No.88 and No.97 (read in full); soft overlaps with No.85, No.104,
No.96, No.90, No.100, No.93. Semantic call: same thread, new decision, so it ships only as an explicit UPDATE.

Why it wins on the criteria:
1. Concrete Alaska impact: 6,800 acres of state land in the Mat-Su, 2,306 public comments answered, a
   reconsideration window that opens today, a new governor on December 7th.
2. Visual potential: the geometry is exact. The decision's legal description resolves to 170 quarter-quarter
   cells of 40 acres on the BLM survey grid (built into assets/geo/ak-houston-ffd-6800.geo.json), set inside
   the July outline. Four plats from 1915, 1917 and 1963. A soil inset of 65 percent upland.
3. Tangibility: a signed document with a date, a fee, a clock.
4. Would an Alaskan forward it: yes, the Houston transfer has been the most contested data center land story
   of the year, and the decision that ends DNR's part of it landed tonight.
