# SELECTION, 2026-11-06 (No.107)

## Decision
Outside AI money in Alaska's U.S. Senate race. An Alaska Beacon / States Newsroom analysis of FEC records (November 3rd) found that Alaska's Senate race drew more money from AI company employees than any other 2026 Senate race: at least 19 employees of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI gave about $119,000 to Sen. Dan Sullivan and Mary Peltola, none listing an Alaska address, most in California (C01 to C06). Peltola at least $56,000, nearly all from Anthropic employees; Sullivan about $63,000 to his campaign plus $35,000 to his True North leadership PAC (C07 to C11). FEC records show a second stream to Sullivan Victory from executives of Twenty First Century Utilities (the North Slope Power developer) and Saige Consulting in late April and on May 1st (C29 to C33), weeks after the Air Force offered about 4,700 acres at JBER, Eielson and Clear for AI data centers (C45 to C50). The race itself was separated by 637 votes in the unofficial count on November 4th, with about a quarter of votes uncounted and ranked choice tabulation on November 18th (C56 to C61).

Why it wins on the four criteria:
1. Concrete Alaska impact: Alaska's own Senate seat, the nation's top Senate race for AI-industry employee money, in a count separated by 637 votes. Named candidates, committees, firms, dates and dollar figures, much of it confirmable on FEC records (14 primary claims).
2. Visual potential: flows from outside into one state, a ranked national field with Alaska first, a $3,500 per election ceiling that most donors reached, two candidates' totals split by company, a dated calendar from the Air Force offer to the May 1st receipts, and a live count.
3. Tangible: filed FEC receipts and a published count, not a forecast.
4. Coworker test: every Alaskan voted in this race last Tuesday, and the money is the AI industry Alaska is debating hosting.

Fairness rule for the deck: both candidates' money is shown on the same scale; the data center executives' receipts are shown as FEC records on a calendar beside the Air Force dates, with the Campaign Legal Center's caveat (C25, C26) that employer data is not proof of coordinated giving. The Alaska Current's $58,000 tally is attributed (C37) and never stated as fact; the transfer-memo double count is respected (C30, C32, C33 are the same money as C29, C31 at the joint committee).
