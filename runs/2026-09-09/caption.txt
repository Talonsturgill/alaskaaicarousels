One sentence in federal law says the grid reliability rules don't apply to Alaska. Executive Order 14421 carries no sentence like it.

The order borrows the statute's definition of the bulk-power system almost word for word. It adds a threshold the statute never had at 69,000 volts. The Alaska Energy Authority puts the Alaska Intertie at 138 kilovolts and calls it part of the interconnected Bulk Electrical System. The Railbelt Reliability Council calls the same grid islanded.

DOE takes written comments through October 9th, 2026. Its rules are due 120 days from the order.

Both definitions sit side by side in the deck at identical size, and the order's stated reason names data centers and artificial intelligence.

Which Alaska institution should put the Railbelt's voltages in front of DOE before that window closes?
