## THE DIRECTORS ROOM, AND WHAT THE SYNTHESIS TOOK

Three lenses pitched in parallel and blind to each other.

- **CARTOGRAPHER, "NO SOUNDINGS INSIDE THE LINE".** Every slide a hydrographic
  survey sheet seen straight down, chart grammar throughout, and the argument
  carried by SOUNDING DENSITY falling to exactly zero inside the claimed limit.
- **DATA JOURNALIST, "THE ACRE BOARD".** A machined slab drilled 55 by 30 with
  1,650 sockets, one socket per acre of the envelope, quantities as turned pins
  seated in holes.
- **EDITORIAL ESSAYIST, "THE VACANT FIXTURE".** The applicant's own named
  component, the Universal Docking Cradle, recurring on every slide and always
  empty, with the whole argument a property of the gap between its jaws.

**THE WINNER IS THE VACANT FIXTURE**, for three reasons and one of them is
evidence rather than taste. The showrunner had already prototyped an empty
machined cradle through the real engine before the room reported, and it reads
unmistakably as an empty seat at full size and at thumb size. That is the exact
defect the last run was named for, a hero object that did not read as itself at
any size, and this hero is the one option in the room already proven not to have
it. Second, the argument IS the silhouette, so the crux sits in the art rather
than in the caption. Third, an empty cradle is a REAL OBJECT that is EMPTY,
which is the only clean answer to the leader gate on a deck whose subject is an
absence.

**GRAFTED FROM THE CARTOGRAPHER,** and it is the best single data-in-art mapping
the room produced. Sounding density is the record. On a chart a sounding is a
depth printed where somebody went and measured, so a field with no soundings is
ground nobody has surveyed. This deck's chart register draws the surveyed water
thick with numerals and the claimed envelope bare, and the density inside the
limit is EXACTLY ZERO. Nothing about that is decorative and a critic can count
it. The cartographer's paper-tooth surface pass, run at a different pitch on land
than on water, is grafted with it.

**GRAFTED FROM THE DATA JOURNALIST,** three things. The true-area nest at one
scale, which all three directors converged on independently and is therefore
almost certainly right. The stage-matched ORPC comparison, which is the best
number in the deck and which only this director found, since ORPC's own 2021
Cook Inlet preliminary permit application set a ceiling of no more than 5
megawatts and DeepGreen's permit-stage figure is 100. And THE CLAIM RULE, a
measured rail living in the bottom band of every slide whose UNIT changes with
the argument, which is a real continuity device and which structurally fixes the
dead lower zone rather than promising to watch out for it.

**REJECTED, with reasons.** The acre board's 1,650 drilled sockets as the deck
chassis, because nine slides on one plate is the repetition risk its own author
named first, and because it competes with the fixture for the same job. The
separation-film stack as the rendered beat, because transparent materials are
the one thing that renders badly on SwiftShader and the cradle is already
proven. The essayist's slide 07 billet metaphor, which its own author called a
pun one bad render from a chart in a costume.

**AND ONE LEDGER VIOLATION CAUGHT IN SYNTHESIS.** The essayist's atmosphere was
"azimuth 118 degrees, elevation 24 degrees". That is, to the degree, run No.37's
RAKING PRESS LIGHT, which the variety ledger forbids. The light below is
re-derived from this deck's own physics instead.

## THE TWO-TERM EXTINCTION MODEL, and why the chart's water colour is a claim

The chart register tints water by depth, and the tint is computed rather than
picked. Light in water falls off exponentially with path length and the
coefficient is wavelength dependent, so red dies first. Pure transmission would
be

    I = I0 * exp(-a * l)

and over the 50.6 m that 166 feet actually is, that drives every channel to zero
and renders two thirds of a frame flat black. The showrunner's second prototype
did exactly that and qa.py failed it top-loaded, correctly.

**Flat black is also physically wrong here**, which is the useful part. Rock
flour scatters at least as much as it absorbs, so silt water goes MILKY and never
dark. The model the deck uses is therefore the two-term one,

    I = I0 * exp(-a*l) + S * (1 - exp(-a*l))

where the first term is what survives the path and the second is what the
suspension sends back. Coefficients are `a = [0.62, 0.30, 0.22]` per metre for
red, green and blue, `I0 = #A2937B`, `S = #6A6152`. The chart's water tint at any
depth is that function of the depth in metres, so the colour on the chart IS the
depth, and the two isobaths the story actually published, 45 feet and 166 feet
(C14), are the only two tint steps.

**It returns an `rgb()` string.** Per the 0.97 instinct it is never fed back into
a hex parser, because `lerpHex` and its cousins return `rgb()`, a hex parser
turns that into NaN, and canvas silently keeps the previous fillStyle.
