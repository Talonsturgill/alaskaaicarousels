# SELECTION, 2026-10-24, No.95

## Decision

THE STORY: the Kenai Peninsula Borough Assembly enacted Ordinance 2026-35 on October 20th, 7 to 0
with two absent, adding KPB 17.10.165. Anyone seeking a commercial lease or land use permit on
borough land for weather modification or geoengineering must now name the intended outcome and list
ten things (chemicals, quantities, flares by serial number and weight, pyrotechnic total, safety data
sheets, frequency, flight duration and altitude, total duration, maximum flights, release coordinates
and predicted impact areas), file a signed closeout statement, and face $1,000 a day and a five year
ban for skipping it. It is effective immediately. Casual-use borough land is closed to it outright.

Why it exists: on August 23rd Rainmaker Technology Corporation flew two autonomous Elijah drones
(EL-151 and EL-153, sub-55 lb UAVs) into supercooled cloud over the Kenai Peninsula and released 19
silver iodide flares, about 374 grams, in seven missions over three hours. Rainmaker's own radar
estimate is 57.6 acre-feet (about 19 million gallons), which DEC puts at about 0.01 inches over the
area analyzed. The borough learned of it from Rainmaker's press release. DEC's commissioner wrote that
Alaska has no regulatory framework specific to weather modification and no DEC permit is required;
DNR issued a letter of no objection; the federal Weather Modification Reporting Act is reporting
only. The borough, a second-class borough, says it can't regulate the airspace, so it regulated the
ground the drones launch from.

The position the deck can take: the only Alaska rule on record for autonomous aircraft that change the
weather (DEC: no state framework) governs the launch pad, not the sky. Never print "first" unless a
claim carries it. Its ten-item list is the only public checklist any
Alaska government has written for this; the Legislature (urged by the borough's own Resolution
2026-046 in September) has not. That is a who-decides story with a concrete artifact a reader can
keep.

AI / automation fit: Beat E (robotics and autonomy deployments). Rainmaker's own page describes
Elijah as "capable of autonomous and sustained high-altitude flight". The validation report names no
AI or machine learning, so the deck never says AI did the seeding; it is autonomous drones and modeled
radar estimates, said plainly.

Criteria: (1) concrete Alaska impact, a borough code section in force since October 20th, and a live House District 6 issue; (2) visual potential very high: a sky
over Kachemak Bay with seven radar signatures, two drones, 19 flares, a layered stack of
jurisdictions (FAA airspace, state with no framework, borough land); real DEM for Kachemak Bay
committed (assets/geo/ak-kachemak-dem.bin); (3) tangible, a ten-line form and a fine; (4) an Alaskan
forwards it: it is odd, true and local.
