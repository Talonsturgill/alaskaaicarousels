# "THE ROOM AND THREE ADDRESSES": where a Palmer room's answer on AI data centers can go

## THESIS
On October 4th the Mat-Su Sentinel asked a forum audience in Palmer's Glenn Massey Theater, live on their phones, "Should the state restrict large AI/data center development in areas like Mat-Su?", and 57 of 112 answered "Outlaw them entirely" (47 strict regulations, 8 allow). The question was addressed to "the state", and on Mat-Su land the state is three rooms at three distances from that theater: DNR holds the proposed 19,950-acre conveyance near Houston to AIDEA (comments closed September 14th, a preliminary decision, no final one announced as of late September); the University of Alaska signed a 20-year lease of 829 acres at Port MacKenzie in late May; and a data center bill, SB 250, passed the Senate 14 to 5 and stopped in a House committee. Of those three, only the Legislature had its candidates in the room: 22 of them, for House Districts 25 to 30 and Senate Districts M and O, with early voting opening October 19th. The deck takes no side on data centers. Its position is structural: a room's answer travels only as far as the rooms it can reach.
PDF document title (copy.json): "112 Answers in Palmer, and Where They Go" (40 chars).
