## THE DECISION (as first made, subsequently corrected)

**H.R. 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act, and the exemption Alaska can't claim.**

A federal bill reported out of House Energy and Commerce on September 10th as
H. Rept. 119-814 would amend the Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act so that
a "large-load customer", defined around information technology infrastructure
with peak demand of 100 megawatts or more at one site or campus, pays the full
incremental cost of any generation, transmission or distribution upgrade built
to serve it. State regulatory authorities get a federal clock to make that
determination.

The bill excuses states that already implemented a comparable standard, already
ran a proceeding on it, or whose legislature already voted on it. Alaska did
none of the three. SB 250, which would have required commission-approved
contracts assigning data center costs to data centers, died when the 34th
Legislature adjourned. So the Regulatory Commission of Alaska would inherit a
federal deadline on precisely the question Juneau declined to answer, and the
Legislature does not reconvene until January 19th, 2027.

## WHY THIS ONE, against the four criteria in order

**1. Strongest concrete Alaska impact.** This is the rare federal action whose
Alaskan consequence is not a forecast but a deduction. The bill names a
mechanism, a threshold, a clock and a body. The body is the RCA. The reason the
clock reaches Alaska is a specific dated failure in Juneau that anyone can look
up. Nothing here requires the deck to speculate about what Alaska might do.

**2. Visual potential.** A threshold is a line. A clock is a length. An
exemption with three conditions is three doors. The story hands the art a
measured axis (megawatts), a measured span (one year, then two), and a set that
Alaska is outside of. Every one of those is drawable as quantity rather than as
illustration, which is what story-art fusion scores on.

**3. Tangibility.** Beat F's framing note is the sharpest thing in the sweep and
it decides the register of this deck. Alaskans denominate this argument in the
household, in this order, the electric bill, noise, water, viewshed, land. Mayor
Grier Hopkins gave the canonical form of it. A deck about cost allocation that
leads with gigawatts is speaking the Outside dialect; a deck that leads with who
pays is speaking Alaskan. The bill is literally a bill about the bill.

**4. Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** A Railbelt utility analyst, an
RCA staffer, a borough attorney, a legislative aide drafting for January. Yes,
and several of them do not know this is coming, because it is a Washington
procedural step reported in trade press.

# POST-CLAIMS RE-CUT (written after the fact-checker returned)

The fact-checker did its job and killed the deck's central Alaska claim. This
section records what changed and why, because the shape of the correction is
more useful to a later run than the conclusion.

## WHAT DIED

The scout's framing was that Alaska missed the exemption because SB 250 died in
committee. Five claims that framing rested on came back unverified or wrong:

1. **"Alaska has done none of the three things that would exempt it."** Requires
   proving three negatives and no Alaska legislative or commission source was
   reachable. Worse, the fact-checker noticed that prong two is satisfiable by
   the Regulatory Commission of Alaska ALONE with no legislature at all, so
   Alaska could already qualify without anyone in Juneau doing anything.
2. **Everything about SB 250.** akleg.gov, LegiScan and the Beacon all refused.
   Zero sources reached on the first pass.
3. **Gov. Dunleavy signed the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.** The White House
   page gives a count of 23 governors and names none of them.
4. **187 organizations signed the pledge.** The primary page says 300 plus. The
   scout's figure would have been a checkable error on a slide.
5. **Any cosponsor count, and anything about the Alaska delegation.** No
   cosponsor list was ever read. congress.gov 403s.

It also caught a thing the whole run would otherwise have gotten wrong. The
exemption's cutoff is **the date of enactment**, which has not happened. It is
not a closed door. Every state, Alaska included, can still walk through it, and
the bill is on the Union Calendar with no date, verified against two separate
published schedules. A deck built on "Alaska missed it" would have been false.

## WHAT REPLACED IT, AND IT IS BETTER

Two showrunner fetches reached SB 250 where the fact-checker's routes had not,
and the fact is the opposite of the scout's:

**SB 250 passed the Alaska Senate on May 16th and died in House Community and
Regional Affairs.** Davis Wright Tremaine's energy practice, June 2026, "Alaska's
principal data center bill, Senate Bill 250 (SB 250) was sent to the Alaska
House of Representatives after passing the Senate on May 16, 2026." BillTrack50
agrees, crossed over May 16th, dead May 20th, last action a House committee
hearing on May 18th.

Set that beside the exemption text the fact-checker verified word for word. The
third prong excuses a state where "the State legislature has voted on the
implementation of the standard (or a comparable standard)".

**One chamber voted. The other never did. Nobody has said whether that counts.**

## THE THESIS, RE-CUT

A federal bill would make any customer drawing 100 megawatts pay the full
incremental cost of the grid built to serve it, and it excuses any state that
moved first. Alaska is half moved. Its Senate passed a comparable standard in
May and its House let it die two days later, and whether that half counts as
"the State legislature has voted" is a question with a real consequence that no
one has answered in public.

The deck does NOT assert Alaska is in or out. That is the point. It states the
mechanism, the three doors, and the one word the answer turns on. This is a
better deck than the one selected this morning because the honest version has a
sharper argument than the wrong version did.

## WHAT THE DECK MAY NOT SAY, carried forward as a hard list

- Not "scheduled". The bill is on the Union Calendar and on no published
  schedule, verified twice. "Eligible for floor action" is the accurate phrase.
- No calendar date on the one year or two year clocks. They run from an
  enactment that has not happened.
- Nothing about Begich, Sullivan or Murkowski and this bill.
- Nothing about Gov. Dunleavy and the pledge.
- No cosponsor count.
- Not "187 organizations". The White House says 300 plus.
- Not the DeepGreen 100 megawatt figure beside the 100 megawatt threshold. The
  FERC notice calls it annual generation, which is energy, and the threshold is
  peak demand, which is power. Identical numerals, different quantities. Pairing
  them would be a false equivalence that looks like the deck's best visual and
  is actually its worst error.
- Nothing asserting what the RCA has or has not done, in either direction.
