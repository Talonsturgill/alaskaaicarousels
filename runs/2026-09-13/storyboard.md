# STORYBOARD — Carousel No. 58 — 2026-09-13
# "THE UNSET BENCHMARK"

## THE ROOM, and how the winner was picked

Three lenses pitched. **Systems-illustrator** brought THE OPENED BAY, a
switchgear cabinet in cabinet oblique exploded along its bus, with a
three-position selector whose gold contact sits unseated between detents.
**Historian-of-the-future** brought THE UNSET BENCHMARK, a bronze monument disc
on a green night pour carrying one punched field labelled DATE OF ENACTMENT that
stays bare on all nine frames. **Field-documentarian** brought NINE THINGS YOU
COULD WALK UP TO, one observed object per frame against a lamplit wall with a
gold tag wired to it.

**THE UNSET BENCHMARK WINS, on one argument.** It is the only treatment whose
motif REFUSES TO RESOLVE. The other two seal into a Polaris at slide 09, which
is the house habit and which would have the artwork quietly assert a conclusion
the copy explicitly refuses. This story's whole point is that nobody has ruled
and the date everything would be measured from does not exist yet. A field that
is drawn nine times and never filled says that with no words, and it is the
first deck in this series whose closing frame leaves its own motif open.

Its second strength is the gold law, which is the best colour reasoning any of
the three produced. **Gold marks a thing that actually happened and is written
down.** That makes the accent budget carry the argument instead of decorating
it, and it makes the bare field's absence of gold a measurable statement rather
than a mood.

Its third is the type well, which is the most physically honest of the three. A
concrete finisher makes two passes, a magnesium float that leaves aggregate
showing and a steel trowel that polishes a band smooth. The words live on the
troweled band. The material genuinely gets smoother and brighter as it
approaches them, which is exactly what this run's standing-weakness brief asked
for and is the opposite of a plate laid on top.

### WHAT THE WINNER TAKES FROM THE OTHER TWO

**FROM THE FIELD-DOCUMENTARIAN, THE COVER, and it fixes the winner's own named
weakness.** The historian's self-critique was right that surveyors' hardware
sits one abstraction step from the household bill, which Beat F says is the
dialect this argument is actually held in. The field-documentarian's cover is a
residential watt-hour meter on an Anchorage wall at two in the morning with a
gold tag wired to its sealing ring. That is the household on frame one. It is
adopted, and it is unified with the winner's chassis rather than bolted to it,
because the pad in this deck is a SERVICE pad and a meter stands at its head.
The camera starts at the meter and dollies away from the house along the pour,
which is the literal path of the question, and the meter returns small at the
far left edge of slide 09 as the thing all of it is measured toward.

**FROM THE SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, THREE THINGS.** Its unit guard discipline, which
names the power-versus-energy trap in print on the threshold slide rather than
merely avoiding it. Its own self-critique's fix, that ambiguity must be a
property of the machine from the moment the machine is introduced, so socket
three already carries a ghost plug on slide 05 and not only at the turn. And its
one addition to the factual fence, which is adopted whole: **no Cook Inlet
imagery anywhere in this deck's art.** A subsea silhouette anywhere near a
megawatt scale would invite the power-versus-energy equation the fence forbids
even with no text making it.

**WHAT ALL THREE FLAGGED AND WHAT IS DONE ABOUT IT.** Every director named
sameness across nine frames as their concept's real exposure. The answer is a
declared CAMERA DISTANCE LADDER, fixed here before any code is written and
checked on the contact sheet by the flow critic, plus a deliberately varied
object silhouette per frame. Both are in the header below.

## HERO STRUCTURE

**NEW: THE UNSET BENCHMARK.** A concrete service pad, poured tonight, still
green, beside a residential service entrance at the head of it. The camera sits
low, about 400 mm off the deck, looking along the pad so its chamfered front lip
runs across the bottom of every frame as a lit form with a dark riser under it.
On the pad lies a cast bronze monument disc, lettered with things that are true,
carrying one punched rectangular field labelled `DATE OF ENACTMENT` which is
**bare on all nine frames and never fills.** Every span in the deck is
dimensioned from that bare field with drafting furniture.

Divergence from the forbidden four, stated as required. It is not the stamped
field and the printed page, because this is cast metal and wet concrete at night
and there is no paper in the deck. It is not the struck register, because
nothing is pressed into damp stock and the register is a horizontal ground plane
rather than a plate. It is not the sheet and its margin, because there is no
neatline window and no margin, and the type sits IN the material rather than
outside it. It is not the occupied station descended, because the camera dollies
LATERALLY along a ground plane and there is no shared depth-to-pixel mapping.
And it is not the curved transect, because the camera travels in a straight line
at a constant height and the subject is an object, not a route.

varied per slide: **azimuth 152 degrees, elevation 22 degrees, key to fill 6.0

## THE FOUR ANSWERS to this run's standing-weakness brief

**1. THE TYPE WELL IS THE TROWELED BAND, AND IT IS DRAWN FIRST.**

A concrete finisher makes two passes. A magnesium float leaves aggregate
showing. A steel trowel polishes a band smooth. That band is where every word in
this deck lives and it is part of the pour, not a plate on it.

After `await document.fonts.ready` the loop measures every text block **per line
box** via a Range and `getClientRects`, so a ragged block reserves ragged rects
and the hole follows the rag, rather than reserving the paragraph's bounding
rectangle, which is the plate this whole idiom exists to avoid. It then builds a
distance field to those rects.

**Declared density ramp.** Aggregate stone probability
`p = clamp((d - 28) / 96, 0, 1) ^ 1.6`, where `d` is distance in design px to
the nearest reserved line box. Inside 28 px no stone is **generated**, not
covered. From 28 to 124 px it ramps in. Simultaneously the surface's specular
roughness FALLS as `d` falls, so the material gets smoother and slightly
brighter as it approaches the words, which is what a trowel physically does.

The only marks permitted inside 28 px are the finisher's swept circular polish
arcs at 0.75 px hair and under 6 percent contrast. **There are zero plates, zero
scrims and zero knockouts in this deck.** Where a slide's own loop draws the
field rather than the bench, reserves are punched with
`AKENGRAVE.punchReserves(ctx, boxes, {blur: 9})`, which is a destination-out
punch of BLURRED RECTANGLES on an offscreen canvas, never a radial gradient.

**2. THREE SCALES OF DETAIL, THREE DIFFERENT GENERATORS.**

- **MACRO, reads at 432 px.** The object silhouette and the lit pour crest
  against night. Generator: **akthree GPU PBR (#87)** on slides 01 and 04,
  bronze and steel presets, 2048 px PCFSoft shadows, ACES, `setPixelRatio`
  before `setSize`, `await AKT.snapshot(R)` with `.ok` checked against the
  black-frame sentinel, rendered offscreen and composited. Every akthree slide
  carries a designed **AK3D painter's solid (#37)** fallback for
  `AKT.webglOK() === false`, in which only the object swaps because the pour is
  2D in both paths. All other frames use the AK3D path directly.
- **MID, resolves near 1080 px.** The drafting register. Dimension calls (#73),
  leader discipline (#72), title block furniture (#83), hatch knockout windows
  (#75), the scribed threshold, the punch tally. Authored as SVG world-coordinate
  geometry so the gates can see it.
- **MICRO, rewards a zoom.** Two seeded populations and neither one is grain.
  (a) **Concrete.** Poisson-disk exposed aggregate (#66) with a LOGNORMAL stone
  size distribution, Box-Muller off a seeded mulberry32, each stone carrying a
  lit crescent and a dark occlusion arc from the one global light, plus air
  voids drawn as dark rings with a lit lower lip. (b) **Metal.** Concentric
  lathe tooling grooves on the bronze at 0.6 px with varying pitch and phase
  jitter, and a diagonal grind lay on the steel.

Grain is a 6 percent final pass applied as `AK.grainTile`, never a full-frame
turbulence rect, and it is NOT counted as one of the three scales.

**3. NOTAN BEFORE PIXELS.** Three masses, stated here so
`scripts/value_structure.py` and this plan speak one language:

- **Mass A, DOMINANT.** The unlit pad and the night. L\* 10 to 16. **About 57
  percent of the frame.** It reaches into the bottom third along the left edge
  and across the far side of the pad on every frame.
- **Mass B.** The lit pour wedge. L\* 42 to 52. About 30 percent. It runs lower
  left to mid right and **owns the bottom third from x 0 to about x 760 on every
  frame.**
- **Mass C.** The objects and their struck marks. L\* 62 to 80. About 12
  percent, with exactly ONE specular glint per slide above L\* 88, which is the
  deck's single hottest point on that frame.

The darks are welded into one connected shape by construction, because the night
and the unlit pad are the same mass and they meet at the pad's far edge.

**4. THE BOTTOM THIRD IS COMPOSED, NOT LEFT OVER.**

The pad's **chamfered front lip** runs across the bottom of all nine frames as a
real lit form, top face lit by the key, vertical riser dark, with an ambient
falloff below it. On top of that, each slide carries at least one of: the
object's two-part contact shadow; the dimension furniture run down into the
band; or a blurred repoussoir stone bleeding off the lower left corner.

**Contact shadows, in the only order that measures.** `litPool` first, then
`cast`, then `seam`. Cast at `foot + 2`, pool centre at `foot + 14`, pool width
in the 70 to 110 design px range whatever the object's own width. Both rects are
declared **side by side at the object's base line**, the shadow over the cast
trough and the ground over the pool peak beside it, never stacked. Every pair is
**measured off the render with `scripts/contact_probe.py --slide N --base cx,cy`
and pasted**, never computed from camera arithmetic. The floor is 4.0 L\* and it
is not a target; if the contact reads, it stops there.

# BUILD RECONCILIATION

Written after the build and before any critic was spawned. Every number below
is what the RENDER carries, not what the dossier planned. A critic measuring
this deck measures against this section.

**WHAT DID NOT SHIP AT ALL**

**THE DECK ARGUES FLAT, AND HERE IS THE ARGUMENT.** Every frame in this deck
is one material under one raking lamp: wet concrete, still green, with steel and
bronze set into it. What that material rewards is not a physically based
renderer, it is a heightfield. The pad's tooth, the trowel's sweep, the
lathe rings on the bronze, the walls of a punched recess and the burr around a
struck mark are all DISPLACEMENT, and `AK.reliefShade` shades displacement
directly from a real height function at 2x, which is why slide 08's fifty-two
punch craters survive a zoom and why the recess law reads identically on nine
frames. A GPU PBR pass would have bought reflections and roughness this
subject has almost none of, at the cost of the one thing it lives on, which is
that every square millimetre of a shallow dome under a 22 degree key has real
gradient in it. The hero is Canvas plus `AK.reliefShade` because that is the
right tool for a poured surface, and the deck is uniform in it: there is no
frame where a rendered object stands beside a drawn one.

That is the argument. What follows is what it cost to get there, because the
same rung took three builds and the reason is worth keeping.

**The GPU rung.** No frame loads `akthree`. Slide 04's hero was planned as a
software-3D form and is Canvas plus `AK.reliefShade`, because the first three
builds of that rung produced a flat tan ellipse and the fix, when it came, was
a callback contract error rather than a rendering one. `height(u,v)` takes
NORMALISED 0 to 1 and returns 0 to 1; the build was passing pixels in and
pixels out, so every sample fell outside the domain and came back 0. The form
now reads, at `strength 34`, `lathe 0.0052` and `fbm 0.0035`. The first tune
after the fix read like a vinyl record and the second like a fingerprint, which
is what a relief shader does when its two high frequency terms outrun its
macro dome.

**#75, the hatch knockout window for the unit guard on slide 06.** Dropped. It
is a plate, and the deck committed to zero plates at plan time. The guard
sentence is set on the pour and the die stands beside the strike it made.

**ROUND TWO, WHAT THE PIXEL ROOM CHANGED**

Five critics read the nine frames. None of them said ship. Their finding was
the same one nine times over in different clothes, and it is worth naming
plainly, because it is the standing weakness this run set out to attack.

**Every display headline broke where the fitter ran out of room, not where the
sentence ended.** `AK.fitText` honours `<br>` and soft-wraps anything you do not
author, so a headline handed to it as one string is a headline whose line breaks
are an accident. Slide 02 read `ONE CUSTOMER, CHARGE` with the verb orphaned
from its object. Slide 03 ended a line on `THE` immediately after a full stop.
Slide 08 put the word `SCHEDULED.` alone on its second line, in a deck whose
factual fence forbids that word near this bill. Every one of the nine now
carries its breaks as authored `<br>`, and the boxes were widened to the width
the longest authored line needs so the fitter never has to choose. Type sizes
came down between 6 and 30 px to buy it, which is the right trade: a smaller
headline that breaks at its sense boundary beats a bigger one that does not.

**Three body paragraphs had silently lost their last sentence.** Slide 02 had
dropped `A large-load customer draws 100 megawatts or more at one site or
campus.`, which is the definition the whole frame exists to carry, leaving the
number 100 on that frame only as a raster label the ranker can't read. Slide 05
had dropped `The window is still open, because enactment has not happened.`, the
sentence that plants the loop. Slide 06 had dropped `and took no floor vote.`,
the clause carrying C46 in prose. All three are restored, and the columns around
them re-set.

**The deck's spine was missing from four frames.** The punched DATE OF ENACTMENT
field is declared present and bare on all nine, and frames 02, 06, 07 and 08 did
not draw it at all. It is now one function, `AKPOUR.bareField`, so there is a
single implementation of the deck's one motif rather than five.

**Three objects were lit from the wrong side.** Slide 01's meter dome measured
59.6 L* upper left against 34.5 lower right on a frame whose own quadrant means
put the lamp at the lower right. Slide 09's struck dates had an emissive halo
and their two edge strokes the wrong way round, so three marks meant to be
struck INTO the pad read as glowing plaques. Slide 04's punched field was a
near-black rectangle with no material response, which reads as a REDACTION and
therefore argues that a date exists and is hidden, the exact opposite of this
deck. All three now obey the same law the reconciliation already recorded for
slide 07: a recess under a raking lamp is lit down its FAR wall.

**Slide 06 printed a bare `MAY 16`** inside the gold strike, with `MAY 16TH`
in the mono label ten pixels below it. The house rule on ordinals was written
about exactly this surface, mono caps on artwork.

**Slide 05's two label columns had merged into one sentence.** The earlier
`.l2` widening to 314 px nowrap put `NEEDS NO LEGISLATURE` and `HAS VOTED` on
one baseline ten pixels apart, so the row read `NEEDS NO LEGISLATURE HAS VOTED`,
which asserts the single thing this deck's fence is most careful never to say.
Both columns are narrower, smaller and further apart.

**Slide 02's unit guard printed straight through the chart's own axis label**,
destroying the leading digits of `100 MW`. The guard moved out of the chart.

**ROUND THREE, WHAT THE SECOND PIXEL ROOM CHANGED**

Three critics read the revised nine. None said ship, and their best finding was
one nobody had named yet.

**The troweled specular was a plate, and then it was a legibility defect.** Four
critics on four different frames reported a faint straight sided lift behind the
type, on a deck whose header says there are zero plates in it. The cause was one
line in `AKPOUR.troweled`: the specular was a LINEAR gradient inside a
`fillRect`, and a linear gradient does not reach zero at its own left and right
