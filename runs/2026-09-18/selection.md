# SELECTION, 2026-09-18, carousel No. 62

## THE DECISION

**A thousand kilometres out of Nenana, and the answer is still in a freezer.**

On August 19th ACUASI, the University of Alaska Fairbanks drone programme, flew
a Windracers ULTRA 1,000 kilometres out of Nenana Municipal Airport carrying
human blood for the International Testing Agency, the body that runs
anti-doping for most of Olympic sport. The flight lasted 8 hours and 47
minutes. It began and ended at the same airport. The ITA published it on
September 16th, UAF the same day, Sysmex on the 17th.

What was published is the FLIGHT. The FINDING is not published, and the ITA's
own release says so, that the results of the scientific analysis will be
communicated once the evaluation has been completed and that it intends to
submit them to a peer-reviewed journal. The question the whole exercise exists
to answer, whether nearly nine hours of vibration, pressure and cold does
anything to white and red blood cell populations, is open.

## WHY THIS ONE, AGAINST THE CRITERIA IN ORDER

**(1) Strongest concrete Alaska impact.** Alaska supplied every hard part of
this. The distance, the empty airspace, the aircraft, the pilots, and a
university unit that is one of nine FAA-designated test sites. A global sports
body ran its longest sample-transport test here rather than in Europe, and the
reason is the thing Alaskans are usually asked to apologise for, which is how
far apart everything is. The payoff is named by ACUASI's own director, nurses
going into a community, taking samples, and getting the medication back before
they leave.

**(2) Visual potential.** The best of any candidate this run, and it is
structural rather than decorative. The flight is a CLOSED LOOP, a thousand
kilometres that returns to its own start, and the Doha trial it supersedes is
about twenty kilometres, which at the same scale is a dot. A deck can draw that
honestly and the geometry carries the argument with no words. Five sensor
channels, 8h47m, a 31 foot wingspan and a 200 pound bay are all drawable at
true scale.

**(3) Tangibility.** Blood in a chilled box, flying itself, for most of a
working day, over the Interior.

**(4) Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to two kinds of
coworker. It is a genuine Alaska accomplishment, and it carries a real edge
that a village clinic administrator will feel immediately.

## THE ARGUMENT, STATED PLAINLY SO THE DECK CAN BE FAIR

This is not a debunk. The test is legitimate, the capability is real, and
sample integrity is exactly the right thing to establish before anyone trusts
an aircraft with a diagnosis. The deck's position is about GEOMETRY and about
WHO GETS THE ANSWER.

The aircraft returned to its departure airport and the laboratory was set up in
a hangar at that airport, so the flight measured whether blood survives the
journey and did not measure a delivery. Those are different questions, and the
one rural Alaska needs answered is the second. The result, when it comes, will
be communicated by an anti-doping body and submitted to a journal, on a
timetable nobody in Alaska sets, for a purpose that is not Alaska's. Alaska
supplied the distance and gets to wait for the finding like everyone else.

That is a fair reading and a position, which is the house voice, and it leaves
the accomplishment standing.
