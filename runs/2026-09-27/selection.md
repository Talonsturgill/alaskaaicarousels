## Decision

LEAD: Bradley Lake's Dixon Glacier diversion and the Railbelt winter. The peg is KTUU's September 24th
report: AEA hosted three of FERC's five commissioners at Bradley Lake, and Curtis Thayer said "there's
nothing for tomorrow" while Fairbanks burns 250,000 gallons of diesel a day (C01 to C21). The filed
record behind the peg is primary: FERC's Federal Register notice on P-8221-124 (C25 to C31) and AEA's
July 20th and 23rd releases plus its Dixon page (C32 to C46).

Why it is an Alaska.Ai deck and not an energy deck: the AI siting pitch on the Railbelt runs into the
same calendar. The Air Force offered about 4,700 acres in twelve parcels for AI data centers (C53, C54)
and its own solicitation said the JBER sites have insufficient power and need a new Chugach connection
(C50). Chugach's Hilcorp gas contract ends in 2028 (C51). The one AI data center plan on the state record
that does not wait for the Railbelt is STAK's, behind the meter on the North Slope (C55 to C58).

The cover pairs the promise with its date: about 38 percent more electricity a year (C30), and a dam
slated for 2030 (C06). The energy versus megawatts point (C28, C29) pays off on 02 and 04 as the reason
the promise is worth waiting for, not as the thesis (No.25 ran that angle).
