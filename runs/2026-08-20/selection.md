# SELECTION — Carousel No. 38 — 2026-08-20

## THE STORY

**Proclamation 11055 and Alaska's drone-in-a-box network.** On August 13th,
2026 the President signed Proclamation 11055, published in the Federal Register
on August 19th, setting a 100 percent import duty on uncrewed aircraft over 25
kilograms, on any UAS that integrates a thermal imager, and on UAS docking
stations, with 25 percent on aircraft at or under 25 kilograms. The first
tranche takes effect at 12:01 a.m. eastern on September 3rd, 2026. A second
tranche, on components, follows February 9th, 2027.

Alaska is the state that spent a $12.4 million federal SMART grant building a
rural response network out of exactly the two hardware categories drawing the
100 percent rate: thermal-capable drones and docking stations, flown beyond
visual line of sight over villages that have no road.

The twist, and the reason this is a deck and not an alarm, is a single word in
the proclamation: IMPORTS. The drone Alaska bought, the Skydio X10, is designed
and assembled in the United States, so the September 3rd tranche does not reach
it. The exposure that does exist is quieter and later, the February 2027 duty on
imported components, and it lands on the parts inside an American-assembled
airframe rather than on the airframe itself. The deck's whole argument is that
distinction. A tariff written to hit Chinese drones reorganizes the cost of a
tool Alaska already chose to buy American, and the bite is a season away, on the
supply chain rather than the sticker.

## WHY THIS ONE

- **Concrete Alaska impact.** Alaska has more distance and fewer roads than any
  state, so a drone that reaches a village in twenty minutes is not a gadget, it
  is the difference in a search. A federal cost shock to that exact hardware is
  Alaska's story more than anyone's.
- **Visual potential.** A tariff staircase (25 to 100 percent), a two-tranche
  timeline (signed August 13th, first bite September 3rd, components February
  9th 2027), a taxonomy of what the duty catches by silhouette (light aircraft,
  heavy aircraft, thermal payload, dock), and an Alaska map of a rural dock
  network. Quantity, date and place all present.
- **Tangibility.** The reader can hold the object: a drone in a box on a
  riverbank, a thermal image of a person in the dark, a customs line item.
- **Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker?** A borough emergency manager, a
  trooper, a village council, a DOT engineer, all touch this and none of them
  has read the proclamation. The deck tells them whether their next dock costs
  more and when.

## THE ANGLE, STATED FLAT

The deck does NOT say "a tariff will cripple Alaska's drones." That would be
false, because the aircraft is American-made and the first tranche misses it.
The deck says the more interesting and true thing: the policy aimed at foreign
drones still reaches Alaska, just not where the headline points. It reaches the
components, in February, inside a plane that counts as American. Alaska bought
American on purpose and the tariff found a seam anyway. That is the thesis.
