# SCOUT MERGE — run No.46, 2026-08-31

Six scouts, six returned, 149 WebSearch calls spent against a 150 ceiling.
Window 2026-08-21 to 2026-08-31.

## The shape of the day

Five of six beats came back pointing at the same argument from different
sides: Alaska is being asked to decide about machine infrastructure faster
than it has rules for it. Beat A found a borough asking the state for a
moratorium. Beat D found two federal comment windows and an executive order.
Beat F found four organizing surfaces and a comment count. Beat E found
federal health money buying machine-assisted medicine. Only Beat B, research
and Indigenous AI, was genuinely quiet in window and said so.

That convergence is a trap as much as a gift. The data center land fight is
the story this deck has already told twice in thirty days, on 2026-08-06 (the
AIDEA and STAK land offerings) and 2026-08-19 (the polling on pausing new data
centers). Taking it a third time would be the same argument wearing a new
date.

## RANKED CANDIDATES

### 1. SELECTED — The FCC asks whether a machine should read Alaska's rural broadband paperwork

Beat D. Two FCC proposed rules, both published August 31st, 2026, the run date.

- "Maximizing Efficiencies in Universal Service Administration", WC Docket No.
  26-173, FCC 26-52, reported at 91 FR 55826. Ten separate sentences ask about
  artificial intelligence, among them whether AI tools to review applications,
  audits and appeal review processes would reduce delays, and whether the
  administrator should expand use of AI in document review for audits and
  program compliance. It asks. It does not propose.
- "Promoting Telehealth in Rural America", WC Docket No. 17-310, FCC 26-54,
  reported at 91 FR 55816. Names Alaska by number. "in Alaska, in funding year
  2024, only 32 of 317 approved Telecom Program requests relied on Methods 1
  or 2, while in funding year 2025, only 34 of 340 approved Telecom Program
  requests in Alaska relied on Methods 1 or 2."
- Both close September 30th, 2026, with reply comments October 30th.
- USAC 2025 operating expenses $266,603,608, and 3.06 percent of operating
  expenses plus disbursements. Board proposed to shrink from 20 to 13.

Why it wins on every criterion in order. Concrete Alaska impact, because the
Rural Health Care Telecom Program and E-Rate are how village clinics and
schools buy bandwidth. Visual potential, because 340 dots with 34 of them
different is a picture and the remaining 306 are the argument. Tangibility,
because there is a filing window a reader can still use. And an Alaskan would
send it to a coworker, specifically a tribal health IT director, a school
district technology director or a small carrier, all of whom file into this
system every year.

The editorial claim, and it is a real one rather than a summary. Alaska is the
place where the FCC's preferred method does not apply, by design, because
Alaska's geography is not standard. The paperwork that results is exactly the
paperwork the FCC has just asked whether a machine should read.

RISK, named now so the directors room designs around it rather than
discovering it. The AI here is a QUESTION inside a docket, not a deployment.
The deck must never imply the FCC has decided to use AI. The two dockets are
also SEPARATE proceedings, and the deck must never print the Alaska numbers as
though they came out of the AI docket. Both constraints are handed to the
fact-checker as explicit tests.

### 2. RUNNER-UP — Fairbanks asks the state for a data center moratorium

Beat A and Beat F, independently. Resolution No. 2026-25 before the Fairbanks
North Star Borough Assembly on August 27th, 2026, with the moratorium language
in BOTH the state and federal priority lists, pulled verbatim from the
borough's own CivicClerk agenda packet, file 13110. Public testimony, signs in
the chamber, and a motion to strike that got 4 votes to 3 and needed 5.

Strong, and not chosen, for two reasons that are both about honesty. Beat A
established that the resolution text says "commercial data centers" and never
says artificial intelligence, so the AI link is contextual rather than
textual. And neither scout could reach a primary record of the VOTE. The
borough's own system had published only the agenda and the packet; the outcome
rests on the Daily News-Miner and KUAC. A deck whose cover is a vote should be
able to cite the vote.

It also sits close to 2026-08-19's deck, which was about Alaskans wanting new
data centers paused. Same argument, twelve days apart.

### 3. RUNNER-UP — Meta's settlement, and Alaska's $16 million

Beat E. Multistate child-safety settlement announced August 26th, 2026, valued
by the attorneys general at up to $17.1 billion, with Alaska's share put at $16
million over ten years rising to as much as $23 million, announced August 28th
by acting Attorney General Cori Mills and Governor Dunleavy. The remedy that
matters here is an option for users under 18 to receive a feed that, in the
California Attorney General's words, "doesn't use an algorithm to target them
with content aimed to keep them endlessly scrolling."

Genuinely fresh and genuinely Alaskan in its dollar figure. Not chosen because
it is a national story with an Alaska line item, which is the weaker shape for
this page, and because Beat E honestly reported that no source uses the phrase
artificial intelligence. Held as the fallback if the FCC story fails the claims
gate.

### 5. NOT USED — the near misses, and why

- Executive Order 14421, signed August 26th, published at 91 FR 55995, declares
  a bulk-power-equipment national emergency and names data centers and AI as
  the reason. No Alaska mention anywhere in it. Alaska teeth are inferred, and
  the Beat E rule against inferred landings applies to Beat D findings too.
- CFTC request for comment on listing compute derivatives, RIN 3038-AF77,
  comments due October 20th. Sizes the compute service flow at $430 billion to
  $1.3 trillion a year. Does not mention Alaska. Real, interesting, and someone
  else's story until an Alaska filing exists.
- Chugach Electric's FERC preliminary permit for the 6.3 megawatt Canyon Creek
  project, docket P-15422-000, comments due October 20th. No AI content at all,
  and Beat A said so plainly. Kept as a scale reference, not as a story.
- AURORA-AI at UAF. Covered 2026-08-12. The August 27th broadcast adds real
  detail, a $325,000 UAF share, an October 1st start and a roughly $7 million
  phase two, but it is the same award nineteen days later.
- The RCA's 3 to 2 refusal to reconsider Enstar's $240 million Kenai Loop
  storage prudency, and Enstar's 18-day winter shortfall. Beat A was careful to
  say neither carries any data center or AI content. It is the fuel constraint
  the compute argument happens inside, and it is worth a deck of its own on a
  day when something in it moves.

  quotation in this run came through govinfo.

  the Fairbanks resolution's verbatim text from

  studio has found, and muni.org and anchorage.legistar.com remain closed.

- **cms.gov, sam.gov, govtribe, datacenterdynamics, nationalfisherman,

## NEW SOURCES TO CONSIDER, deduplicated

- Federal Register JSON API, keyless, dated, and the fastest way to prove a
  negative about federal activity in a window.
- NSF Award API filtered by awardeeStateCode=AK, keyless, returns full
  abstracts and exact dollar figures. It found an award nothing else surfaced.
- govinfo.gov FR full-text URLs, the replacement for federalregister.gov HTML.
- fnsb.api.civicclerk.com, primary borough agendas and packets as plain text.
- akml.org conference pages, which post Alaska municipal officials' actual
  slide decks as PDFs.
