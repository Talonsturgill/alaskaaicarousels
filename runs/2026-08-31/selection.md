## THE STORY

**The FCC asked, nine times in one notice, whether artificial intelligence
should help run the Universal Service Fund. In a separate notice published the
same day, in the same Federal Register issue, closing on the same date, it used
Alaska to show that roughly nine in ten Alaska rural health connectivity
requests fall off its preferred rate methods. Nothing in the record connects
the two.**

Two documents, both fetched and read in full, both quoted verbatim in
`claims.json`.

- **WC Docket No. 26-173**, "Maximizing Efficiencies in Universal Service
  Administration", FCC 26-52, 91 FR 55826 to 55835. Nine sentences name AI.
  One paragraph poses eight consecutive AI questions. C17 is the deck's
  central quote, "Would using artificial intelligence (AI) tools to review
  applications, audits, and appeal review processes help reduce delays while
  maintaining accurate results?"
- **WC Docket No. 17-310**, "Promoting Telehealth in Rural America", FCC 26-54,
  91 FR 55816 to 55825. "in Alaska, in funding year 2024, only 32 of 317
  approved Telecom Program requests relied on Methods 1 or 2, while in funding
  year 2025, only 34 of 340 approved Telecom Program requests in Alaska relied
  on Methods 1 or 2."
- Both adopted August 6th, released August 7th, published August 31st in issue
  167. Comments due **September 30th, 2026**, reply comments October 30th.

## WHY THIS ONE, against the criteria in order

**1. Strongest concrete Alaska impact.** The Telecom Program is one of the two
halves of the Rural Health Care Program (C15), it has existed since 1997 (C16),
and it pays the difference between what a rural Alaska health provider is
charged for connectivity and what a comparable urban customer in the same state
pays. 340 approved Alaska requests in one funding year is 340 real connections
to real clinics. The FCC reached for Alaska, by name, as its example of the
trend it is worried about (C13).

**2. Visual potential.** 340 against 34 is a picture before it is a statistic,
and the 306 that remain are the argument rather than the leftover. There is
also a genuine second geometry available, two documents published on the same
day with a line between them that the FCC did not draw.

**3. Tangibility.** There is a filing window open right now and it closes in
thirty days. Neither notice gives Alaska a separate deadline or a separate
route (C35), so an Alaska tribal health consortium, school district, library or
small carrier files on the same clock as a Virginia trade association.

**4. Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker?** A tribal health IT director, a
school district technology director and a rural carrier's regulatory staffer
all file into this system every year and none of them will hear about this from
either Alaska newsroom, because hearing about it requires reading the Federal
Register.

## THE ARGUMENT THE DECK MAKES, and it is ours

Alaska is the place where the standard method does not fit, by design, because
Alaska is not standard. The FCC's own hierarchy prefers Methods 1 and 2, which
use commercial rates, over Method 3, which uses cost studies (C14). In Alaska
nine in ten approved requests did not use the preferred pair, in FY2025 and in
FY2024 both (C11, C12). That is the most laborious paperwork in the system and
it is disproportionately Alaska's paperwork.

An automated reviewer is built for, and judged on, the typical case. Alaska is
definitionally the atypical case. That is the tension, and it is a fair one to
raise because the FCC itself asked about safeguards, data integrity and privacy
in the same breath (C21, C22).

## THE FOUR HONESTY CONSTRAINTS, handed to every downstream agent

These are in the fact-checker's kill list and they bind the storyboard, the
copy and every slide string.

1. **The FCC ASKS, it does not propose.** All nine AI sentences end in a
   question mark and the notice's own summary says the Commission seeks
   comment (C25). The deck may never say the FCC plans, proposes or has decided
   to use AI.
2. **The two notices are SEPARATE proceedings.** The AI notice never names
   Alaska (C26). The Alaska notice never says AI (C27). The connection is the
   deck's argument and the deck says so on a slide.
3. **306 of 340 "did not rely on Methods 1 or 2"** (C11). It does not license
   printing "Method 3" as the destination, and the deck never does.
4. **The Methods are not defined in either notice.** Methods 1 and 2 use
   commercial rates, Method 3 uses cost studies, and no further (C14). No
   invented definitions.

A fifth, from C23. The count is **nine** sentences naming AI, not ten. The
showrunner's brief said ten and the fact-checker corrected it by finding that
the tenth sentence in the run, "In what ways should it be used?", refers to AI
only by pronoun. The safer headline figure is C24, **eight consecutive
questions in one paragraph**, because every one of the eight is unambiguously
about AI.

## SYNTHESIS, the directors room

Three treatments, all complete, all usable. Recorded here because the winner is
a synthesis and the losing organs are the reason it is better than any one pitch.

**THE WINNER, and why. HISTORIAN-OF-THE-FUTURE, THE SPINE WALL AND THE UNSEWN
BLOCK.** Twenty nine bound volumes standing shoulder to shoulder, 1997 at the
left through 2025 at the right, then a slot where the 2026 volume is not bound
yet, with two unsewn blocks of loose leaves standing on a brass ledge.

It won on three counts.

1. **The best data slide in the room, by a distance.** THE FORE EDGE. A book's
   fore edge IS a stack of leaves, so funding year 2025 presents 340 approved
   requests as 340 fine parallel lines, 34 of them gilt, and funding year 2024
   sits below it at THE SAME PIXELS PER LEAF, so its band is honestly narrower
   because 317 is honestly fewer. The director's own sentence is the argument
   for it. It is not a chart standing in for an object, it is the object being
   the chart. That is the Reuters standard in DESIGN_DOCTRINE section 6 met
   literally.
2. **The best continuity device in the room, and it is made of this run's own
   honesty constraint.** THE ACCESSION RULE, a brass strip panning across the
   bottom band, nine ticks, every one a date with a claim-id behind it, and
   TICKS AFTER THE CURRENT SLIDE ARE NEVER DRAWN. The rule runs on bare past
   the present with a cut terminal. The deck is forbidden to write the future,
   because the FCC asked rather than proposed, and that prohibition is now
   visible in the artwork on all nine frames rather than asserted in a caption.
3. **It is the most feasible of the three.** Bound volumes are cabinet
   extrusion plus engraving, both proven benches. The fore edge is parallel
   hairlines, which is cheap and exact.

**ORGANS TRANSPLANTED FROM THE EDITORIAL-ESSAYIST.** Its central idea was that
the government's words should be dimensional and the deck's own voice flat, so
a reader can tell at 432px which sentences are the record's and which are the
argument's. That is the right instinct and the wrong implementation, because
extruding a serif nine times is, in its own director's words, one degree from
cliche. The winner already had the cheap version of the same idea, so it is
adopted and hardened. **Instrument Serif ITALIC is the FCC talking and nothing
else ever.** Roman is the deck. Every argument string additionally carries a
mono ALASKA.AI READING stamp, which is the essayist's slide 06 tag. Its slide
07, nobody at the FCC joined these two, survives as this deck's S7.

**ORGANS TRANSPLANTED FROM THE FIELD-DOCUMENTARIAN.** Two.

Its solution to the no-named-place problem was the best single idea in the
room, and it generalises. On a trades drawing, a detail marked TYP. is a
professionally honest way to draw a thing you are not identifying. The winner
faces the same risk in a different costume, because no such shelf of volumes
exists, so the same discipline is imported. **Every object in this deck is
stamped with a real docket number, page range or date in mono**, so the blocks
read as documents rather than as books, and no on-slide string ever claims the
shelf exists.

Its monotony mitigation is also imported wholesale. A single continuous scene
at nine stations can produce three frames that are indistinguishable at 432px,
so the register changes are structural rather than cosmetic and are written
into the dossiers as requirements.

Its rack of 340 hanging tags was the runner-up data slide and loses only
because the fore edge does the same job with less machinery.

**WHAT WAS REJECTED FROM THE WINNER.** Its cover, `Comments close September
30th. Nine AI questions. No Alaska.` The phrase No Alaska rests on C26, an
absence claim at 0.82 confidence whose own note warns against a slide that
depends on the absence being exhaustive, and it was the loudest string in the
deck. The director offered the swap itself and the swap is taken.

**THE COVER, decided.** `ONE NOTICE ASKS ABOUT AI. THE OTHER COUNTS ALASKA.
SAME DAY.` Twelve words. It carries both halves of the story, which matters
because the studio's own instinct ledger records a caption that cleared every
mechanical gate while containing no AI at all on an Alaska plus AI page. It
rests on C01 through C05, the highest confidence claims in the package, and the
mono stamp beneath it bounds the assertion to two named documents.

**ENGINE SPIKE, run before any dossier was written.** aksnow, akpost, aktype
and Bricolage were exercised in one throwaway frame to prove the stack in this
container before the deck depended on it. It found a real trap that TWO of the
three treatments would have hit. `AKPOST.grade` takes `bloom` and `grain` as
OBJECTS and the keys are `contrast` and `sharpen`, not `logContrast`, `pivot`
or `unsharp`; a number throws a render hard fail. The corrected spike renders
