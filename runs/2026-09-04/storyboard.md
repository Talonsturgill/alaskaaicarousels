## THE HERO STRUCTURE

### `NEW: THE RULED WORLD AND THE UNRULED ONE`

**The hero is not an object. It is a relationship, and it is enforced as a
vocabulary rule on every slide.**

> **THE GROUND CARRIES NO STROKES ANYWHERE IN THIS DECK. It is rendered
> entirely in modelled tone. EVERY STRAIGHT LINE IN NINE SLIDES BELONGS TO THE
> DOCUMENT.**

The land is drawn. The instrument is ruled. A reader can tell them apart from
across a room, at 432 pixels, with no words at all.

That one rule does four jobs simultaneously, which is why it beat three
perfectly good object heroes.

1. **It makes the argument.** The deck is about a legal category being laid
   over real country. The picture is literally that.
2. **It attacks the standing weakness at the root.** A ground with no strokes
   available to it can only be built out of modelled tone, so the middle
   distance has to be constructed rather than outlined. The recurring flat mid
   band becomes impossible rather than discouraged.
3. **It makes the parcel boundary undrawable.** A boundary needs a closed
   polygon and the ground has no strokes to make one from. The claims give area
   and townships, never a shape, and now the deck physically can't assert one.
4. **It makes a data centre undrawable.** The vocabulary contains no enclosure,
   no wall, no roof, no rack and no cabinet, following run No.40's method of
   making the worst available error impossible rather than merely avoided.

**The ground.** Boreal lowland of the kind that lies two miles north of Houston
in the Mat-Su (C06). Black spruce standing thin and spired on saturated
muskeg, many leaning where the peat gives. Kettle hollows, rounded closed
pockets with no outlet stream, which is the actual glacial geology and reads
instantly as different from a stream valley. Tussock sedge. Birch and aspen on
the better drained rises, going yellow in the first week of September. Ice fog
at ankle height in the hollows only.

**PROVENANCE, and it is printed on the deck.** Slides 01 and 08 carry a small
mono line, `GROUND DRAWN FROM THE NOTICE'S TOWNSHIP DESCRIPTION. NOT A SURVEY.`
The ground is representative boreal lowland, not a survey of the parcel, and
the deck says so rather than letting a reader assume. This is the historian
director's own recommended repair, taken.

**Divergence, structure by structure, named.**

- Not No.46's **spine wall of twenty nine bound volumes on a brass ledge**.
  Nothing stands vertically, nothing is a repeated manufactured object, there
  is no paper anywhere in the deck and no drawn document. The instrument is
  present only as ruled lines, never as a depicted sheet.
- Not No.47's **count line of survey markers on muskeg at eye height**. This is
  the nearest neighbour and the divergence is deliberate and total. The camera
  climbs from 1.5 m to 620 m across the deck instead of sitting at 1.6 m. There
  is no marker in the vocabulary, no object standing on the ground in a row,
  and no count of anything physical. Its whole read was cast shadows from a
  directional key on standing objects; this deck's read is a ruled overlay
  against modelled ground.
- Not No.48's **macro oblique section into cut ballot sheet edges**. Every
  frame is a landscape at landscape distance. There is no macro crop, no stack

- Not No.49's **wet island night apron with mass in the lower half**. Dry cold,

## ATMOSPHERE

### `NEW: LOW RAKING LIGHT ON FROZEN MUSKEG, FIRST WEEK OF SEPTEMBER`

- **Key** azimuth 318 degrees, elevation 9 degrees. The sun is below the tree
  line and the light arrives through gaps.
- **Key to fill 6 to 1.** Fill is skylight, so it is blue, and the key is a
  pale cold amber. Warm light and cool shadow arrives numerically through
  `AKC.ramp` hue drift rather than by hand.
- **Why elevation 9.** This is a craft decision, not a mood. `AKENGRAVE` sets
  stroke width from the light, and a low elevation spreads `ndotl` across
  nearly its full range so the swelled line actually swells instead of
  flattening into a uniform hatch. It is also what gives every object a long
  cast shadow to declare a contact against, which is the craft lever the field
  documentarian's diffuse sky dome would have given up.
- **In the air.** Ice fog at ankle height, under 40 cm, sitting in the hollows
  between tussocks and nowhere else, drawn with a modelled upper surface and
  never as a wash, so it separates the near band from the middle band without
  softening the frame. Above it the air is clear and cold, which is why the far
  tree line loses tonal range and keeps its edge. Fog is exp2 in the sky's own
  hue, never grey.
- **Divergence.** Not No.47's first hour under a high cloud lid, because there
  is no lid and no diffuse overhead source and the key is directional at nine

  and natural and every surface is organic. Not No.49's rain at a 17 degree

### THE GOLD RULE, and it is an inversion

## THE STANDING WEAKNESS, and how this deck attacks it

`trend_check --window 10` names artwork craft and genuine detail as weakest in
8 of 10 runs, mean 6.7. The diagnosis is the MIDDLE DISTANCE, the band between
a well drawn anchor and an atmospheric far field that ends up one flat value.

Three answers, all structural rather than exhortative.

1. **THE GROUND CARRIES NO STROKES.** An outline is the cheap way to suggest a
   mid ground. Removing outlines from the ground removes the cheap way, so the
   middle distance has to be modelled.
2. **THE ORDER RULE, MADE STRUCTURAL.** `AKPARCEL.band()` takes the caller's
   own `draw` function, runs it at FULL CONTRAST into an offscreen canvas, and
   only then applies per channel extinction toward the far value and composites
   once. A slide physically can't apply the attenuation first through this
   entry point. Attenuation costs a surface its tonal RANGE and never its
   INCIDENT DETAIL, and doing it backwards is the flat zone itself.
3. **THREE SCALES DECLARED PER REGION**, in every dossier's field 7. What
   carries the silhouette at metres, the surface at centimetres, the tooth at
   millimetres. A region that can name only two is unfinished and is redrawn
   before it renders, not after a critic says it reads flat.

Deferred and named so the deferral is not silent. Top loaded composition, the
number one recurring machine warn, was worked three runs ago by No.49 and is
now under instrumentation by `value_structure`. It is held rather than pushed,
so this run's effort goes to the criterion that has been weakest 8 times in 10.

## ENGINE LESSONS BANKED BEFORE ANY SLIDE WAS WRITTEN

Three probe renders were run against the real engine during Phase 5, which is
cheaper than a pixel critic round.

1. `AKPOST.grade` enforces its option SHAPES and THROWS. `grain` is
   `{amount, size, seed}` and not a number. The probe threw on `grain: 0.05`
   and the finish was silently absent until the console was read.
2. `AKENGRAVE.surface` collapses onto one iso-line when `form` is SEPARABLE,
   even when it varies in both axes. A `sin(x) + cos(y)` form printed as a
   single knotted ribbon. Every `form` in this deck is a genuine two axis field
   and every surface passes `seedDeg` to turn the seeding raster alone, and the
   `LAY_ALIGN` console warning is read on every engraved slide.
3. `AKGeo.zoomTo` did not zoom as called, so the deck does not use it. Slide
   08's grid is drawn directly, and the cartography probe confirmed the state
   outline, all 29 boroughs, the Matanuska-Susitna highlight and Houston's
   projected position all render correctly through an explicit `fitExtent`.
