# Selection, 2026-10-29 (No.99)

## The pick
THE COUNTING LINE. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's final Beaufort Sea and North Slope incidental take regulation (91 FR 66204, October 19th, effective through October 19th, 2031) authorizes five years of incidental harassment of polar bears and Pacific walruses by North Slope oil and gas. The number of disturbed dens in it comes from a computer simulation, 10,000 runs of simulated dens, and between the March draft and the October final FWS found that one line of its code had been counting only dens "specifically marked undisturbed", so dens with a missing value were never counted. Fixed, the model's five-year count of harassed denning cubs went from 4 to 13 and of denning bears from 6 to 22. In the same rule FWS says it gives that model no more weight than industry's own estimate (123 bears, no other take), and it dropped a proposed third aerial infrared den survey as not practicable. The first required den survey window opens November 25th.

Why it wins (criteria in order):
1. Concrete Alaska impact: every North Slope operator (ConocoPhillips, Hilcorp, Santos, Repsol, ExxonMobil and the rest of AOGA) works under it for five years; it sets the winter den surveys that start in four weeks; it bears on a threatened Southern Beaufort population and on subsistence.
2. Visual potential: geometry (the Beaufort coast and the den buffer), quantity (10,000 simulated winters, 4 to 13, 6 to 22, 123 versus 432), place (the North Slope in polar night), and a literal object (one line of code) the art can make the hero.
3. Tangibility: a published rule with dates, a quoted code fix, survey windows.
4. Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker: yes, to anyone who works the Slope, regulates it or writes about it, because the rule itself documents a software defect in a permit's arithmetic and says plainly how much the agency trusts the model.

Brand fit, stated honestly: this is a computation-governs-a-decision story, not an AI story. The deck says "model" and "code" and never calls the simulation AI. That is the right register for this publication's audience, the same governance question the AI beat keeps asking (who audits the model, and how much weight does it get), on the one decision this week where a model's code is on the record.
