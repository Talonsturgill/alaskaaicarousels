# STORYBOARD, No.99, 2026-10-29, THE NIGHT CRANE

## THESIS
The polar bear rule that North Slope oil and gas works under until October 19th, 2031 takes its den numbers from a computer simulation, and between the March draft and the October final the Fish and Wildlife Service found that one line of that simulation's code counted only dens "specifically marked undisturbed", so dens left with a missing value were never counted. Fixed, the model's five-year count of harassed denning bears went from 6 to 22 and of denning cubs from 4 to 13. In the same rule FWS says the corrected model gets no more weight than industry's own estimate of 123 bears. Position: when one line of code moves a permit's count, the code is part of the rule, and the fair question is how much a checked, published model should count, not whether the bears are fine.

Document title (copy.json `document_title`, under 60 characters): `One Line of Code in a Polar Bear Rule`

- The model is a computer simulation (C21). Nothing in the deck calls it AI or machine learning.

- Level A is potential injury of cubs and Level B is disturbance; "take" here is incidental harassment, and the deck never writes "killed". Lethal take appears only as the slight decrease (C24).
- Industry's 123 (C37) and the model's figures are both printed; FWS's own concession that the model runs high (C41) and AOGA's criticism (C45, C46) are attributed as the rule gives them.
- The den positions, drift, cutbank and aircraft view are ILLUSTRATIVE; every frame that draws a den says so in its slate or a micro tag. The bear is never drawn.
