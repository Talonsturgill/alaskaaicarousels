## THE BOARD, after the dedupe gate

### SELECTED

**NSF opens $100 million for state and regional AI infrastructure hubs, and
caps every state at one award.** (Beat D)

NSF announced the State and Regional Artificial Intelligence Infrastructure
Hubs program on August 4th, 2026; the solicitation, NSF 26-513, posted July
31st. About $100 million, up to 10 hubs per cycle, $4 million to $12 million
each over 5 years. Three rules decide the shape of it. Only one award per state
or multi-state region will be made. An institution may appear on only one
proposal, and if it appears on more only the first is reviewed. Voluntary cost
sharing is prohibited. Full proposals are due November 4th, 2026. NSF's own
stated reason for the program is that "access to AI infrastructure remains
highly uneven across the nation."

Primary sources, both fetched and read in full by the showrunner:

- https://www.nsf.gov/news/new-nsf-state-regional-ai-infrastructure-hubs-will-power-ai

## WHY THE SELECTED STORY IS THE RIGHT ONE

It is the only in-window item whose subject IS artificial intelligence
infrastructure, whose numbers all come from primary federal documents, and
whose Alaska consequence is still open. Every other strong candidate was either
already shipped inside 30 days, or AI-adjacent rather than AI, or unverifiable
today with the search budget gone.

It also carries an argument this page is built to make. NSF's stated problem is
that access is uneven. Its remedy hands out one award per state. A state with
one research university and a state with thirty are capped identically, and the
solicitation forbids anyone from adding their own money to change that. That is
a structural claim, it is provable from the solicitation text alone, and it has
a date attached that has not passed.
