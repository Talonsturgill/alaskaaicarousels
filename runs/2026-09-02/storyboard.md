# STORYBOARD — Carousel No. 48 — 2026-09-02

# DECK HEADER

## Thesis

Fairbanks built a test for its ballot machines, wrote down in advance what
failure would look like, ran it, and the machines came back clean. On October
6th the borough votes on prohibiting them anyway, with no fiscal analysis in the
ordinance and a two year lock on the answer.

## Deck title (PDF document title, 46 chars)

`Counted Twice. Fairbanks Votes on the Machines.`

## The directors room, and what was taken from where

Three lenses ran in parallel. **The field documentarian's THE MEASURED SECTION
wins the hero**, strengthened by organs from the other two.

The systems illustrator's sectioned tabulator was the most inventive pitch in
the room and it is not built, for the reason its own author raised first and
loudest. No source describes the internal geometry of the borough's machines, so
that hero would have required inventing engineering, inside a deck whose entire
argument is about what a record does and does not establish. A `SCHEMATIC
SECTION` stamp does not repair that; it advertises it. The lens still supplied
three of the deck's best devices, all of which draw an ABSENCE honestly rather
than asserting a number.

Taken from the **field documentarian** (the winner): the medium and the camera,
the riffle as a type reserve made of MORE paper rather than less, the deck's
sharpest line ("the tolerance was 33 ballots wide, the gap was zero"), the
standing honesty fixture `DRAWN TO SCALE. NOT DRAWN TO COUNT.`, the drafting
break line for the thirty unsampled precincts, and the Space Grotesk and
JetBrains Mono pairing with no serif anywhere.

Taken from the **editorial essayist**: the registration pair, two independently
produced marks landing on the same sheet edge with zero separation, which is the
deck's wordless claim and the single best answer to the counting-error trap; the
concession made BEFORE the counter speaks; and the two year lock drawn at the
same day scale as the election calendar so it visibly runs off the frame.

Taken from the **systems illustrator**: the threshold the light never touches
(the 1 percent trip mark is the one mark in the deck the key never finds); the
unstruck field on the spec plate for the missing cost estimate, with the hard
rule that no number, bar or arrow goes near it; and the declared refusal to
encode turnout (C30) in artwork at all, because it is a range in different units
from everything else and encoding it would invite a misread.

## Arc, and the emotional temperature

1. **HOOK** the whole edge, the fact stated flat
2. **CONTEXT** what is actually on the ballot, in the clerk's own words
3. **POINT** the test the borough built for itself, with its scale attached
4. **HERO / PEAK** the tolerance and the gap. The argument in one relation
5. **POINT** the proponent's case, at full strength, larger than anything else
6. **BREATHER** what was not sampled. The concession, declared
7. **POINT** what a yes actually decides, and the number nobody printed
8. **DATA / KEEPABLE** the register a Fairbanks voter screenshots
9. **CLOSE** one ask

Temperature rises through 4, turns at 5 where the deck hands the microphone to
the other side, rests at 6, and goes cold and procedural through 7 and 8 because
that is what the material is.

## Slide count rationale

Nine. The house default band is 8 to 10. The argument has four load-bearing
beats (the test, the result, the counter, the lock) and each needs its own
frame, plus a cover, a payoff, a declared breather, a keepable register and a
close. Eight would force the counter and the concession into one slide, which
would read as rebutting the proponent in the same breath as quoting them. Ten
would add a slide with nothing new to verify.

## Continuity system, with full motif state tables

Three devices. Two are load-bearing.

### DEVICE A — THE RIFFLE LANE (type reserve, motif and edge-tease)

The type reserve is a splayed fan in the mass, the shape a stack makes when a
thumb runs down it. Inside the lane the sheets separate, the fan opens toward
the key, the interleaved gaps catch light and the local spatial frequency drops
by about 4x. It is MORE paper, not less, which is the whole point: the reserve
is generated by the same lay function as the rest of the mass with a splay
multiplier, so it can never become a plate laid on top. It is also the swipe
cue, cut by the right edge of slide n and continuing on n+1 at the same y.

## Variety ledger check, and this deck's REQUIRED divergence

Stated against the four most recent decks, one by one.

**Hero structure.** THE MEASURED SECTION, a macro oblique three quarter view of
a continuous mass of cut ballot sheet edges filling the frame corner to corner,
with no table, no room, no ground plane and no horizon.
- vs No.44's underlit interior bench in plan view keyed from beneath. This is an
  oblique elevation at macro range with a key from camera left at 22 degrees. No
  plan view, no bench, no interior architecture, no light from below anywhere.
- vs No.45's cut plate of cotton rag on a tabletop with borough shapes on
  extrusions. No tabletop, no plate, no single sheet, no borough silhouette, no
  extrusion. That deck looked at one sheet lying flat from above at 1:1. This
  looks at thousands standing on edge, obliquely, at roughly 20x, and the paper
  is a mass rather than a surface.
- vs No.46's spine wall of bound volumes seen straight on. The nearest
  neighbour, so the divergence is enforced as a build rule. Volumes are
  discrete, countable objects with gaps and titles seen frontally; this medium
  is unbound, compressible and continuously varying with no object boundaries,
  seen obliquely with a real z recession, a blurred foreground plane bleeding
  off frame and a focal plane. **HARD RULE FOR THE BUILDER, no vertical register
  of evenly spaced like-sized units anywhere in the deck. The mass leans,
  compresses and shears. A passage that reads as identical spines is a rebuild.**
- vs No.47's count line of galvanized survey markers on muskeg at eye height
  with solved cast shadows. No ground, no horizon, no eye height, no discrete
  standing objects, and no cast shadows onto a ground plane anywhere except one
  declared object on slide 07.

**Atmosphere.** NINE P.M., ONE TUBE ACROSS THE EDGE. Not No.45's north window at
ten a.m. high key on paper white, because there is no daylight in this deck at
all, the key sits at 22 degrees rather than a window's 55 plus, and the paper
reads mid grey and never reaches white except on specular crests. Not No.46's
closing-time rake from a single hard lamp with dust in the beam, because the
source is LINEAR rather than a point, which gives a completely different shadow
signature, there is a genuine 5 to 1 fill rather than a single-lamp ratio, and
there is no beam, no shaft and no motes. Not No.47's first hour under a high
cloud lid contre-jour with a hot rim, because the key is side-raking from camera
left IN FRONT of the subject, with no backlight, no rim, no sky and no lid.

**Continuity devices.** Not No.46's brass accession rule panning one window per
swipe with an unbuilt 2026 slot, and not No.47's fixed station-line camera with
a doubled station changing shape. Device A is a physical property of the medium
that also holds the type. Device B is a mark defined by the light that never
reaches it.

**Hook archetype.** NEW: THE EXHIBIT THAT CHANGED NO VOTES. Not No.45's address,
not No.46's pair nobody joined, and above all not No.47's COUNTING ERROR. See
the guard below, which is structural rather than asserted.

**Palette family.** BALLOT EDGE UNDER A FLUORESCENT TUBE, WITH ONE SIGNAL RED.
Not buckram, brass and unsewn paper (No.46), because there is no brass, no cloth
and the light is green cast rather than warm. Not muskeg, galvanized steel and
one aluminium tag (No.47), because there is no wetland, no zinc and no landscape.
Not diffuser white on darkroom petrol (No.44), because that was a high key deck
and nothing here reaches white. This deck's fingerprint is a **cool green key
against a cool blue violet shadow**, which inverts the house warm-light
cool-shadow default and appears nowhere in the last four.

**Type pairing.** Space Grotesk + JetBrains Mono. **No serif appears anywhere in
this deck**, which is itself the divergence, since both forbidden pairings led
with a serif display (Instrument Serif + Manrope + JetBrains Mono, and Archivo +
Fraunces + JetBrains Mono).

## THE COUNTING-ERROR GUARD (structural, not asserted)

No.47's hook archetype was a countable population drawn so the headline count
and the picture disagree. This deck's drama is the opposite, it is AGREEMENT,
and three build rules make the wrong read unavailable rather than merely
unintended.

1. **The sheet edges are a MEDIUM, not an ISOTYPE population.** No number
   anywhere in the deck maps to a count of drawn sheets, and sheet pitch is set
   by the camera stage, never by a quantity.
2. **A standing mono fixture on every quantitative slide** reads
   `DRAWN TO SCALE. NOT DRAWN TO COUNT.`
3. **All quantities live in parallel projection with a printed scale**, on
   slides 03, 04 and 08 only, and never in the hero's perspective mass. The
   hand count and the machine count are drawn as ONE COINCIDENT EDGE, not two
   edges near each other, so there is no discrepancy in the artwork to misread.

## Variance dials, chosen deliberately

- **design_variance 4.** No.47 ran 3. Artwork craft has been the weakest
  criterion in nine of ten runs and the answer is not another cautious deck.
- **visual_density 4.** Deliberately high. The run's whole structural bet is
  that a continuous medium makes emptiness unavailable, and that only works if
  the density is genuinely carried.
- **type_temperature 2.** Cool and technical, which buys room for a dense field
  and is what a serif-free pairing is for.

## Line voice

Drafting. Butt caps, miter joins, no wobble. Five weight tokens assigned by
MEANING, per akengrave's token system: hair 0.75 for fibre and hatching, fine
1.25 for leaders and dimensions, standard 2.0 for object strokes, bold 3.5
reserved for edges the cut plane passes through, hero 5.5 for the riffle's
opening edge.

**Hard bans for this deck.** Zero rounded rectangles. Zero icons. Dense mark
fields are NEVER labelled in place; balloon numbers on the art, numbered key set
in the riffle. No gradient inside a fillRect anywhere.

## Render approach, and the flat argument stated deliberately

The paper is drawn with **akengrave** (white-line intaglio, light-driven stroke
width) over an **akrelief** substrate whose `height` is the stack's own lay
function, finished with **akpost**. This is a deliberate choice against a GPU PBR
hero and the dossier argues it rather than defaulting to it: a shadow-mapped
stack of several thousand thin boxes at macro range is precisely where shadow
acne and moire live, and a raking-key engraved surface whose per-stroke width is
set by the light is the higher-craft answer for THIS subject. The deck still
touches the top rung where a real solid is genuinely present, slide 07's slate
lock bar, which is **akthree** GPU PBR with a designed AK3D cabinet-extrusion
fallback for `AKT.webglOK() === false`.

**Engine findings from this run's own spikes, which are build requirements.**
- `AK.reliefShade` CANNOT BE CLIPPED. It writes with `putImageData`, which
  ignores the canvas clip path, silently. Use its `mask(u, v)` option for the
  relief and clip only the ordinary draws, to the same edge function.
- Sheet pitch must not fall below about 6 CSS px or the medium stops reading as
  paper at 432 px and becomes a texture frequency.
- Density must vary across x (an fbm at about 3 cycles) or the field reads as
  uniform weave, which is wallpaper and fails the anchor rule.
- Per-sheet REACH, giving each sheet its own extent, is what makes a silhouette
  ragged. A straight vertical edge reads as a slab.
- A reserve made of a second material must be lit enough to SURVIVE the grade.
- `cx.filter` applies PER DRAW OP. All defocus is drawn into an offscreen and
  blurred ONCE at composite. Never set `cx.filter` inside a stroke loop.
- Load order is noise, akcolor, aksnow, akengrave. akcolor before akengrave or
  it throws.

## ROUND 6, THE PANORAMA SEAM

The scorer's one-sentence fix, and the only change available that lifts three
weighted criteria at once. The artwork ledger has declared `panorama` as this
deck's primary continuity device since slide 01 was written, and both the flow
room and the scorer measured the same thing in the pixels: it was not there.
No two adjacent frames shared a continuing edge, no right edge carried anything
cut, and four frames (02, 05, 08, 09) were a large light rectangle with type on
it and a ragged edge as their only compositional event.

A panorama cannot be an intention. It has to be a FUNCTION OF GLOBAL X, and the
seam has to be true by construction, because nobody is going to butt nine 1080
px frames together and check. So `seamY(globalX)` is one continuous curve across
all 9720 px of the deck, with `globalX = (n - 1) * 1080 + x`. Every slide draws
the same curve over its own window, which makes the lane's height and slope at
x = 1080 on slide n identical to its height and slope at x = 0 on slide n+1, at
every one of the eight seams, without any slide knowing anything about its
neighbours.

The lane is not a stripe laid over the art. It is the tube's brightest pass
across the paper, drawn in two passes. The second lights the slide's own sheet
arrises inside the band. The first, which is what makes the seam true, carries
nine arrises of its own whose path is a pure function of globalX and which run
the full width and off both edges: relying on the slide's real sheets alone left
four of the eight seams with no lit band at the frame edge at all, because a
sheet only reaches x = 1080 when its reach noise happens to send it there.

MEASURED OFF THE SHIPPED PNGS, not asserted. For each seam, the expected lane
height at globalX = n * 1080 was read out of the browser, then the luminance
profile of the last 30 px of slide n and the first 30 px of slide n+1 were
searched for a peak near it. Before: 3 of 8 seams carried a matching lit band.
After: 8 of 8, with peak offsets of 4, 5, 15, 12, 20, 2, 11 and 7 design px.

The lane keeps its own reserve test rather than borrowing each slide's `inRes`,
because those have three different signatures and pads across the nine files and
the lane's strokes run up to 3.1 px wide.
