# SELECTION — 2026-09-02 — Carousel No. 48

## THE STORY

**Fairbanks North Star Borough Proposition 3, on the October 6th, 2026 ballot.**
A citizens' initiative that would require every borough ballot to be counted by
hand, by registered borough voters, and would prohibit electronic tabulation
systems outright.

The reason it is a deck and not a news item is what the borough already did. In
2025 the FNSB ran a hand-count audit of its own election, checking a sample of
ballots against what the machines reported, and the two agreed completely. The
borough tested its machines, the machines passed, and the question on the ballot
six weeks later is whether to ban them anyway. The initiative carries no fiscal
analysis, by the borough's own printed admission, and Alaska law locks a passed
initiative for two years against the Assembly and the voters both.

## WHY THIS ONE, AGAINST THE CRITERIA

1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** A binding vote in six weeks in Alaska's second
   borough, with a registration deadline four days out from the run date and
   early voting opening September 21st. A reader can still act on every one of
   those dates.
2. **Visual potential.** The story's core is a comparison of two counts that
   agreed. That is a countable, drawable population and a genuinely unusual
   argument to have to draw, because the interesting thing is SAMENESS. Paper
   is also the story's physical material, and a stack of ballots seen edge on is
   a continuous striated medium, which is exactly the hero this run committed to
   in plan.md.
3. **Tangibility.** Ballots, a room, people counting, a date.
4. **Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker?** Yes, and to an argument. This is
   a live October ballot question with a real evidentiary record on one side and
   a real distrust on the other, and both are legible.

## IS IT AN AI STORY? SAID PLAINLY

No, and the deck will not pretend otherwise. Nothing in Proposition 3 concerns
artificial intelligence, and no slide will say it does. It concerns optical
scanners, which are old, dumb, single-purpose machines.

It runs here because it is the same question one layer down, and because
Fairbanks is asking it out loud right now. The question is what evidence would
persuade a community to let a machine do a public job, and what happens when the
evidence arrives and the answer is still no. This page spends most of its runs
on the front edge of that question, where the machines are new and the record is
thin. Here the machines are boring and the record is complete, and the vote is
still close enough to hold an election over. That is worth an Alaskan's
attention precisely because the harder version is arriving behind it.

The deck states the audit's limits honestly and does not use the word AI as a
lever.

## HONESTY FIREWALL

Three things this deck must not do, written down before the art is planned:

1. Do not call optical scanners artificial intelligence, or imply the initiative
   is about AI. It is not.
2. Do not present the 2025 audit as more than it was. It was a SAMPLE, not a
   full recount, and the deck prints what share of the ballots it covered.
3. Do not assert a cost for hand counting. The borough itself printed that the
   ordinance carries no fiscal analysis, and the absence is the finding. An
   invented number would be the one thing that discredits the argument.
