# SELECTION — 2026-07-29 — Carousel No. 19

## THE STORY

**Shinkei Systems has put a computer-vision harvesting robot on Cook Inlet
salmon boats during the 2026 sockeye season.** The machine, PSDN-S, scans a
fish, spikes its brain, cuts the gills and drops it into slush ice in 6.5
seconds or less, automating the Japanese ike jime technique. The company
announced it on 2026-07-23 as its newest harvesting robot, built to suit the
smaller boats used throughout Alaska's salmon fisheries (C04, C05). IntraFish
published the rollout on 2026-07-22 under a photo captioned from Alaska's Cook
Inlet (C01, C03).

## WHY THIS ONE

**(1) Concrete Alaska impact.** Sockeye is the most economically important
species in Upper Cook Inlet by the state's own account (C24), a fishery with
more than 1,300 drift and set gillnet permits, about ten percent of every
salmon permit in Alaska (C23). A machine that changes what a fish is worth on
the deck changes the arithmetic of that fishery.

**(2) Visual potential.** The story's true subject is TIME, and time is the
easiest quantity in the world to draw honestly. Rigor mortis at about 7 hours
becomes up to 60 hours (C14). Shelf life triples (C21). The cycle is 6.5
seconds (C10). Those three clocks, at wildly different scales, are the deck.

**(3) Tangibility.** A refrigerator-sized robot (C09) bolted to a working boat
deck in July, doing what the company's own chief executive calls the
equivalent of surgery in a very difficult maritime environment (C30).

**(4) Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** In a fishing state at the
peak of the season, yes. This is the first time AI has shown up in an Alaska
fishery as a HARVEST tool rather than a counting or monitoring tool.

## THE THESIS, AS THE FACT-CHECK FORCED IT

The brief went in with a sharper and less true argument, that Alaskans sell
into a commodity market while somebody else takes the value. The fact-checker
killed the simple version and it was right to. Two verified facts contradict
it. Khawaja says Shinkei owns the machines and puts them on the boats for free
(C06), and he says the company splits proceeds variably with the fishermen it
works with (C07), with trade coverage of the Alaska rollout describing catch
bought at premium prices (C29).

So the honest argument is better and stranger than the grievance version:

**The machine does not make a better fish. It makes TIME. And every asset
built out of that time is held outside Alaska.**

The fish is caught by an Alaskan, in Alaska water, on an Alaskan's boat, and
the seven-to-sixty-hour extension is created right there on the deck. But the
brand that time becomes (Seremoni Grade) is Shinkei's, by Khawaja's own words
(C08). The plant that time is processed in is 16,000 square feet in Tacoma,
Washington, bought from FATHOM Seafood (C17). The system that certifies how
many days a fish can still be sold is NERA, running on Shinkei's proprietary
datasets (C20). The company is in El Segundo, California (C21). Alaska
supplies the fish, the water and the labor. Somebody else owns the clock.

Closing question the deck earns: **when the value of an Alaska fish moves from
the fish to the clock, who should own the clock?**

## HONESTY SPINE (binding on every slide)

- Every performance number in this deck comes from Shinkei. Say so on the
  slide that carries them. 3x shelf life, 60 hours, 6.5 seconds, 70 percent,
  all vendor figures (C10, C13, C14, C21).
- No count of Cook Inlet boats exists on any fetched page. The deck says a
  handful and never a number. The verified eight-boat figure is December 2025
  and is not Alaska (C16).
- No price spread may be drawn. There is no verified 2026 Cook Inlet ex-vessel
  price and no verified Seremoni retail price. The one Cook Inlet number that
  survived is a Deckboss estimate of about 1.73 dollars a pound in 2025 (C27),
  and it must be dated and hedged on the slide. The Bristol Bay 1.60 base
  price (C28) may NEVER stand in for Cook Inlet.
- The retail product is not on shelves. IntraFish says Shinkei EXPECTS to roll
  it out this summer (C02), and as of 2026-07-29 Seremoni's own species list
  carried no salmon at all. That gap is a slide, not an embarrassment.
- The in-window sourcing is one paywalled trade headline, one company LinkedIn
  post and two retellings. The deck discloses that.

# DIRECTORS ROOM SYNTHESIS

(appended after the room reports)

The room ran three lenses in parallel: FIELD DOCUMENTARIAN, SYSTEMS
ILLUSTRATOR, DATA JOURNALIST. All three independently arrived at the same
structural insight, that the deck's real dataset is TIME and its real
weakness is that almost every number is a vendor number. That convergence is
itself a signal the thesis is sound. They diverged on the chassis, and the
winner is a synthesis rather than any one pitch.

## WINNER, and what it took from whom

**The hero is the SYSTEMS ILLUSTRATOR's central idea, THE HOUR COLUMN.** A
vertical column standing on a wet boat deck under one orthographic camera,
built so that one world unit is one hour at a printed, constant pixels-per-
hour. The bottom 7 hours render as BRUSHED STEEL, the natural window a fish
has always had. The 53 hours above it render as POLISHED BRASS, the
manufactured window. The material changes at the 7 hour line. That single
decision states the entire thesis with zero words and survives the 432px
thumb, and orthographic projection is the only doctrinally honest way to draw
a quantity in 3D. Nothing else in the room came close to that economy.

**The honesty spine is the DATA JOURNALIST's PROVENANCE CHIP system.** Three
chip forms carry the source class of every figure in the deck, dashed outline
for VENDOR, solid fill for AGENCY, dotted for TRADE AND HEDGED. Form carries
the meaning, not colour, so it survives both the thumb and grayscale. This is
the best answer anyone gave to the deck's actual weakness. Its parent
treatment wanted a whole meta slide auditing the deck's own sourcing; that
slide is CUT as inside-baseball, and its finding is folded into one plain
English headline instead.

**The atmosphere, palette and type are the FIELD DOCUMENTARIAN's.** This
matters more than it looks. Both other directors proposed a COLD-dominant
dark register, one prussian indigo, one cold-room instrument dark, and both
sit uncomfortably close to deck 18's forbidden graphite-teal sea fog on wet
steel. The field documentarian was the only one to notice the trap and price
it, and its answer is right: SODIUM ON SILT, a warm sodium deck lamp over
warm brown-black glacial silt, clear air with no fog at all, with cold
confined to exactly one material in the deck, the slush ice. That is a hard
divergence from 18 and it serves the TYPE_TEMPERATURE 4 dial. Its type
proposal wins for the same reason: both other directors pitched Instrument
Serif plus Manrope plus JetBrains Mono, which is deck 16's exact pairing from
five runs ago. UNBOUNDED has never carried a deck in eighteen runs and
FRAUNCES has never been used as body text. That is the freshest legal move on
the board.

## What the room got wrong, and what was cut

- **The panorama spine is CUT.** The field documentarian proposed one
  continuous slush tote running the deck's full 10,800 px width at 180 px per
  hour, and then named the fatal risk in its own self-critique, that a band at
  the bottom of ten consecutive slides stops being a hero and becomes
  furniture. It was right. The 180 px per hour precision is also craft no
  reader can verify, which is real cost for invisible payoff.
- **The one-object-under-one-camera chassis is CUT as a deck-wide device.**
  Both the systems illustrator and the data journalist wanted their object on
  every slide under a fixed projection. That is deck 17's chassis (one slab,
  ten times, one axonometric camera) and it is barred. The column is the hero
  on two slides and is deliberately absent from the rest.
- **The 1-below-5-above ownership census is CUT.** The systems illustrator
  flagged it honestly as rhetoric wearing a measurement's clothes, and a
  hostile Alaskan reader would be right to say the permit holder owns the
  boat, the permit, the crew and the catch. It becomes a NAMED LIST WITH NO
  SCORE, exactly as that director recommended when asked what to tame.
- **Ten slides becomes NINE.** Two of the three treatments proposed foldable
  slides and both were right about which ones.

## The lower-third answer

Not a band device. Every slide stands on a WET DECK PLANE, a graded, lit,
sodium-raked boat deck receiving each object's two-part contact shadow, with
the annotation furniture running down onto it. Modeled tone by construction
on every slide, which is what the doctrine clause and qa.py's frame_balance
actually ask for, and it gives the deck the physical presence the field
documentarian was right to insist on.

## Continuity devices (neither forbidden)

**(A) THE HOUR MARKER.** A slim vertical hour scale in the left margin of
every slide, 0 at the deck plane and 60 at the top, with one gold marker that
climbs across the deck and doubles as the progress read. Vertical and
marginal, which is structurally unlike deck 18's horizontal span states and
deck 17's cut register.

**(B) THE PROVENANCE CHIP.** Introduced on S04 and worn by every figure

**(C) PALETTE ARC.** Sodium warmth drains out of the frame after the S07
