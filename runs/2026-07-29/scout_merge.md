## The shape of the week

The in-window AI-in-Alaska harvest is genuinely thin, and it is thin in a
specific way: the loudest stories are all the SAME story the series has now
shipped four times (data centers, land, power cost, and who pays). Beat A,
Beat D and Beat F all independently landed back on that beat. Meanwhile three
of the strongest-sounding candidates fail on a technicality that matters:

- Beat C's headline find (the Alaska Earthquake Center's automated landslide
  detector now running on about 200 seismic stations, Alaska Public Media
  2026-07-23) is NOT machine learning. The scout flagged it honestly and the
  showrunner verified it directly. Karasozen and West's own write-up says they
  "created an algorithm to continually scan seismic data ... to look for a
  landslide wave signature," keyed on landslides producing disproportionately
  more long-period energy. No source uses AI, ML, model or network. It is
  signature matching. Spectacular numbers (a 330 foot wave at Tracy Arm, 64
  million cubic meters, a 1,580 foot run up) and the wrong deck for a brand
  called Alaska.Ai. KILLED on honesty, not on interest.
- Beat B's best story (a DOGE staffer pasting NEH grant descriptions into
  ChatGPT with a one line "Does the following relate at all to DEI?" prompt,
  which flagged and killed the UAF Alaska Native Language Center's grant) is
  extraordinary and is dated March to April 2026, months outside the window,
  with the reported May summary judgment unverifiable (acls.org 403).
  HELD as a strong future candidate with a fresh peg.
- Beat E's Anthropic Claude Impact Lab at Alaska Pacific University (Aug 4,
  23 registered) is in window and primary sourced, and the studio is not
  going to build a deck about the company that makes the model writing it.
  DECLINED on conflict, not on facts.

1. **Beat D — the FY2027 NDAA data center land lease restriction.** The House
   passed H.R. 8800 on 2026-07-22, 216 to 212, Begich yea (House Clerk roll
   call 278, primary). A Mills amendment bars DoD from leasing land for data
   centers to operators using components made in China, Russia, Iran or North
   Korea, which lands directly on the Department of the Air Force's offer of
   about 4,700 acres across 12 parcels at JBER, Eielson and Clear. Sullivan

   impacts. Excellent primary sourcing, genuinely new actors (Congress, DAF).
   PASSED OVER only because it is the fifth data center adjacent deck in nine
