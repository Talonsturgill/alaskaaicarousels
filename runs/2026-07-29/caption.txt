On a Cook Inlet salmon boat this month, a sockeye enters a steel box on deck and leaves it iced.

Computer vision picks the fish and the path. Brain spiked, gills cut, iced, 6.5 seconds or less by Shinkei's count.

Upper Cook Inlet holds more than 1,300 drift and set gillnet permits, and sockeye carries the value there. Boats in that fleet now carry the machine. It goes aboard free. The catch reportedly sells at a premium. That deal is fair.

A suffocated salmon stiffens in about 7 hours. Shinkei says its process stretches that to 60. Those hours become a brand, Seremoni Grade, a 16,000 square foot plant in Tacoma, and NERA, the firm's own system rating how many days a fish can be sold. Headquarters is El Segundo.

Alaska holds the fish, the water and the crews. Who gets to decide where the clock is owned?
