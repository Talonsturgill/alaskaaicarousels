# STORYBOARD, Carousel No.84, run 2026-10-11
# "THE PAINT LAW": who sets the clock on a plate read, an UPDATE of No.55

## THESIS
Anchorage's surveillance ordinance has three texts on the table for October 20th, and the difference between them is where the clock lives. The sponsors' August 18th original wrote numbers into code: plate data deleted within 14 days, a warrant to search anything older, sixty minutes of live access, a 2031 end date. The mayor's September 15th substitute leaves retention to a policy "based on articulable operational needs", the sponsors' own S-1 contains no fourteen-day rule either, and both are the first texts to define surveillance by "machine learning, artificial intelligence, or similar technology". Meanwhile the one new kind of camera in town, the Flock readers mapped at two Lowe's stores, answers to none of the three. A number a resident can read is worth more than a limit the public has to ask for, and the deck leaves that verdict to the reader.
PDF document title (copy.json): "Who Sets the Clock on a Plate Read" (34 chars).
