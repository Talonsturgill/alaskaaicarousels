## Pick
Alaska's SNAP bill under Public Law 119-21, read on the day after it started.
- October 1st, 2026 (federal FY2027): the federal share of SNAP administrative cost fell from 50 to 25 percent (sec. 10106). Legislative Finance puts Alaska's added share at about $6.6 million a year; ADN at nearly $11 million.
- The benefit cost share (0/5/10/15 percent by payment error rate, sec. 10105) starts FY2028, but Alaska's FY2025 rate (about 23 percent, the nation's highest) times 1.5 clears 20 percent, so Alaska starts FY2029. If FY2026's rate times 1.5 also clears 20 percent (about 13.34 percent), Alaska starts FY2030.
- From FY2029 the share uses the error rate of the third preceding fiscal year. FY2029's share rests on FY2026, the fiscal year that ended Wednesday September 30th.
- The rate is produced by an eligibility operation running on EIS (COBOL on a mainframe, the state's own words on GitHub) and a half-finished ARIES, with eligibility technician vacancies at 34.3 percent in SFY2025 (Legislative Finance). The state is buying the way out in milestones ($20M, then $5M to move SNAP data off the mainframe).

Why: (1) concrete Alaska impact in dollars with a statute clock that turned two days ago; (2) visual potential, a staircase of tiers, a threshold line at 13.34, a three-year lag, a mainframe; (3) tangible, 66,000 Alaskans; (4) a staffer, a legislator or a DPA manager would forward it. The machine is not AI and the deck will not pretend it is. The technology claim is the one the state makes itself, that the system that measures the error is the system being replaced.
