## THE STORY

**The Permafrost Discovery Gateway's near real time lake drainage tracker, and
the roughly 30 lakes it flagged in northwestern Alaska in June and July 2026.**

On August 26th, 2026, Anna Liljedahl of the University of Alaska Fairbanks and
Woodwell Climate Research Center and Ingmar Nitze of the Alfred Wegener
Institute published a first-person account of a machine learning pipeline that
reads aerial photography going back to the late 1940s against modern satellite
imagery to detect Arctic lake drainage. The Alaska Beacon republished it on
August 27th. The near real time drainage tracker itself went live on the
Gateway on August 13th, 2026.

## WHY THIS ONE, against the four criteria in order

**1. Strongest concrete Alaska impact.** About 80 percent of Alaska's land is
underlain by permafrost and more than 140 Arctic communities are built on it.
The mechanism here is not weather, it is ground. A lake that drains is the
surface receipt for ice that has gone from underneath, and the same collapse
takes runways, road embankments, tank farms and houses. This is a tool a tribal
government, a DOT engineer or a village utility can open in a browser for free,
today, and it is watching the exact failure mode that costs Alaska villages
their infrastructure.

**2. Visual potential, and this run needs it more than usual.** The standing
weakness is artwork craft, weakest in nine of the last ten runs. This story
hands the art three things nine previous decks would have paid for. It has real
geography on true lon and lat, the Seward and Baldwin peninsulas, Kobuk Delta.
It has quantity at four orders of magnitude, from about 30 drained lakes to
about 4,600 in the study region to more than 4 million monitored to 70 million
ponds, which is a magnitude ladder the art can encode honestly. And it has a
before and after built into the source material, a survey photograph from the
late 1940s against a 2026 satellite pass of the same basin.

**3. Tangibility.** A lake vanishing is a thing a person can picture without a
diagram. Nothing on this beat in the last month has been this concrete.
Megawatts, acres and award tables are abstractions a reader has to convert. A
lake that was there in June and gone in July is not.

**4. Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to a specific
coworker. Anyone who sites, engineers, insures or maintains anything north of
the Alaska Range has a professional reason to open this tool. That is a
different and better reason to share than outrage.

## THE ANGLE

The deck is not "AI helps scientists." That framing is available to any outlet
and this page does not write it.

**The argument is that the instrument is now faster than the thing it watches
was ever supposed to be, and that its power comes from an archive nobody built
for this.** The machine learning is doing something specific and unglamorous. It
is comparing today against a photograph taken from a survey plane in the late
1940s by someone with entirely different intentions, and calling the difference.
The tell is not that a lake got smaller. It is that a lake can empty in hours,
because what failed was not the water, it was the ice holding it.

The second beat, and the one that makes this a story rather than a capability
announcement, is that the 2025-26 winter was cold and about 30 lakes drained
anyway. A cold winter is the intuition every Alaskan has for "the ground will be
fine this year." The instrument says otherwise, which is the whole reason to
have an instrument.
