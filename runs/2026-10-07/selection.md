# SELECTION, run 2026-10-07, Carousel No.80

## Decision
THE STORY: On October 5th the Department of Energy said it will put up to $150 million of Defense Production Act money into the Alaska Energy Authority's Beluga-Healy Transmission Project, a second high-voltage path between Southcentral and the Interior: about 223 miles of new line, $418 million in all, $268 million of it non-federal and not yet found, construction not expected until after 2030. DOE justified it by "the major U.S. military expansion currently underway in Alaska" and by mining. On those same Interior installations the Air Force has offered about 4,700 acres to private developers for AI data centers, and Senator Murkowski told the American Homefront Project on September 28th she is not a proponent of data centers at JBER or the interior installations "because of an insecure energy base at this time". The DOE release never says data center.

Why it wins on the criteria, in order:
1. Concrete Alaska impact: the one wire that carries power between the two halves of the Railbelt, which serves about three quarters of Alaskans (DOE). A primary federal decision dated October 5th, inside the window.
2. Visual potential: real geography (Beluga's gas plant on the west side of Cook Inlet to Healy's coal plant in the Alaska Range), a real existing line (AEA's 170-mile Alaska Intertie, Willow to Healy, mid-1980s, built for 345 kV and run at 138 kV) and committed geodata for both (ak-transmission-69kv, ak-generation-20mw, ak-denali-dem). Quantities that draw honestly: 150 of 418, 223 vs 170 miles, a calendar from August 27th "Under Consideration" to October 5th funded to construction after 2030.
3. Tangibility: a wire, a dollar figure, a route, a date.
4. Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker: yes. Every utility, Fairbanks and Mat-Su reader, and anyone following the base data center leases.
