# SELECTION 2026-08-05, Carousel No. 26

## THE STORY

On July 28, 2026 the Administration for Native Americans published its final
Fiscal Year 2026 program policies, standing up three funding opportunities
(EAGLE, the AI3 Action Institute, and NCNTTA) and leaving SEDS-AK, the
Alaska-specific economic development program, out of the set. Two days later
it posted the AI3 Action Institute, a single national award with a $3,500,000
ceiling to build an artificial intelligence resource center for Native
communities. Alaska Native villages under ANCSA and nonprofit village
consortia are named eligible applicants. Both windows close August 27.

The argument the deck makes is not that a program was cancelled, because the
agency disputes that word and the evidence does not settle it. The argument is
narrower, harder, and fully documented. In the same week that a national AI
door opened to Alaska Native villages, the door that had Alaska's name on it
was folded into a national competition, and the objection to that is printed
in the agency's own answer to comments.

## WHY THIS ONE

1. CONCRETE ALASKA IMPACT. A live deadline an eligible Alaskan organization
   can still act on, 22 days out at publication. Named eligibility for ANCSA
   villages in a federal notice of funding opportunity.
2. VISUAL POTENTIAL. Real countable geometry. Three notices where there used
   to be a different set. 31 awards against 1 award. $24,000,000 spread wide
   against $3,500,000 concentrated in one national center. Five project areas
   where Alaska's own lane used to sit beside the general one.
3. TANGIBILITY. Primary documents throughout. 24 of 28 verified claims come
   from the Federal Register, GovInfo or the grants portal.
4. WOULD AN ALASKAN SEND THIS TO A COWORKER. Yes, and to a specific coworker.
   Anyone working at a tribal consortium, a village corporation, a Native
   nonprofit or a regional health organization has 22 days and probably has
   not seen it, because no Alaska newsroom has covered it. Every scout that
   swept Alaska outlets came back empty on this story.

## WHAT THIS DECK MAY NOT SAY

The fact-checker's four corrections and one hole, carried forward so the
directors room and the copy chamber cannot re-introduce them.

1. The complement-not-replace and ethics-and-governance passage is in the
   FEDERAL REGISTER, not on the AI3 grants listing. Cite C20 correctly.
2. The IDEAS expansion and the five project-area names are on the EAGLE
   GRANTS LISTING and are NOT in the Federal Register, which never spells the
   acronym out. Cite C08 and C09 correctly.
3. The Alaska Federation of Natives quote is dated April 14, 2026, four
   months before this window, was written while the comment period was open,
   and belongs to the ORGANIZATION and to no named person. Label it as AFN's
   position during the comment period.
4. The deadline zone label is the agency's own. The listing prints Eastern
   Standard Time for a date that falls in Eastern Daylight Time. Quote it as
   printed, convert nothing, and derive no Alaska hour.
5. THE HOLE. What SEDS-AK was actually worth in dollars is not obtainable.
   The ACF grants page returns 403. No slide may imply a dollar loss to
   Alaska, and the deck must say what it does not know.

Two things must always run in pairs or not at all. The objection (C04) runs
with the agency's answer (C05). The commenter's 70 percent staffing figure
(C22) runs with ANA's correction that the money is annual rather than
three-year (C23), or it does not run.

And the word "replace" is contested. ANA says SEDS has evolved. Tribal
Business News says the new grants replace it. The deck shows both and lets the
reader judge, because the evidence does not settle it and asserting either
word would be taking a side the record does not support.
