# STORYBOARD, No.91, 2026-10-20: THE PINNED RELIEF SHEET

## THESIS
Open speech AI barely hears Central Alaskan Yup'ik. Played clips from Bethel's own broadcaster, the one public model that lists Yup'ik answered Central Siberian Yupik, the language of St. Lawrence Island and eastern Russia, 111 times and Yup'ik 110. In a four-label pilot trained on about 1,600 labeled Yup'ik and Samoan clips, Yup'ik recall rose from 0.41 to 0.92. So the scarce thing is labeled hours, and whoever labels them draws the map. On Saturday, AFN delegates take up 26-20, which asks Congress for permanent language money that Tribes run, with technology among its uses. It never mentions AI.

## THE STANDING WEAKNESS, and what is different here
Artwork craft was the weakest criterion in 7 of the last 10 runs: the largest object drawn with least care, the same marks on four frames, dead lower thirds, texture artifacts. Here the largest object on every map frame is the plaster relief slab itself, built first as a lit material (Lambert plus a Blinn sheen on resin, cavity occlusion, a marched cast, a bevelled slab edge) before any pin or type goes on. Every map frame bleeds its relief through the lower third, so no bottom band is empty. No mark-making family carries more than three frames. Type sits in the sheet's dark margin or on open sea, never on a box.
