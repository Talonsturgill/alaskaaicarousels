# STORYBOARD, Carousel No.87, run 2026-10-15
# "ONE GIGAWATT, TO SCALE": the size of the ask is the question

## THESIS
On October 12th Golden Valley Electric issued its first Energy Emergency Alert, a Level 2: two units were down for planned maintenance when a third failed, and KUAC reported a 15 MW cushion of reserve. Two days later the Interior Secretary told the Fairbanks Chamber that people "want to drop 10 billion on a data center". The same week UAF's Alaska Center for Energy and Power published "Computing in Place", which sizes five kinds of data center in megawatts, from an edge site at about 0.1 to 0.5 MW to hyperscale at 50 to 100+ MW "or even GW". The position: before yes or no, the first question is how big, and the guide hands Alaskans the ruler. The deck draws everything at ONE scale, 10.8 px per MW, every frame exactly 100 MW wide, so the carousel itself is one gigawatt long and a reader can see the sizes without being told what to think. It takes no side on data centers.

## SLIDE COUNT RATIONALE
Ten, because the law is the count: ten frames of 100 MW are one gigawatt, the guide's own upper word for the largest types. Nine would make the carousel 900 MW and break the cover's promise. Every frame still adds a verified specific; the breather (09) is the only frame without a new number, and it carries the guide's question.

## THE STANDING WEAKNESS, and what is different here
Artwork craft, weakest in 7 of the last 10 runs (6.5 last). The repeated cause is the largest object being the least modelled thing. Here the largest object on most frames is the MAT, and it is modelled everywhere: a lit sun pane with a 9 px penumbra and mullion shadows, a printed 10 MW grid, a rubber tooth at two frequencies, healed knife scores, and every board's cast and cavity occlusion marched from the same sun. Every object is a height field shaded per device pixel (Sobel normals, Lambert plus Blinn, a marched penumbral cast), never a fill. The two decks that scored art 8 were one coherent material world in modelled 2D; this is one. Cover first, then 06 (the masterful depth frame), both closed before round 3.
