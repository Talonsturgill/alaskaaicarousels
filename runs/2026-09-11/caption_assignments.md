## THE TWO ASSIGNMENTS

Both were chosen because the STORY rewards them, not for novelty's own sake. The
assignment is a starting constraint and not an order. A director who finds it
fighting the story says so and proposes the move the story rewards, and the
critic weighs clarity ABOVE ledger freshness every time.

**DIRECTOR A. Opening move QUOTE FIRST. Structure BRAID.**
Neither has run in the visible window. QUOTE FIRST is the strongest available
opening for this story because the deck owns two sentences that do the work
cold. C17, Enstar's own "This doesn't mean we will be short this many days this
winter", is a utility pre-emptively denying something, which makes a stranger
ask what was said. C20, "phased by customer class under the utility's tariff", is
the rule stated in the passive voice by the only party who has read it. BRAID
fits because the deck genuinely has two threads, a measured number that anyone
can check and a written order that nobody outside can read, and they meet in the
last line.

**DIRECTOR B. Opening move LEDGER TALLY. Structure Q AND A.**
LEDGER TALLY suits a deck whose spine is a daily measurement, 7.65 of 13.0, 36
days on record, 34 of them verified, receipts up front. Q AND A has not run in
the visible window and is the honest shape for a story where the reader's real
questions are short and answerable. What is in the tank. Who decides who loses
it first. When does more gas arrive.

## THE HARD RULES BOTH BRIEFS CARRY

- 300 to 900 characters, hook complete inside 140, exactly 3 niche hashtags on
  the final line, no links, no sources, no credits.
- A DECK SUMMARY LINE is mandatory, its own line, not the hook and not the
  closing question, naming the CONTENTS rather than pointing at the artifact.
  Not a three-item inventory, which is burned.
- No em or en dashes, NO COLONS (clock times excepted), no semicolons, straight
  quotes, no emojis, no first person, no sentence opening on "And" or "But",
  "can't" and never "cannot", dates month first and ordinal.
- Comma ceiling 6.2 per 100 words of body. The cure is splitting the sentence,
  never deleting the comma and leaving a run-on.
- **THE DECK DOES NOT PREDICT A SHORTAGE.** If the caption's shape requires the
  reader to conclude Southcentral will run out of gas, it is wrong. C17 is the
  shield and belongs in or near the caption.
- C19 may never be written as though the tariff says it. It is a mayor's account
  of what Enstar described. C45's five cents is an outlet's paraphrase and may
  never appear in quotation marks as Dunleavy's words.
