# STORYBOARD, Carousel No. 56, 2026-09-11

## DECK HEADER

## THESIS

Southcentral's gas position can be measured, and this page measures it every
morning. The order in which that gas is taken away is a real published
instrument with a number and a title, and the only public account of where a
large industrial load sits inside it is a mayor repeating a meeting.

**TITLE (for the PDF), 34 characters.** `Section 1200 Is Called Interruption`

## THE ROOM, AND WHAT WAS TAKEN FROM EACH

Three lenses pitched. Editorial-essayist, cinematographer, cartographer.

**The CARTOGRAPHER's chassis wins**, the sheet and the margin, nine discrete
extents. Its reserve answer is the only STRUCTURAL one of the three. Type lives
in the margin, every art layer is clipped to the neatline before a mark is
generated, so art touching glyphs is impossible rather than mitigated. A map
sheet's widest margin is its bottom one, which puts modelled furniture in the
lower third by the form's own convention rather than by apology. That is the
standing weakness answered by construction.

**The ESSAYIST's best image is promoted to the thesis object.** Section 1200 is
present, solid, heavy and real, and the deck sees it END ON, so the reader sees
that it has thickness, a tab and a structure, and never sees its face. This
REPLACES the cartographer's invented second scale bar, which that director
himself flagged as an overclaim in borrowed standardised furniture. An object
seen edgewise invents no convention. It is the literal truth of the situation.

**The CINEMATOGRAPHER overrules the cartographer on light.** The cartographer
proposed an aerial gain arc falling from 0.95 to 0.50 across the deck as the
argument moves away from measured ground. That is a dread gradient, and the
cinematographer named the failure exactly. The light is not allowed to argue,
because the data could flip and the light would then be a lie. **ONE DAY, ONE
WEATHER, ONE EXPOSURE, NO ARC.**

Also taken: the essayist's Archivo width axis as an EVIDENCE GRADE. The
cartographer's guard that C15 and C03 may never share an axis. All three
converged independently on gold meaning measured-or-written-down, which is
strong enough evidence to make it law. All three independently refused to print
C10.

## THE STRUCTURAL RESERVE, stated once and binding on all nine slides

Type lives in the MARGIN. Every art layer, relief, hachure, stipple, d3
coastline, borough strokes and leaders, is drawn into an offscreen canvas that is
`cx.clip()`ed to the sheet's neatline rect BEFORE a single mark is generated. The
margin is a different plane the art has no access to. That is why art cannot
touch glyphs on this deck.

For the few labels that must sit INSIDE the neatline, the pipeline is
`await document.fonts.ready`, then `AKSTIPPLE.reserve('[data-reserve]')` per LINE
BOX, then every in-window layer including the d3 path pass consults
`AKSTIPPLE.reserved(x, y)` before emitting, then `AKENGRAVE.punchReserves(off,
boxes, {blur: 9, pad: 10})` with BLURRED RECTANGLES, never a radial punch. In
addition, the margin's own stipple carries the prototyped BASIN TERM from
`storyboard_header_draft.md` section "THE RESERVE IDIOM", so the margin's quiet
bands read as tonal basins rather than as plates.

THE CONTACT TRIPLE WAS REBUILT TWICE, and the second time is the one worth
reading.

Round one. The first attempt stacked the shadow rect under the ground rect,
which put the ground sample on the pool's dark edge and made the shadow read
LIGHTER than the ground it sits on, by 13 to 15 L* on every one of six slides.
`contact_probe.py` named the cause in one line, that a pool is brightest at its
centre and the two rects belong side by side at the object's own base line.
Fixed by lifting each cast to the foot, dropping each pool centre 14px below it,
and re-measuring off the render. The six went to 12.6 through 14.9 L*, against a
4.0 floor.

Round two, and it reversed part of round one. Those numbers were bought by
raising the lit pool, because dL is ground minus shadow and the pool is the
cheap half of that subtraction. Five pixel critics then read the nine slides
independently, in five sessions with no knowledge of each other, and every one
of them called the pool a lens flare, a bloom, a glow sticker or a blob. Nine
slides out of nine, while `qa.py` returned PASS on all nine. The gate measures a
difference and a difference has two sides: a ground lit brighter than the object
standing on it is an object floating over a light source, which is the same
defect the gate exists to catch. `aksheet.litPool` now peaks at 0.28 and is
documented as a lift rather than a lamp, `cast` gained a `k` strength so
separation is earned from the shadow side, and the cast is biased along
`S.LIGHT.azDeg` because a symmetric shadow is a vignette. The nine now measure
4.7 to 9.9 L*, lower numbers than round one and a better picture, which is the
whole lesson.
