# "THE EXHIBITS": software can read the tape, and the camera is still the cost

## THESIS
On October 9th the North Pacific Fishery Management Council backed NMFS's draft 2027 observer plan and a yearly test for Alaska's fixed-gear camera pool: a boat that averages under 3 trips a year over 3 years goes back to human observers and hands its camera to a boat that fishes. NMFS ran the test on 172 camera boats with a full 2023 to 2025 history and 76 would move, 39 that never fished and 37 more under the line. Software from an AFSC, APU, Nexus and NMFS team found 96 of 97 sleeper sharks on longline camera video in earlier research, and NMFS intends to scale those tools if staff are available. The position: the AI is real, and it works on the cheap side of the bill. NMFS's own analysis puts the deciding cost in the camera gear, a high buy-in of about $6,003 a boat a year, while reviewing a selected trip adds little. So the line that decides who keeps a camera is a trip count, not an algorithm, and the human cost of the line lands on a bunk.
