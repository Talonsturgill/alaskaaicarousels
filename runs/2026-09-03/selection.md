# SELECTION — 2026-09-03 — Carousel No. 49

## THE STORY

**Kodiak's grid is isolated, a new Navy building will draw on it, and the
cooperative that has to plan for that load was never given a number.**

KMXT reported on September 3rd, 2026, the run date itself, that the Navy is
building a "multi-mission support building" at its Special Warfare Center
Detachment on Spruce Cape, after an island rumor that it was an AI data center.
The Navy's spokesperson says flatly it is "not a data center" (C02), and told
Sen. Sullivan's office the same thing (C15). The Navy also says the building will
run off the existing grid (C03).

That grid is the story. Kodiak Electric Association's own words, and this is the
part that needs nobody's reporting, are that it "owns, operates, and maintains an
isolated grid" (C08), that Terror Lake carries approximately 85 percent of
generation (C09), and that the balance is six wind turbines, two flywheels and a
battery, with diesel as back up (C10). Peak load hit just over 31 MW in March and
the cooperative almost had to start the diesels (C06).

And the two sentences that sit next to each other in the same paragraph and make
the deck. The Navy has not told the utility how much power the building will use
(C04). The utility's own planning document expects it could be up to an
additional two megawatts (C05).

**THE THESIS. On an isolated grid every new load is subtracted from a fixed
number, and the entity adding this one is not obliged to say how big it is. The
question that matters is not whether it is a data center. It is that Kodiak has
to plan for it either way, from an estimate the utility had to make itself.**

That framing is fair to everyone in it. It does not call the Navy a liar, it does
not call the rumor true, and it does not predict a blackout. It reports a
measurement problem, which is what the record actually supports.

## WHAT THE DECK MAY NOT DO

Straight from the fact-checker, and these are build rules rather than notes.

1. **Never assert it is a data center, and never assert it is not.** The Navy's
   denial is a denial. The rumor is a rumor. Neither is established, and the deck
   is better for saying so out loud.
2. **Never print a flat 2 MW.** It is "could be up to", it is the utility's own
   ceiling estimate, and it comes from a document nobody outside the cooperative
   has read. The whole point is that the Navy supplied no number.
3. **Write "in March" with no year.** The page states no year and the inference
   was killed.
4. **No start date and no imminence.** It is unclear when work begins (C18).
5. **Say "isolated", the utility's own word.** Do not write that the grid can't
   import power. No source says it.
6. **No slide may be built on the Coast Guard expansion.** It exists only inside
   a paraphrase of an unobtainable document (C16).
7. **The Sullivan quote is second hand and ends on a comma.** It was said to the
   Kodiak Daily Mirror on August 11th, not to KMXT, and the originating outlet
   rate limited every attempt to read it.

## THE SOURCING DISCLOSURE, WHICH IS A DESIGN PROBLEM AND NOT A FOOTNOTE

The news spine is single outlet. Every event, quote and megawatt figure outside
the cooperative's own website comes from KMXT, and the fact-checker could find no
second outlet or primary document despite trying DVIDS, HigherGov, the senator's
own newsroom, Alaska Public Media, Alaska Beacon, the Kodiak Daily Mirror and
four search engines.

The deck ships anyway, because the argument does not rest on the news. It rests
on the shape of the grid, and four claims carrying that shape are PRIMARY, in the
utility's own words. But the deck has to SAY so, on a slide, in the artwork, not
in a footnote. TECHNIQUE_LIBRARY 58e, Source-Provenance Evidence Tags, exists for
exactly this and has not been used in the last four decks. The honesty beat is
now a required slide, and drawing the difference between what one newsroom
reported and what the utility publishes about itself is a better slide than a
disclaimer would be.

## DOCKET DECISION, AND IT IS A DELIBERATE NO

This story does NOT go on the Alaska AI Docket. The docket tracks AI
infrastructure decisions, and putting a building the Navy says is not a data
center onto a page titled that way would assert exactly what the fact-checker
says is unestablished. There is also no decision to track, no comment window, no
decider and no published date. Recorded here so a later run does not read the
absence as an oversight.

# PHASE 5 — THE DIRECTORS ROOM, AND THE SYNTHESIS

Three lenses, three complete treatments, all three usable. Rotation this run was
CARTOGRAPHER, SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, FIELD-DOCUMENTARIAN.

## WHAT EACH ONE PROVED

**CARTOGRAPHER, "THE ISLAND PLATE AND THE STAFF WITH NO TOP MARK".** The best
single device anyone produced. A graduated steel staff standing at Spruce Cape,
16 px per megawatt, whose graduations climb past the last numeral and exit the
frame with NO TOP MARK, because the ceiling of this system is not a number the
record gives anyone. It also independently found the borough-versus-island trap
that I had already measured, and proposed the same fix.

**SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, "THE BOLTED ONE-LINE ON A BASALT SHELF".** The best
object. Every solid on the island's one-line carries a stamped nameplate boss
with its rating on it, and one boss is milled bright and blank. Its hook
archetype, THE BLANK NAMEPLATE, is genuinely new. Its own self-critique is the
reason it does not win, and the critique is correct, that its cover "leans harder
on its headline than a cover should".

**FIELD-DOCUMENTARIAN, "THE RUNOFF MIRROR AND THE UNLIT BAND". THE WINNER.**
It produced the one cover that argues with no words at all. Five turbine rotors
drawn as motion-blurred sweep arcs and the sixth dead sharp, blades locked in a
frozen Y, shot from above and behind so the machines sit below the horizon
against the dark town. A reader at 432 px sees five moving and one stopped and
has the story before reading a syllable. Nothing in the other two treatments
does that.

It also had the sharpest answer to the standing weakness. Its focal points are
below the midline on NINE of nine slides, its lower band is a population of
discrete specular chips on wet ground rather than any gradient, and it bans fog
from the near band by rule. That is aimed precisely at No.48's measured finding
that soft radials read as nothing after a 6x box downsample.

The field-documentarian's world, light and camera are the spine. Four transplants.
