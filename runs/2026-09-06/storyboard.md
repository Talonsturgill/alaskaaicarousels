# STORYBOARD — Carousel No. 52 — 2026-09-06
# THE WATERLINE IS THE ARGUMENT

## DECK HEADER

### Thesis

A fisheries survey record survives an instrument swap only where both instruments measured the same
water at the same time, so the real cost of modernizing is not the robot, it is the years of paying
for two of everything, and nobody has put a price on that overlap.

### Document title

`Run Both Instruments, or Lose the Record` (40 characters)

### The directors room, and what was taken from whom

Three treatments, three lenses. **The field documentarian's WATERLINE won** and is the deck.

It won on one test. The data journalist's PAPER ROLL and the historian's ACCESSION DRAWER are both
excellent and both argue about an object that stands FOR the survey record rather than an object that
IS in it. Both directors said so themselves in their own self-critiques, which is to their credit and
is also the finding. A chart roll on a dry-lab table and a specimen drawer in a cabinet are metaphors,
and a metaphor has to be explained by the copy before the picture can prove anything. The waterline
deck argues with the actual furniture of the story, water, a trawl, a camera rig, a boulder, an
otolith, a glove, a boat rail, and it never needs a sentence to license the picture.

It also solves this run's declared compositional problem by PHYSICS rather than by decree. A hanging
object's mass falls; the bright near-surface water sits above it. Seven of nine frames have no ground
plane at all, which does not dodge the contact-shadow defect class so much as delete it, and the two
frames that do have one are lit from a broad near-vertical key, which means the ground is bright
BEFORE anything casts onto it.

**Grafted from the data journalist.** The guard-line discipline, which is that any frame carrying a
schematic prints its own honesty label in the frame (slide 03 and slide 07 both do). And the treatment
of an ABSENCE as a fully modelled bare surface rather than as emptiness, which is why slide 08's empty
bracket sits on a rail rendered at the same craft as the two objects beside it.

**Grafted from the historian.** The record-as-message-to-strangers framing, which is what slide 09's
ask is built on, and the explicit no-connection guard on the twelve days, which the fact-checker also
demanded in C33's note.

**Overruled from the winning treatment, twice, and both on feasibility.**

1. The bare hand on slide 05 becomes a **gloved hand**, orange fisherman's glove, built as an
   akrelief heightfield rather than an aksdf raymarch. The director flagged the bare hand as the
   deck's biggest render risk and proposed a gloved fallback held in reserve. Taking the fallback up
   front is the cheaper decision and it is also the truer one, because nobody sorts a catch bare
   handed in September. It gives safety orange a second job and it removes anatomy from the critical
   path.
2. **No WebGL anywhere in this deck.** The treatment wanted one akthree PBR render for the stereo rig
   on slide 02. A scattering medium is a compositing problem rather than a materials problem, every
   frame here is built out of extinction ramps, discrete populations and relit heightfields, and
   introducing a GPU path for one object would add a fallback design and a sentinel check to buy an
   object the 2D bench already renders well. Stated as a choice, not hidden.

### Variety ledger check

The last four decks ran, in order, a macro cut-sheet mass filling the frame (09-02), a wet island
night with the subject held low (09-03), a world where the ground carries no strokes and every
straight line belongs to the document (09-04), and one absorbent sheet photographed at nine declared
magnifications (09-05).

**THIS DECK'S REQUIRED DIVERGENCE, stated explicitly.** Nothing here is a macro; the camera never gets
closer to an object than a working distance and never enlarges anything, and slide 05 proves it by
showing a small bone inside a whole glove instead of blowing the bone up. It is daylight, all of it,
and the darkest frame is lit by an overcast sky at noon seen from 96 m down. The ground carries almost
all the strokes here and the only true straight lines in the deck are a steel wire and a boat rail,
both objects. And magnification is held FIXED for nine frames while DEPTH changes, which is the
literal inverse of the retention sheet.

Atmospheres of the last three were bearing-17 rain gated by a lamp cone, low raking light on frozen
muskeg at azimuth 318 elevation 9, and a coaxial near-axial key on damp rag. This deck's key is a
broad source at **elevation 76**, which is neither a cone, nor a rake, nor near-axial, and which is
the physically correct model because refraction compresses the whole sky into Snell's window.

Continuity devices of the last two were a camera pulling up through declared eye heights and a plate
chain. Neither is here; the camera's height changes but so does the medium, and the deck carries no
plates at all.

Hook archetypes of the last three were the machine that isn't moving, the half nobody is arguing
about, and a count of people against a count of people. This deck's hook is **THE BILL FOR THE
OVERLAP**, which is none of those: both machines work, everybody is arguing about this half on a
published agenda, and the cover carries no counts at all.

Palette families of the last three were sodium-free island night, cold boreal night with one warm
token, and peat and mineral. This is **diatom green and wet steel**, a turbid green water register
with a manufactured steel second family, and its dominant hue is a green nobody on this page has run.

Type pairings of the last two were Bricolage Grotesque plus Manrope plus JetBrains Mono, and Fraunces
plus Space Grotesk plus JetBrains Mono. This deck runs **Instrument Serif plus Archivo plus JetBrains
Mono**, and neither forbidden display family leads.

### Depth language, deck wide

Four cues on every submerged frame. **Wavelength-differential extinction** (red dies first, so the
OKLCH ramp shifts hue toward `#17565A` and chroma collapses with distance, built with
`AKC.mixOklab`). **Occlusion**, silhouettes overlapping. **Scale gradient**, repeat elements at
0.72^i. **A discrete marine-snow population**, Poisson placed, count scaling with depth, drawn as
hard-edged specks with a size-and-blur gradient, with one aggregate blurred across the near edge.

The snow is discrete on purpose. A graded wash dies at the 432 px downsample; a population of discrete
steps survives it.

**One light, two media. Key azimuth is declared 000 and is functionally irrelevant below the
waterline; elevation is 76.** Key to fill 2.6:1, the lowest this series has run, and the honest one,
because in a scattering medium the fill IS the medium. Two declared practicals, each meaning
something: slide 04 is lit by the rig's own strobe at azimuth 214 elevation 28, because the machine
brings its own light and that is the deck's argument stated in its lighting; slide 06 is lit by a
synthetic sun at azimuth 315 elevation 45 and says so in the frame, because it is a relief product
rather than a photograph.

### B. COMPOSITION

**4. Layout map.** Twelfths. Display block occupies columns 1 to 9, rows 2 to 5 (y 300 to 660). Focal
point is the gold intersection lens at (612, 1146), which is the lower-right rule-of-thirds
intersection. Eye path is display type, then down the left beam cone, then across the seabed to the
gold lens. Quiet zone is the graded mid water, y 690 to 950, roughly 19 percent of the frame. One
permitted grid violation: the right beam cone crosses the display block's right margin, which is the
swipe cue.

**4a. Lower-third treatment.** The bottom band carries the seabed itself, a modelled terrain field
rather than a plate. `AK.hachureField` runs over a real stored height grid so every stroke's width is
the local slope and every stroke's rotation is the local aspect, which means the texture cannot be
uniform unless the ground is. On it sit two lit ensonified ellipses whose brightness falls with
grazing angle, drawn as accumulated low-alpha passes so the light has depth in it rather than an edge,
plus the gold intersection lens where they overlap, plus a blurred sediment plume bleeding off the
bottom-left corner as a repoussoir foreground. Nothing rests on the seabed in this frame, so the frame has no
ground plane in the sense the engine measures, and the mass in the lower band is light on terrain rather
than an object on a surface.

**5. Depth plan.** Background is the extinction ramp from `#04141A` at the frame edges to `#17565A` at
mid depth. Atmosphere is the marine-snow population, 340 specks, Poisson placed with the near third
carrying 60 percent of them. Structure is the two beam cones. Anchor is the seabed. Type sits above
all of it in DOM. Grain last, `AK.grainTile(280, 52, 20260907)`. Four depth cues, all present:
atmospheric extinction, occlusion (the near cone crosses the far cone), scale gradient (the two hulls
in Snell's window at true relative length, the near snow larger than the far), and depth of field (the
sediment plume and the nearest snow aggregate are blurred, the seabed at y 1140 is the sharp focal
plane). Focal plane is stated: the gold lens.

**6. Continuity device state.** Waterline at y 168, the frame's only bright band. Shot line is PAIRED,
two dashed beam axes at x 402 and x 664, not yet a wire. What bleeds off the right edge is the right
beam cone's outer wall, which completes into slide 02's bridle.

alpha, so the rig is shaded onto its own offscreen layer and composited, never shaded over the water.
Its `scale` option is 2 because the canvas is a 2x backing store; passing 1 paints the top-left
quarter.
