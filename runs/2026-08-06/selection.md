# SELECTION — 2026-08-06 — Carousel No. 27

## THE PICK

**The week Alaska's supply side answered.**

Alaska has spent 2026 selling itself to AI data center developers: state land
assembled at no cost, 4,700 Air Force acres offered across three installations,
and a pitch of 4 to 5 cents per kilowatt-hour. That pitch rests on four
preconditions, and each one is owned by a different institution.

Inside ten days, 2026-07-27 to 2026-08-06, all four owners said something on the
record, and none of them said yes.

| The precondition | Who owns it | What they said, in window |
|---|---|---|
| Gas to burn | Enstar | Has ALREADY turned away new, large-scale customers to supply existing ratepayers. A repeat of last winter brings likely rolling blackouts. |
| A grid that holds | Matanuska Electric | Published a worst-case plan of rolling 30 minute outages and diesel burn at Eklutna. Storage about 4 Bcf against a roughly 7 Bcf winter target. |
| Land with power on it | AIDEA | Its own deputy director, defending the Houston conveyance in a signed op-ed, enumerated what the flagship site does not have. A power source is on that list. |
| A pipeline to make it cheap | The Legislature | Third LNG special session convened July 27th on HB 381. The Speaker put passage chances very low. |

**The thesis.** Alaska is not being told no by the public process everyone is
watching. It is being told no by its own suppliers, in four separate documents,
none of which is a decision anyone can petition, appeal or comment on.

**What the deck does NOT assert.** No causation. No coordination between the
four. No prediction that any project dies. No adequacy verdict about the winter.
Four statements in one window, and the pitch they sit against. The pitch itself
is BACKGROUND (an ADN piece of May 5th) and will be labeled on the slide.

# REFRAME AFTER THE CLAIMS GATE (2026-08-06)

**The fact-checker killed this deck's framing device and the replacement is
better.** This is the second time in three runs (see No.25, 2026-08-04).

## What died

The deck was selected to set four in-window refusals against a marketed price of
4 to 5 cents per kilowatt-hour, compared with 14 to 17 cents paid by large
commercial users. **Neither number exists.** The fact-checker fetched the cited
ADN article of May 5th twice and asked it to enumerate every occurrence of cent,
cents, kilowatt, kWh and megawatt. None of those words appears anywhere in it.
Two independent searches for the phrase returned nothing. The scout had carried
both figures in good faith and neither traces to a source.

Also killed: the 4,700 acres and 12 parcels (the string 4,700 is on no fetched
page), the bill number HB 381 (akleg.gov 403s, no fetched news page names one),
the 90 percent property tax exemption, the 44.5 billion lower bound, and the
Edgmon "chances are very low" quote that reached the scout only through a search
summary of a rate-limited page. Thirteen claims killed in total.

## What replaced it, and why it is stronger

Strip the invented price and the surviving claims say something sharper, because
every one of them comes from the SELLING side rather than from a critic.

**THE LAND IS ON OFFER. THE POWER IS NOT.**

Alaska keeps offering land for AI data centers. On the evidence of the sellers'
own documents, land was never the constraint.

- The Air Force offered five JBER sites of about 60 acres to nearly 200 acres,
  and its own solicitation says those sites have **"insufficient power"** for a
  data center and will require **"a new connection"** to Chugach (C28, C29).
  That is the offeror describing its own offer.
- Chugach's gas contract with its primary supplier ends in 2028 (C30).
- AIDEA's deputy director, defending 19,950 acres north of Houston in a signed
  op-ed, wrote that there is **"no committed data center tenant, industrial
  developer, site plan, power source, cooling system, water supply plan or
  construction schedule"** (C15, C16). Seven blanks, and the fourth is power.
- Enstar has already turned away new, large-scale customers, and its president
  says **"nobody has the gas right now that they're willing to sell"** (C01, C07).
- Storage sits at about 4 Bcf against a 7 Bcf target, and MEA's chief executive
  describes 30 minute rotating curtailments in a worst case (C11, C12, C13).
- The pipeline that would change the arithmetic is in a third special session,
  with the Speaker saying he does not know there will be another opportunity this
  year (C19, C23).

**Why it beats the original.** The price deck argued that a promise was being
undercut. This one does not need a promise. It observes that the two parties
offering the land, the Air Force and AIDEA, each put in writing that the site has
no power, and the two parties who would have to supply it said they have none to
sell. Nobody is contradicted, because nobody disagrees. That is a much harder
thing to argue with than a rate comparison.

## Discipline the storyboard inherits, non-negotiable

1. **No causation, no coordination, no prediction of failure.** Three of the four
   statements are conditional or worst-case, and C15's author is arguing the
   opposite of collapse. He is making an early-stage transparency argument and
   the deck must not quote him as confessing one.
2. **No cost-allocation argument.** That was No.10 on 2026-07-18. This deck is
   about whether the supply exists, never about who pays for it.
3. **No adequacy verdict about the winter.** Same discipline the Gas Watch page
   holds. The deck reports the storage figures and stops.
4. **Background is dated on the slide.** C28, C29 and C30 are from May 5th, well
   outside the window, and every slide carrying them says so.
5. **No bill number, no clock time on August 19th, no rate figures.** All killed.
