## THE SYNTHESIS, and how the room was judged

Three directors pitched. All three independently reached for `akengrave` and all
three independently refused to draw a magnitude in perspective. That convergence
is the strongest signal the room has produced since No.21.

**WINNER, THE FIELD DOCUMENTARIAN'S TRAVERSE**, on one idea that neither other
pitch had and that turns this run's craft attack into an editorial argument:

> **The `form` callback IS a story quantity. Every engraved surface in this deck
> is the shape of a number from the record, and every slide PRINTS what its
> surface is made of.**

`akengrave.surface()` takes `form`, an arbitrary `(x,y) => height`. Every other
deck this studio has shipped would have handed it an fbm heightfield and called
the result Alaska. This deck can't, because nobody went to any of these places
and there is no observed detail anywhere in claims.json. So the surface is the
data, and a mono legend on every slide reads `SURFACE = <what> (<claim-id>)`.
The honesty is drawn rather than disclaimed, which is the only version of a
field-documentary lens this story can support.

Its second gift is the deck's best image, and nobody has drawn it: **19,950 acres
and nearly 200 acres at ONE printed scale with one kilometre bar under both.**

**GRAFTED FROM THE DATA JOURNALIST.** Its price-ladder encoding, and the reason
is that it killed the winner's own weakest slide before the winner built it. The
documentarian drew four engraved cylinders and then named `wMax 4.2` on a 156 px
barrel as its most likely render failure, "four soft smudges". The data
journalist had refused bars outright, on the ground that four bars from a shared
baseline read as one series when these are a contract price, a weighted average
paid, an offer to producers, and an import price. **Four readings on one
graduated rod replaces four cylinders.** Also grafted, its guard line and its
footnote making C04's missing unit visible on the slide rather than in a note.

**GRAFTED FROM THE CINEMATOGRAPHER.** TEST B, the anti-murk tile test, adopted
whole as a pixel-critic check on every slide. It is the most objective quality
gate anyone in the room proposed and it directly measures the defect that has
made artwork craft the weakest criterion in 7 of 10 runs. Also its finding that
`akthree`'s renderer has no alpha channel, which is why the rendered rung
composites through a gradient map rather than through a mask.

**CUT, from the winner's ten.** Station 04, the 192-mark well field. Its 192
exponential terms were the deck's largest performance risk and C08 says more as
one printed line under the quote on the bare plate, where all that drilling

### FALSIFIABLE PALETTE DIVERGENCE TEST, handed to the pixel critics

Sample the 200 highest-chroma pixels of each slide at 432 px wide, OKLCH hue.

1. At least **70 percent** of that mass lands in **190 to 210 degrees** (flame).
2. **Zero** pixels land in **60 to 110 degrees**. That band is olive, moss and
   tundra rust, and it is what TUNDRA AUTUMN drift would look like.
3. Any pixel above **L 0.60 with chroma under 0.03** is type or gold, never a
   large field. That is the GLACIAL FLOUR test.
4. Diesel hue **22 to 40 degrees** totals **under 12 percent** deck-wide and
   **over 45 percent on slide 07 alone**. A palette arc that does not measure
   did not happen.
5. `#FFC72C` occupies **under 0.25 percent** of every slide.

### TEST B, THE ANTI-MURK TEST, grafted from the cinematographer, run on every slide

Tile each slide into 120 x 120 px tiles, 99 per frame. **At least 95 of 99 must
have L\* standard deviation of 2.0 or greater AND L\* range of 6.0 or greater.
Zero tiles may be uniform within 1.0 L\*. No pixel anywhere is `#000000`.**

This is the deck's answer to the standing weakness, stated as a measurement
rather than as an intention. White-line intaglio can't produce a flat fill,
because every region is built from strokes whose width is set by the light and
whose direction is set by the form, so tone and detail arrive in the same pass.
If TEST B fails, the deck failed, and it fails before anyone argues about taste.

## PERFORMANCE RULES THAT ARE NOT OPTIONAL

1. **Precompute any multi-source `form` into a 4 px lookup grid (271 x 338) and
   make `form` a bilinear sample of it.** `normalAt` calls `form` four times per
   sample and `_walk` samples every 6 px. A form with many terms evaluated
   directly puts millions of calls on the main thread and will blow the
   navigation budget.
2. **Never set `cx.filter` inside a stroke loop.** All blurring happens once at
   composite on an offscreen canvas. This is akengrave's own header warning and
   it cost a run on 2026-07-30.
3. **`AKPOST.grade` runs on the ART canvas BEFORE any WebGL canvas is drawn into
   it.** 0.9 seconds before the composite, about 34 seconds after.
4. **No colour helper is ever nested inside another.** All engraving colour comes
   from the integer-indexed ink ladder akengrave builds from `inkLo` and `inkHi`.
5. **Determinism.** No `Math.random`. `AK.rng(20260806 + N)` and
   `AKENGRAVE.create({seed: 20260806 + N})` everywhere.

## TEXT AGAINST GEOMETRY, SOLVED STRUCTURALLY

```
await document.fonts.ready;
eng.reserve(AKENGRAVE.boxesFor("[data-reserve]"));   // feather 22
```

Every text node in this deck carries `data-reserve`. **There is not one knockout
plate over engraved ground anywhere in this deck.** The colliding stroke is never
generated, so there is no plate to size wrong. This is the structural answer to a
hard fail that has landed in 3 of the last 10 runs, and it also makes contrast a
computed constant rather than a review argument, because `reserve` exposes the
region's base fill under every label.
