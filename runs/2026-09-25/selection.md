# SELECTION — No.68, 2026-09-25

## The pick

**What Alaska's November 3rd governor ballot decides about AI compute, and what it doesn't.**

The Alaska Beacon (James Brooks, first published September 18th, republished by Alaska Public
Media September 21st and KTOO September 22nd) put the four remaining governor candidates side by
side on data centers for the first time (C01 to C10). Two are for, each with a condition (Bronson,
"We will produce our own", C02; Taylor, "bring their own energy", C04). Kreiss-Tomkins backs a
moratorium until the Legislature passes a framework (C06, C07). Wilson says she "will never allow
Alaska to be littered with hyperscale data centers" (C08).

The deck sets those four positions against the decisions actually pending, sorted by whose pen
reaches them:

- STATE. The STAK Energy North Slope lease, ADL 422741: a preliminary decision to offer a 50-year
  lease on 715.4 acres (C23, C24), whose Final Finding and Decision is signed under authority
  delegated from the Commissioner of DNR (C53), a department head who serves at the governor's
  pleasure (C45, C60). The AIDEA Houston conveyance, about 19,950 acres, no final decision on the
  notice as of today (C28, C31). And the bill: the Senate passed a data center bill 14 to 5, the
  House never took it up, and the Beacon expects the next governor to face similar legislation
  (C13, C14, C16), with the veto the governor's own tool (C47, C48).
- FEDERAL. The Air Force's offer of about 4,700 acres at JBER, Eielson and Clear, no award (C33 to
  C37), which a borough "can't tell the Pentagon" about (C39) and which Sen. Murkowski opposes
  (C38). FERC's DeepGreen permit in Cook Inlet, where the state's tool is to intervene, as the
  Kenai Peninsula Borough has (C42, C58) and as AEA's chief says AEA will (C44). The FERC deadline,
  November 2nd, falls the day before the election (C43, C46), stated, not dramatised.

The analytic turn, from the primary document: STAK's plant is designed to produce 1 to 3 GW
behind the meter, "not interconnected to the Railbelt or any other Alaska utility grid" (C54,
C55). The condition two candidates put on their support is already how the largest state-land
proposal is drawn. That is a fact about the design, not a verdict on anyone.
