# SHOWRUNNER'S ARC, No.57, 2026-09-12

What the run decided, in the order it decided it, with the reason each decision
turned out to be right or wrong. This is the document a later run reads to find
out what a phase was actually FOR.

## THE STORY CHANGED UNDER THE RUN, and the fact-checker is why

The research put an Inupiat co-management framing at the centre. It was a good
story and nothing in either notice supports it, the study area being 204
kilometres offshore, so the fact-checker killed it along with nine other claims.
Among them, and each one worth naming because each was already written into a
draft. Any take number at all, which four fetch routes failed to find; the
vessel's operator; the word GPS, which appears nowhere in either document; the
190 dB very low frequency source, which is a permission rather than a plan; and
the 185 dB figure as a bare number, which belongs to the 8 to 14 kHz band and
not to 10 kHz.

The rewritten thesis is better than the one it replaced. **The permit knows the
machine's position and does not know the animal's number.** It is entirely
documented, it needs no adversary, and it gave the deck its colour law.

THE LESSON. A killed story is not a lost run. The run lost about forty minutes
and gained a thesis it could defend line by line.

## THE PROTOTYPE EARNED ITS PLACE, and it will again

One frame built on the new chassis, rendered, gated and LOOKED AT before the
directors room reported. `qa.py` returned PASS at zero fails and zero warns on a
frame carrying four real defects, including three hard edged black rectangles
where the reserve should have been, which is a trap written down in two of this
repo's own docstrings and walked into anyway.

Every one of those four was cheaper to fix on one frame than on nine. Do this
every time a run builds a new chassis.

## WHERE THE RUN ACTUALLY LOST TIME

**Layout algebra.** Slides 03, 05, 07 and 08 each took three or more rounds of
collision fixing, and almost all of it was the same problem in different
clothes, a rail lane and a type column and a label stack all competing for one
horizontal band. The depth rail was the aggressor in every case. A shared axis
across eight frames is a commitment to solving that eight times.

**Three wrong diagnoses of one bug.** Slide 02's interference field walked an
offscreen buffer as though it carried the slide canvas's 2x backing. `qa.py`
reported the symptom precisely, naming the line box and the exact 88 px^2, and
three fixes aimed at the arcs, the basin pad and the field threshold all missed,
because the arithmetic was wrong rather than the gating. When a gate is precise
and three fixes fail, stop fixing and go measure.

**The em dash nobody wrote.** Two attempts at that fix each broke a different
gate, and both failures were correct. Dimming the tick made every declared mark
weaker than the band's own texture. Offsetting it 8 px MOVED THE MARK. The
answer was to move the tick to the other side of the spine, which occurred to
nobody until both wrong answers had been measured.

## WHAT THE REVIEW ROOMS WERE WORTH

Six rooms, per slide scores of 4.5 to 6.5 and a sequence at 6.6 against a
threshold of 8.3. They were right about almost everything and the four findings
they reported IN COMMON were worth more than every per frame note combined,
because each was a chassis defect that would have shipped into every future deck.

They were also wrong three times, and the run said so in writing rather than
quietly declining. Moving slide 01's dial to its planned position would have
cost a round to fix a plan rather than a render. Resetting slide 04's window
would have broken that frame's own acceptance test. And drawing slide 07's
uncertainty as CV times the value would have put a mark on the frame that its
own printed disclosure does not describe.

A critic's finding is evidence, not an instruction. The run owes each one a
measurement and a written answer, and owes exactly nothing to the ones that turn
out to be wrong.

## THE ONE THING TO CARRY

A gate that measures a declared quantity cannot see a broken promise about
colour. This deck's entire argument was a colour law and it was broken in three
places behind a clean gate table. Count the pixels.
