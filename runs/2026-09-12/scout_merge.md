### 1. SELECTED. The Navy's under-ice navigation grid is permitted, and the permit is a licence to make noise in subsistence water

Found independently by Beat C and Beat E, which is the strongest signal the sweep
produced. Both read the primary Federal Register documents.

On September 10th NMFS published notice that it has **ISSUED** an incidental
harassment authorization to the Office of Naval Research for Year 9 of its Arctic
Research Activities, effective **September 14th, 2026 through September 13th,
2027**. The action line reads "Notice; issuance of incidental harassment
authorization", so this is DECIDED and not proposed. Document 2026-18459, 91 FR
57547, RTID 0648-XF889.

The activity is the Arctic Mobile Observing System, and what it is building is an
acoustic navigation network so autonomous vehicles can hold a position under sea
ice, where GPS does not reach. Platform inventory, from the July 22nd proposed
notice (2026-14816, 91 FR 46055) that the issuance incorporates: up to six fixed
acoustic navigation moorings at 900 to 950 Hz, two drifting Ice Gateway Buoys, one
REMUS 600 uncrewed undersea vehicle carrying an 8 to 14 kHz modem, sea gliders,
and up to 20 Argo-type autonomous profiling floats. The 900 Hz moorings transmit a
30-second pulse every four hours. Not-to-exceed source levels by system design
limit are 180 dB at 900 Hz, 185 dB at 10 kHz, 190 dB for the 35 Hz VLF sources.

Area, **639,237 square kilometres** of the US EEZ in the Beaufort and eastern
Chukchi, closest approach **204 kilometres** from the Alaska coast, partially high
seas. First of up to two cruises is **22 days** beginning **September 22nd**, aboard
R/V Sikuliaq or USCGC Healy.

The take: **Level B harassment only**, of Beaufort Sea and Eastern Chukchi beluga
and of ESA-listed Arctic ringed seal. Level A harassment is "neither anticipated
nor authorized". Beat C carried abundance estimates from the authorization
analysis, 39,258 for the Beaufort stock at CV 0.229 and 13,305 for Eastern Chukchi
at CV 0.51. Beat E could not extract the per-species authorized take table and
says plainly not to assert take numbers. THE FACT-CHECKER SETTLES THAT.

WHY IT WINS. It is decided, it is this week, it is on two primary federal
documents, its Alaska stake is a subsistence species in water Iñupiat hunters use,
the ship is one the University of Alaska Fairbanks operates, and the cruise leaves
in ten days. It is also the best art brief in the sweep by a distance, and the
one that breaks this house's month of paper metaphors.

## ROUTES, for the next run

Every scout independently hit federalregister.gov's HTML pages 302-redirecting a
bot to unblock.federalregister.gov, and all three found the same two ways around
it. Use them and stop rediscovering them.
- `https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[term]=...&conditions[publication_date][gte]=YYYY-MM-DD` for search and metadata, and `conditions[comment_date][gte]` for open windows.
- Full text at `https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/YYYY/MM/DD/<docnum>.txt` or at `https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-YYYY-MM-DD/html/<docnum>.htm`.

`https://alaskabeacon.com/feed/` carries FULL article text and answers a bot,
which is the route into a host sources.yaml had listed as reachable only through
alaskapublic.org. Verified independently and already written into sources.yaml
with its two limits, that it holds only the newest handful of items and can carry
a collective byline where the page names an author.

`https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?awardeeStateCode=AK` is keyless and
returns every Alaska award with amount, PI and abstract. The HTML award pages are
JavaScript-only and come back empty.

`https://egov.kpbsd.org/agenda/<id>` serves full board agendas with verbatim item
numbers and action labels; the BoardDocs mirror 403s.
