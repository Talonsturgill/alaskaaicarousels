# SELECTION — carousel No. 40 — 2026-08-25

## THE STORY

**Anchorage votes September 1st on whether to write surveillance rules into city
code, while the surveillance system is still about 2 percent built.**

The Assembly holds a public hearing that day on two linked items. One is roughly
a $600,000 three-year equipment contract with ThunderCat Technology to build out
the Anchorage Police Department's Real-Time Crime Center on Elmore Road, paid
from federal and state fine and forfeiture funds. The other is an ordinance by
Keith McCormick, Daniel Volland and Sydney Scout that would put four rules into
Anchorage code before the buildout finishes. A case or incident number and a
specific justification for every search and every live monitoring session.
Automatic deletion of surveillance data after two weeks unless lawfully
preserved for an active case. Plate readers restricted to specific public safety
investigations and barred from parking and code enforcement. And Assembly
approval before adding new surveillance capabilities, in the co-sponsor's own
words, "including AI-powered tracking or identification technology."

The system is designed to stream up to 750 camera feeds. It opened in September
2025 with about 15 cameras and one employee.

## WHY THIS ONE

Judged on the four criteria in order.

**1. Concrete Alaska impact.** Anchorage is roughly 40 percent of the state's
population and this decides the privacy floor for all of them. It is a real vote
on a real calendar, seven days out, and an Alaskan can testify at it. Compare
the runners-up, which are all things that have already happened to Alaskans or
are being decided somewhere else.

**2. Visual potential.** This is the criterion that settled it. The story hands
the artwork a COUNT and a STATE, which is the rarest gift a story gives this
deck. 750 designed feeds against about 15 running. More than 250 city traffic
cameras, roughly 216 privately registered ones, plus drones, body cameras and
plate readers. A retention clock at 14 days and a 3 to 5 day overwrite against a
proposed two weeks. Every one of those is drawable as a measured quantity rather
than as an illustration of a quantity, and section 3 below turns that into the
run's attack on its standing weakness.

**3. Tangibility.** A room on Elmore Road. Cameras on poles people drive past.
216 doorbell cameras that neighbours volunteered. The most abstract thing in the
story is the word "ordinance" and even that resolves to a hearing with a date.

**4. Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and specifically to one who
lives in Anchorage and has not heard that this is happening. Three local outlets
covered it inside eight days and a sponsoring Assembly member wrote an op-ed the
same day as the news coverage, which is what recruiting testimony looks like.
The audience for this deck is exactly the audience that ordinance sponsor is
trying to reach.

**Corroboration.** THREE beats converged on it. E and F returned it as their top
finding and C as its second, all working different briefs, all with matching
figures. That is the strongest signal this process produces and it has not
happened before in this series. The showrunner then re-verified all of it
directly against the three sources rather than taking any scout's word.

Beat C also adds the one detail the others missed and it sharpens the thesis.
The 750 feeds include DRONE-MOUNTED cameras and officer-worn cameras alongside
the traffic poles, so what is being wired together is not a camera network, it
is a fusion console. That is the thing a rule is being written about.

## RUNNER-UP, and why it lost

**Alaska's hydro fleet against one campus ask.** Governor Dunleavy's Alaska

## THE LATE COMPETITOR, and the honest reason it lost

**Rainmaker's drone cloud seeding over the Kenai Peninsula.** Beat C's top
finding and the freshest thing any scout returned. A venture-funded El Segundo
company published two documents on August 24th reporting that on the morning of
August 23rd it flew two sub-55 pound autonomous drones over the Chugach and Kenai
coastal ranges, 12 flights across 7 seeding missions in about three hours,
releasing 374.3 grams of silver iodide from 19 flares, and claims 57.6 acre-feet
of seeded precipitation, which it frames as roughly 19 million gallons. No
permit, regulator, Alaska agency, borough, tribe or customer is named anywhere in
either document.

The Alaska hole in that story is real and it is a good one. A three-hour
precipitation intervention happened inside state borders over watersheds that
feed Bradley Lake, Cook Inlet salmon systems and Southcentral water, and the
state has no visible role in it.

**It lost on evidence, not on interest.** Every figure is the company's own,
published by the company, two days ago, with no independent verification of any
kind. The claims gate would let this deck say "the company reports 57.6
acre-feet" and would not let it say 57.6 acre-feet happened. A nine-slide deck in
which every number on every slide carries "the company says" is a weaker deck
than one built on figures three outlets and a sponsoring legislator all
corroborate, and the honest version of the Rainmaker deck is mostly a deck about
not being able to check something. That is a legitimate story and it is not this
run's, because this run has a better one that is also more useful to a reader
this week.

**Flagged as a live lead for the next run**, by which point independent reporting,
a borough response or a DEC statement may exist and turn it into a checkable
story. Deliberately NOT written into `prompts/NEXT_RUN.md`, because that file is
a maintainer directive that overrides a run's own selection and it is not this
run's place to write one.

## THE THESIS

**A rule is cheap to write while the machine is still small, and the machine does
not stay small.**

Anchorage is doing the unusual thing here and the deck should say why it is
unusual. The normal sequence is that a capability arrives, then gets used, then
gets argued about, then maybe gets governed. This ordinance is trying to run that
sequence backwards, on a system that is funded for 750 feeds and is running about
15. The deck is not an argument that the crime center is bad. It is an argument
about WHEN a rule can still be written, and about the fact that the answer is
almost always "earlier than this" and for once is "now".

## THE ACCURACY TRAP, carried into every phase

The three sources word the AI provision differently and the deck must print the
defensible one.

- The sponsor's op-ed, which is the sponsor describing their own ordinance, says
  Assembly approval is required "before adding new surveillance capabilities,
  including AI-powered tracking or identification technology." **This is the
  wording the deck uses.**
- Alaska Public Media reports it as the crime center's use of AI needing Assembly
  approval, and says the ordinance carries no specific facial-recognition or
  human-feature-recognition provision.
- ADN is the only account that mentions human-feature recognition.

**Do not print "human-feature recognition" as the ordinance's text.** Separately,
the Real-Time Crime Center is not currently an AI identification system. Chief
Sean Case says APD uses object recognition only, for plate reading, and has never
tested facial recognition, which Anchorage banned in 2023. Any slide implying the
system already runs AI identification is a hard fail and must be killed on sight.

The fact-checker was briefed on this specifically and its ruling governs.

## PHASE 5 — THE DIRECTORS ROOM, and why the synthesis went this way

Three lenses pitched: DATA-JOURNALIST, SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, HISTORIAN-OF-THE-FUTURE.
The full reasoning is at the head of `out/2026-08-25/storyboard.md`. In short.

**THE 750 APRON (data-journalist) wins the chassis.** It is the only treatment
whose hero structure IS the story's own count, so the field-is-subject mandate
and the argument are the same object. It also produced the best single device of
the three, a colour key where gold means already true and blue means only
proposed, which converts this deck's number one hard-fail risk into something a
pixel critic can call from the render alone.

**GRAFTED FROM THE HISTORIAN.** The phantom law, that no slide may draw an
unrealized state in solid ink, which restates the colour key in a second
independent channel that survives a 432px thumb where hue may not. Also the
placement of the accuracy slide EARLY, at 04, so it protects the deck rather than
apologising at the end. Also Scout's own sentence as the breather, which is the
warmth the winning director's own self-critique said its deck lacked.

**GRAFTED FROM THE SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR.** The undrilled boss. A capability the
system does not have is drawn as a VOID, never as a dark version of a lit thing,
which makes the worst available error, implying live AI identification, something
the deck's drawing vocabulary cannot express.

**REJECTED, with reasons.** The ORDINANCE MANIFOLD was the most beautiful pitch
and it is a metaphor. An anodized signal plant is not Anchorage, it carries one
literal anchor across nine slides, and DESIGN_DOCTRINE 6.4 sets the floor at one
literal anchor plus one annotation per composition. The SEPTEMBER SURFACE's
haze-is-data idea, where fog volume equals ungoverned volume, is genuinely
elegant and was cut on build risk rather than on merit.

**THE WINNING DIRECTOR'S SELF-CRITIQUE WAS ACTED ON.** It named its own slide 02,
a stepped date terrace, as the weakest drawing in the pitch and sitting in the
one slot that must not be weak. Slide 02 is replaced by THE LAUNCH STATE, a 1:1
read of what actually opened in September 2025, and the calendar moves to 08
where it lands the ask. One employee in a room is a person; 750 is not.
