# SELECTION — 2026-08-03 — Carousel No. 24

## THE STORY

**Graphite Creek, Brevig Mission, and the two clocks.**

On July 20, 2026 an executive order titled "Securing America's Defense
Supply Chains and Ensuring Domestic Acquisition of Critical Materials"
raised the national demand signal for domestic critical materials. Two
days later Graphite One published a release aligning its proposed Graphite
Creek project with that order, and that release names artificial
intelligence infrastructure among the end markets its graphite is meant to
feed. Six days after that, about 80 residents of Brevig Mission, a village
of roughly 400 people an hour by boat from the deposit, filled the
community building and said no. And the Army Corps of Engineers has
escalated the federal review from an Environmental Assessment to a full
Environmental Impact Statement, now targeted for September 2027.

## WHY THIS ONE, AGAINST THE FOUR CRITERIA

**1. Concrete Alaska impact.** A named deposit, a named village, a named
federal decision with a date on it, and an Alaska Native corporation with
$2 million and an advisory board seat on the other side of the table.
Nothing here is a trend piece.

**2. Visual potential.** The strongest in the sweep, and the only candidate
whose geometry, quantity and place are all literally drawable. A 1.1-mile
pit on the Seward Peninsula is a true-scale object you can put next to a
village. The terrain is real and mappable. And the deck's central tension
is a TIMELINE mismatch, which is geometry.

**3. Tangibility.** An hour by boat is a unit every Alaskan understands.

**4. Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and to a legislator.
This is the consent question the whole Alaska AI build-out keeps running
into, in its clearest instance yet, and it is not the data-center version
everyone has already argued about.

## THE THESIS

The AI build-out is usually argued in Alaska as a power question, megawatts
and gas and transmission. This is the other half of the bill, and it comes
due somewhere else. The materials the build-out needs come out of ground
somebody lives next to, and the demand signal and the consent process run
on two different clocks. The executive order moved in one day. The company
answered in two. The village answered in eight. The Corps just added a
year. That is the deck.

Position taken, per the house voice: the mismatch is the story, and neither
speed nor delay is automatically the right answer. A year of EIS is not
obstruction and a July executive order is not consent. What the deck argues
is that the clocks are not the same clock, and that Alaska keeps being
handed the fast one.

## HONESTY CONSTRAINTS BUILT INTO THE PLAN

- The AI link is the COMPANY'S OWN WORDS, not our inference. That is the
  whole reason this story beat the NTSB story. If the fact-checker cannot
  produce that sentence verbatim, the deck's frame changes and the AI hook
  goes with it. This is the one claim the deck can't ship without.
- A press release is a primary source for what a company SAYS and is not
  evidence the thing is true. Every slide drawing on the Graphite One
  release carries that distinction.
- An EXIM Letter of Interest is not committed financing. If the $2.07
  billion figure ships, it ships with that word.
- Brevig Mission's meeting is not a referendum and must not be drawn as
  one. About 80 people at a meeting in a village of about 400 is what it
  is, and the deck says exactly that.
- Bering Straits Native Corporation's $2 million investment means Alaska
  Native institutions are not on one side of this. Any deck that flattens
  that is dishonest, and the slide that carries it is the deck's fairness
  beat.
