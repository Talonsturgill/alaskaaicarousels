## The story
**A Google AI Overview gave a Kodiak hunter Alaska's majority answer, and Kodiak's law is written by place.**

On September 5th a Kodiak hunter searched "Snipe season", read that snipe season began September 1st, took three snipe at Bull Lake in Pasagshak, then self-reported when duck season's October 8th opener made her doubt it. Trooper Alex Wick's sworn affidavit (Kodiak District Court, 3KO-26-00162CR, filed September 18th) records his own search and the AI Overview's exact words: snipe season "generally from September 1st through December 16th (or matching general migratory bird/waterfowl windows depending on the specific zone...)". That window is correct for the Gulf Coast and Northern zones. Kodiak (Unit 8) and the Pribilof/Aleutian zone open October 8th. She pleaded no contest and paid $150. DPS: "This is the first time that I think we have seen AI cited."

Why it wins, by the four criteria in order:
1. **Concrete Alaska impact.** A real enforcement case, a real court record, a real regulation, and a statewide habit (Alaskans search before they hunt). DPS says it expects more. Every Alaskan who hunts, fishes or reads regulations by zone is the audience.
2. **Visual potential.** Geometry (five migratory bird zones, Kodiak archipelago and the Aleutian chain against the mainland), quantity (a 37-day shift between two equal-length calendars, three birds, $150), place (Bull Lake, Pasagshak, Kodiak Island). The AI's sentence itself is an object: the caveat is in parentheses.
3. **Tangibility.** One search, one date, one lake, one citation. No abstraction.
4. **Would an Alaskan send it to a coworker?** Yes. It is the first AI-cited hunting citation in the state and the mistake is an Alaska-shaped mistake: the answer was right for most of the map.

## Editorial decisions
- **The hunter is not named on any slide or in the caption.** She is a private individual cited for a misdemeanor she self-reported; the deck's argument is about the machine and the map, not about her. She is "a Kodiak hunter". The trooper and the DPS spokesman are public officials acting in role and are named. No date of birth, address or license detail is used anywhere, even though the court record prints them.
- **The AI's hedge is the honest centre of the deck.** The Overview did say "depending on the specific zone". The deck does not claim the AI lied; it shows that the caveat was in parentheses and the number was in the lead, and that the regulation book is organised the other way round (zone first, then dates).
- **Sources are honest about what they do not say.** No source says how often AI Overviews give wrong season dates, whether Google changed the answer since, or how many other hunters relied on it. Google did not respond to the Beacon. The deck says none of those things.
- **Not a scandal about Google.** It is a story about how Alaska's rules are written (by zone) versus how a summary is written (by majority).
