Alaska's coastlines, rivers and lakes get their depth read out of satellite imagery starting September 1st.

The air above them gets a model for the weather windows safe enough for a prescribed burn. The ground under them gets one that forecasts landslides. Coal refuse and ash get read for critical minerals. Concealed rock gets searched for ore nobody has found.

Six National Science Foundation awards to University of Alaska campuses obligate $18,647,929 down that column.

Each award's obligated dollars beside its end date, what the five models read, and the fifteen UAF headlines from August 9th to August 21st that name none of the six.

Erin Trochim's water award ends August 31st, 2027. The rock award runs to July 31st, 2036. Who pays to keep a model running after its award ends?
