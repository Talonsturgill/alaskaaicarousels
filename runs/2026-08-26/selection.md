## WHY THIS ONE

1. **Strongest concrete Alaska impact.** These are Alaska institutions, Alaska
   principal investigators, and five Alaska physical systems. Not a national
   story with an Alaska sticker: the awardee field on every record reads
   University of Alaska.
2. **Visual potential is the best of the six beats.** A fan of five horizons all
   starting at one line, and five real Alaska surfaces to draw at true
   projection. Geometry, quantity and place in one thesis.
3. **Tangibility.** September 1st is six days out. That is a date a reader can
   hold.
4. **Would an Alaskan send this to a coworker?** Yes, and specifically to a
   coworker at the university, at a borough, or at a mine. It is the rare AI
   story here that is about Alaskans being paid to build the thing rather than
   about a company arriving to take something.

And the reason it is worth a deck rather than a paragraph: **three scouts found
it independently on three different beats, working from a keyless public API
that anyone could query.** What can be said about the coverage is narrower and
has to stay narrow, per the fact-checker. UAF's own news index listed fifteen
headlines between August 9th and August 21st and none of them names any of these
six awards (C46), while the same index carried an unrelated AI headline on
August 11th about rural power grids (C47). So the quiet is specific to these
awards rather than a blanket silence, and the deck says exactly that and no
more.

## THE SHOWRUNNER'S OWN ERROR, CAUGHT BY THE FACT-CHECKER

Recorded here in full because it is the most useful thing that happened in this
phase, and because the run nearly published it.

A scout reported a fifth AI award, FIRE-WUI, a $1,588,147 fire weather toolkit
for Alaska. The showrunner fetched award 2536745, ran a regex over the abstract
for AI, artificial intelligence, machine learning, neural, deep learning,
digital twin, surrogate and reinforcement learning, got zero hits, and killed
the award. That kill was written into this file and into the fact-checker's
brief as a claim to independently confirm, with the deck intending to print the
exclusion as its honesty beat.

The fact-checker reversed it, loudly and correctly. **The abstract writes
"machine-learning" hyphenated**, so a search for the two-word form misses it,
and the sentence is right there: "Using historical weather reanalysis data and
statistical and machine-learning techniques, the research team will characterize
the geographic and seasonal patterns of hazardous fire weather". Worse for the
kill, NSF itself files the award under its **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**
program and stamps it with progRefCode 075Z, the same AI reference code carried
by three of the other awards. The bathymetry award does not even carry 075Z.

So the award is folded back in. The deck is FIVE projects across SIX awards and
**$18,647,929** obligated, not four and $17,059,782.

Two things follow, and both bind the rest of the run:

1. **The deck may not print the exclusion in any form.** There is no honesty
   beat about an award that was examined and cut, because no award was cut. Any
   sentence to that effect would be false on a fetchable primary document.
2. **A tool that answers "no" is not the same as a fact.** A regex over one
   field is a measurement of the regex as much as of the abstract, and the
   hyphen is exactly the kind of thing that makes it lie. The lesson goes to
   FIELD_NOTES and to Phase 12, because the machine now has a documented case
   where the showrunner's own verification step produced a confident false
   negative that only an adversarial second reader caught.

## VARIETY, AND THE GENRE THE LEDGER IS ACTUALLY STUCK IN

The last four decks are one genre wearing four props: a manufactured object or
surface, staged under one modelled key light, raking across a dark ground. 37 a
press plate, 38 a floor, 39 a cradle, 40 an apron. Every field-level divergence
rule was satisfied every time and the genre never moved, which is the most
plausible reason artwork craft has been the weakest criterion in 9 of the last
10 runs at a mean of 6.65.

This story hands the run the way out. There is no object to stage. The subject
is four Alaska SURFACES, and a surface has no background to leave empty, which
is the direct structural answer to No. 40's unspent one-sentence fix about
frames where the field simply stops.

Divergence is therefore declared at genre level: **no staged manufactured
object, no single modelled key lamp, no dark bench.** Real Alaska geography at
true projection, or a measured field, lit by the place rather than by a lamp.

## WHAT COULD STILL GO WRONG

- **The silence claim is the fragile one.** "No Alaska outlet reported it" is a
  universal negative and the fact-checker refused to certify it, correctly. What
  survives is C46, a narrow claim that the UAF news index for August 9th to 21st
  listed fifteen headlines and none of them names any of these six awards. C47
  tempers it further: UAF DID publish an AI headline on August 11th, about rural
  power grids, so the quiet is specific to these awards rather than a blanket
  silence. The deck must carry the narrow claim and never the wide one.
- **The Engine's two numbers.** $15,000,000 is an estimated total on a ten year
  cooperative agreement and $10,500,000 is obligated. A slide that prints the
  first as money in hand is wrong, and the money total uses the second.
- **The start-date alignment is not a conspiracy.** Federal fiscal and academic
  calendars produce August 1st and September 1st routinely. The deck states the
  alignment and never implies coordination.
- **The fire season date is unverified.** C48, that Alaska's fire season ends
  August 31st, came from a scout and the fact-checker did not re-fetch it. It is
  a lovely juxtaposition with a September 1st start and it may not ship until
  someone fetches the page.
