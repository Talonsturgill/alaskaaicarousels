### The ONE weakness this run attacks, and how

**Artwork craft and genuine detail.** Weakest in 8 of the last 10 runs, mean 6.7,
and it has never once been the criterion a run designed around at plan time. It
gets attacked at Phase 9, in repair passes, which is why it keeps coming back.

Read the three most recent scorer notes together and they are one defect, not
three:

- No.41 (6.0): pools widened and casts deepened until every marker sat in a
  spotlight beside a detached black hole.
- No.42 (6.0, fix): "Stop buying contrast with opaque rectangles. Design each
  slide's type reserve INTO the art at dossier time as a real sky, shadowed
  slope or snowfield the words sit inside."
- No.43 (6.5, fix): a resolution beat floating detached in the dead middle,
  "the reason its bottom third reads top-loaded."

And the machine defect table says the same thing from the other side. Top-loaded
composition in 4 of 10 runs. Busy art under text in 3. Both are the symptom of
one habit, which is composing the picture first and then finding somewhere to
put the words.

**THE DESIGN LAW FOR THIS DECK, decided here and binding on every Phase 5
dossier:** the type reserve is a MODELLED SURFACE, and it is at the BOTTOM.

Every slide is built around a real, lit, near-field surface occupying the lower
third to lower half of the frame, in the world of the story, on which the
headline and body physically sit. Not a plate. Not a scrim. Not a hairline. A
surface with a light gradient across it, its own material texture, its own
contact with whatever stands on it, and its own perspective. The art mass lives
ABOVE and BEHIND it and recedes into atmosphere. The words sit on the near
plane, where a reader's eye is closest and the tone is calmest by construction.

That single decision pays four of the standing defects at once:
- top-loaded composition cannot happen, because the near surface IS the bottom
  third and it carries modeled tone by definition (dossier field 4a is satisfied
  by the composition rather than annotated onto it);
- busy art under text cannot happen, because the surface under the type is the
  quietest region in the frame on purpose;
- no opaque plate is ever needed, so the No.42 fix is structural rather than a
  note;
- contact shadows get a LIT GROUND to subtract from, which is the exact cure
  FIELD_NOTES names for the 2-run contact-shadow warn.

Second half of the attack, on "genuine detail" rather than composition. The
rubric's 10 says the hero climbs the rendered ladder. This deck puts a real
akthree GPU PBR hero on the cover with an akpost film grade and a designed
Canvas fallback, and gives at least two more slides genuine near-field material
detail at 100 percent zoom (tooth, grain, edge wear, tool marks on the surface
the type sits on). Line-weight hierarchy is declared per slide as 3 distinct
weights with stated meaning, so "uniform line weights" cannot pass unnoticed.

Third: the contact-shadow pairs are MEASURED with `scripts/contact_probe.py`
off a real render, never computed off camera arithmetic, and the probe's own
output is pasted into `data-contacts`. The floor is not a target. If a contact
reads, stop.
