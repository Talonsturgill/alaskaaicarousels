# STORYBOARD — Alaska.Ai Carousel No. 44 — 2026-08-29

## THE DIRECTORS ROOM, AND THE SYNTHESIS

Three treatments came back, all buildable, none shippable as-is.

**A, CINEMATOGRAPHER, THE LEE SLOPE.** A dolly along the Chukchi bed, nine
stations, one refracted sun. Genuinely beautiful, strong rendered ladder, and
the mid-key green broke the palette run. Rejected as chassis on its own second
self-critique, which was honest and correct: the milled slots cut into the
seafloor are an invented object inside a photoreal scene, so the deck's most
literal claim would rest on its least literal object. Its underwater camera also
sits closer to No.42's SOUNDING COLUMN than its defense admits.

**B, SYSTEMS-ILLUSTRATOR, THE NARROWING BENCH.** Refuses the water entirely and
stays in a shop under a tungsten lamp, on the grounds that the decisive act
happened ashore. The thesis, drawn. Rejected as chassis because its own critique
is right that the Alaska anchor thins to one breather slide, and because a warm
tungsten interior spends the palette divergence on a register the story does not
need.

**C, DATA-JOURNALIST, THE UNDERLIT BENCH.** WINNER AS CHASSIS.

The reason is structural rather than aesthetic. Every other answer to this run's
design law is a discipline the build has to keep. This one is a geometry the
build cannot break. The key light is a diffuser panel UNDER the reading surface,
so the lower 43 percent of every frame is the brightest, calmest and most
materially detailed region in the picture, the art mass sits above and behind it
in a dark room, and top-loaded composition is not a defect the deck has to avoid,
it is a shape the deck cannot make. Busy art under text is impossible for the
same reason, because the ground under the words is polished glass carrying one
smooth ramp. And it hands the contact-shadow gate a genuinely bright ground to
subtract from, which is exactly the cure FIELD_NOTES prescribes for a warn that
has now shipped in two of ten runs.

It also diverges from the last three decks on the axis that actually matters. All
three were dark-ground, low-sun or near-black registers. This one inverts the
value structure: dark room, lit ground, dark ink.

### Organs transplanted from the others

- **From B, THE NARROWING.** The gate slide takes B's discipline that an
  aperture's SHAPE encodes its rule and never its magnitude, because no source
  gives the fraction lost at any stage and inventing one would be the exact
  dishonesty this deck argues against. B's swarf tray, where the discarded ocean
  falls off the reader's own frame edge, is kept.
- **From A, THE OBJECT HERO.** The glider itself appears once, on the cover, as
  a real akthree PBR render lying in a foam cradle at the back of the bench. C's
  deck never showed the machine and that is a real loss on a page whose first
  criterion is tangibility. A's attenuated-ochre physics is irrelevant in air and
  is dropped; the hull is painted composite under a cool rim.
- **From A, THE SPECULAR BUDGET.** Exactly three hard speculars deck-wide.
  No.42 shipped nine slides without one.

### And one place the showrunner overrules all three

**The Alaska stake moves to slide 03.** C put it at 09 of 10 and named the
problem itself, that four consecutive specification slides sit before it. B put
it at 05 and called its own anchor thin. Story arc and swipe momentum is a
repeat weakness on this page and was No.43's weakest criterion at 6.0.

So the deck is NINE slides, C's pipe beat folds into the gate slide as a footer
strip, and "three of these columns are food, one is Air gun" lands at slide 03,
where it gives every specification slide after it a reason to be read.

### Slide count rationale

**Nine.** One cover, seven content, one close. Inside the 8 to 10 default. The
argument has three escalating beats (the list is closed, the list disagrees with
itself, so the humans write outside it), and each needs its own slide. Between

stake; folding the pipe beat into the gate slide removes it. Eight would cost the
breather, and the deck runs four dense slides before the turn.
