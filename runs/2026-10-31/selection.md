## Decision
THE FCC'S OCTOBER 20TH ORDER (DA 26-1124) LETTING GCI BUY QUINTILLION, the 1,176-mile subsea fiber along Alaska's Arctic coast (Prudhoe Bay, Utqiagvik, Wainwright, Point Hope, Kotzebue, Nome; the Nome to Homer Express adding Emmonak, Hooper Bay and Naknek). ACS told the FCC the two are "the only providers of middle mile transport for substantial portions of Western and Northern Alaska, respectively." The FCC approved anyway, with price conditions, resting its competition finding on Starlink while acknowledging "disagreement in the record as to whether Starlink is currently providing wholesale middle mile transport service in Alaska" (C18) and deciding "regardless of whether" (C19).

Compute thread, stated honestly: the order never mentions AI or data centers (C37). The cable lands at Prudhoe Bay (C04), where North Slope Power plans a 3 GW gas plant "designed to power colocated AI data centers" (C41 to C43). Whatever runs compute on the North Slope or in the Arctic villages buys its long-haul bits from this one system, now inside GCI. The deck uses that as context on one frame, never as the order's subject.

Why it wins on the criteria: (1) concrete Alaska impact, 13 named communities on the rural network with wholesale prices frozen by condition (C24 to C35), a seven to nine month 2025 outage (C21, C22, C39); (2) visual potential, a line along a real coast on real seabed (bathymetry is in the committed DEMs), a capacity contrast (10 Tbps per pair vs 100 Gbps gateways), conditions with hard numbers; (3) tangible, the price a school district or clinic pays for bandwidth; (4) an Alaskan in telecom, a tribal IT office, a school district would forward it. Forward date: petitions for reconsideration within thirty days of October 20th (C36).
