What this screen will never do

We ran six candidate builds down the do you even need AI ladder for you and every one of them bottomed out at plain rules. So the absences below are the design, not gaps in the demonstration. Look for them while you click.

  • It never proposes a vessel. When a count drops it will show you 71 guests on a 118 seat hull and then say nothing at all.
  • It never assigns or suggests crew. Crew sits under Coast Guard manning, credentialing and rest rules we could not source from public pages, so it is out of version 1 entirely.
  • It never writes a word. Every notice is one fixed template with your numbers merged in. You can read the template itself in scenario 2.
  • It never predicts a count. It records the number a person typed and where that person got it, and it labels the source it does not know.
  • It never decides. Nothing leaves the desk until a person presses record. There is no queue of pending robot actions anywhere in this build.
There is no AI in this build. We are an AI shop, and that is still the recommendation. A notice that commits a caterer to a head count should be identical every time so a dock reads it in two seconds, and a fixed template can be tested exhaustively in a way a model cannot.

The Change Desk. Typed fields only, no free text anywhere

She is not asking the software what to do. She already decided. This form is where the decision she made gets recorded as a structured event instead of spoken into four phones. Change any field you like and watch what it does to the notice on the next step.

Reason is a closed list and Other is a code, not a text box. Removing free text is the whole reason this design needs no model to read it back. If Other keeps coming back through one season, that is a finding, and we add a code rather than a language model.

Who has actually read it

The notices from the 0912 change went out. Today, we assume, finding out whether the caterer saw one means calling the caterer. Here every notice carries a single use link with three answers on it and no login. Play the vendors below and watch the board. Advance the clock to see what happens to the ones who say nothing.

Three answers and no login, because a coach dispatcher will not create an account for you. Confirm, cannot do, or a number. A number is there because the coach company decides how many coaches it runs, not us. The notice carries the head count and asks. It never tells a vendor what to do about it, because that is their business and not ours to automate.
The telephone stays in the design on purpose. Email first, one SMS escalation on silence, then a human calls. When she calls, she logs a manual acknowledgement here so the record stays complete. We are not replacing the phone and the marine radio. We are making sure the day they carried gets written down.

What the day counted

How it sits in your operation

Three steps, and a person on the trigger at both ends

Step one
She keeps deciding

The Juneau operations specialist makes exactly the call she makes today. The Change Desk is where she types the decision she already made, into fields instead of into four phones. The software has no opinion and no draft waiting for her approval, so there is nothing to rubber stamp on hour twelve of the day your posting describes.

Step two
One action reaches everyone

She presses record once and the same fixed notice goes to every party that change touches, on the channel they already use. Acknowledgements come back onto the board on their own. Silence turns red instead of turning into a phone call she has to remember to make.

Step three
The day becomes a record

Every observation, change, notice and acknowledgement lands in an append only log you own. At season close you hold the first count of operating day change this business has ever had, and the winter build gets funded or refused on that count instead of on anyone's opinion, ours included.

The human on the trigger is not a policy we wrote on a slide. It is the architecture. There is no path in this system from an incoming number to an outgoing commitment that does not pass through a person pressing a button. That is why version 1 needs no approval queue, no confidence threshold and no model.

Before you forward this

What this demonstration is not

  • It is scripted, and a real Juneau day is messier than six sailings and five parties. We built the shape from your own job postings, not from watching a dock. Three shadow days on the Auke Bay dock are in the ask precisely because we would rather design from a real day than from a posting.
  • Every number in the tally comes from the invented day above. Your real figure today is zero, because no count of operating day change exists anywhere. Producing your first real one is the entire first engagement, and we deliberately did not tune the sample day to clear our own gate.
  • We do not know what system carries your cruise line contracted manifests, which is your primary line. That is why the intake here is a person typing a number, and why four rows on the board say source system unknown. That single answer is the biggest swing in our whole estimate.
  • Party names are placeholders. We have no list of your Juneau motorcoach companies or caterers and we were not going to guess at one. The lodge galley is a sample party, included because catering sits downstream of the same head count.
  • Hull capacities are invented inside the 24 to 140 passenger range you publish. No per hull capacity table exists anywhere public.
  • The digest strings in the log are a short non cryptographic stand in so you can watch the chain move. The real thing is a hash chain sealed nightly to write once storage.
  • The churn may turn out to be small. If Juneau sailings rarely change once a day is set, the premise behind this whole screen is wrong, the slice tells you that for the price of a slice, and we recommend you do not build.
