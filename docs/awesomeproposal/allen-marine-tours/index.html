Family and Alaska Native owned, founded in Sitka in 1970 by Bob and Betty Allen, now owned and operated by their grandson Jamey Cagle. Monitor updates to passenger counts and modify vessel tour schedules as needed. Make changes to crew schedules and assign relief crew as needed.

Why a change is realistic

Push is visible in the posting itself, which describes coordinating vessels, motorcoaches and passengers by radio and phone across a single dock. Pull is legible, one shared plan with confirmed fan out is something an operator can picture in a sentence. Anxiety is real, crew and capacity work is regulated and a wrong automated decision is an incident rather than an inconvenience. Habit is the strongest force against us. Whoever runs this today runs it on channels that already work, and any tool that demands attention during the busy hour dies in week one. That balance is why the first version proposes nothing at all.

The recommended build

The Operating Day Ledger

One read only screen for a single Juneau operating day, showing every sailing, its current head count, its assigned vessel and its committed vendors. A typed form captures every change to that day as a structured event. An identical templated notice goes to every party the change touches, with a one tap acknowledgement so you can see who has read it without chasing anyone. Underneath sits an append only log that can replay any past day exactly as it happened, including the corrections. Its first job is not to optimize anything. Its first job is to count the churn, which nothing public shows anyone has counted, so the decision about whether to build anything larger gets made on your numbers instead of on anyone's opinion.

Where AI fits, and where it does not

We are an AI shop, and we ran six candidate builds for you down the do you even need AI ladder, two of which had a plausible AI component. Every single one bottomed out at plain rules. So there is no AI in this build at all, and that is the recommendation rather than an omission. We would not use AI to write the change notices, because a notice committing a caterer to a head count should be byte identical every time so a dock reads it in two seconds, and a template can be tested exhaustively in a way a model cannot. We would not let software assign crew, because that work sits under Coast Guard manning, credentialing and rest rules we could not source from public pages, so it is outside what this study can honestly establish. If a crew feature is ever built its only job is to refuse a combination that breaks a hard rule, and a refusal is a rule check rather than a recommendation. We are also declining to sell you the guest facing voice agent you probably expected us to pitch, for four separate reasons set out in the plan below. One more thing we would rather say than hide. Of everything we looked at, settlement and commission reconciliation looks like the surest return, and it contains no AI either. We are not putting it first for one reason only, which is perishability. An operating day can only be watched while the season is running, and your season closes 1 October. Reconciliation can be built in any month of the year. So the thing that expires goes first, and if you would rather start with reconciliation, say so and we will.

Build vs buy

We buy the commodity substrate, email delivery, SMS escalation, identity, managed Postgres, write once storage and monitoring, and we build the ledger core, the structured change capture, the byte identical notice templates and the no login acknowledgement tracker. On the build side, we looked for something off the shelf that models a day tour operating day and did not find one, and we did not run an exhaustive procurement scan. If you are already looking at a tool that does this, name it on the call and we will tell you straight whether it does, including if the answer makes this engagement smaller.

The honest base rate

The outside view first, and it applies to us. MIT's 2025 work found about 95 percent of enterprise generative AI pilots showed no measurable profit impact, a figure its own authors call directional with a debated denominator, and RAND puts AI project failure above 80 percent, roughly twice the rate of ordinary IT projects. Now the part most vendors skip. That research does not blame model quality. RAND's root causes are a misunderstood problem, missing or inadequate data, chasing technology rather than solving a problem, weak infrastructure, and problems too hard for current AI. This engagement carries several of those. Our own biggest risk, that the four datasets we would need are inferred from your job postings and may not exist in readable form, is squarely RAND's second cause. The honest part, what could make this not work

  • We do not know what system carries your cruise line contracted manifests. Your own Juneau Office Manager posting names FareHarbor and Viator as systems that role manages, and True Alaskan Tours books on FareHarbor, but nothing public tells us whether the contracted cruise line sailings run through either of them. If those manifests arrive by phone, radio and email, the integration work is larger than anything in our estimate, and you would hear that before you funded it.
  • We have no guest side evidence whatsoever. Every review source we tried was unreachable, so we have asserted nothing about guest complaints, hold times or refunds anywhere in this study, and we have not built against a pain we cannot show you.
  • Our read that contracted business limits your pricing lever is an inference from public pages, not a fact. It goes on the call as a question. If we are wrong, the opportunity is larger than we scoped it.
  • The calendar is genuinely tight and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Your season ends 1 October. About nine operating weeks remain. Miss that window and the next chance to observe a real operating day is late April 2027, a gap of about seven months that would force this whole case onto assumption instead of your own data.
  • A late season slice measures late season. We do not know where a late September slice sits against the season's peak, and we will not assume it is average. Any extrapolation across a season inherits that uncertainty. We will report the slice as the slice, state any extrapolation separately with its assumption named, and if it lands in shoulder season we will say so in the readout rather than annualise it quietly. The gate is measured on the slice as observed, not on the extrapolation.
