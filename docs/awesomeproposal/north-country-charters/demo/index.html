Step 5  ·  then it stops

No text is sent, and the message is still on the evening summary

Nobody's phone buzzes for this one. The guest already has the acknowledgement, the message sits in the inbox untouched, and it appears on the 11:00pm summary with everything else. A quiet result is the normal result.

Three of the six messages on this scripted day get a text. That share is invented, the same as the six. We picked it to show the range of cases, not because we think it is your rate. A real Tuesday may flag none, and if a real fortnight flags very few, the honest verdict is that you build nothing.

Step 4  ·  the one model call

Unclear is treated as yes, on purpose

The only model call in the whole build
The question, fixed and identical every time
Does this message say the guest is cancelling, may cancel, or may not show up for a trip they have booked? Answer yes or no, then give one short reason.
Answer
yes
Reason
The guest raises calling off Thursday because of the wind. It reads as a possible cancellation rather than a settled one.

A wrong yes costs one text message. A wrong no costs a seat nobody knew was free. Those two errors are not worth the same, so the question is written to lean toward yes when a message is unclear. You should expect texts about messages that turn out to be nothing.

Step 5  ·  2:09pm  ·  one text, then it stops

Kathy's phone

The text ends there. It suggests nothing, drafts nothing and books nothing. Kathy opens FareHarbor herself, sees which Thursday trip this is, and picks up the phone.

A weather day is bad news, and bad news is where we deliberately keep software out. A study of 467 people in tourism booking scenarios found that when a booking got worse, and the case they tested was a price increase, willingness to book dropped further if a chatbot delivered the news than if a person did. Weather is our extrapolation from that, not something the study tested. This build makes sure a person is the one having that conversation.

It sits in the roadmap as a later job, behind a read-only FareHarbor lookup that can actually check.

04    HOW IT SITS IN YOUR OPERATION

Three steps, with a person on every trigger

Week one has no AI in it

You read the reply wording and change anything you want changed. Kathy switches it on inside your own Gmail. Ten minutes of work, it answers every message from that morning on, and it stands on its own if you buy nothing else.

Access is read-only, through Google's own consent screen

One click to grant, one click to revoke, and no password shared with anybody. A forwarding rule works if you would rather grant nothing, and it costs you two things, it shows what arrives but never when someone answered, so there is no median-minutes number and no second text when a guest has been waiting. The watcher copies what arrives. It has no permission to change, delete, file or send anything in that mailbox.

Every decision stays with you

A text says a message reads like a cancellation and gives you the word the guest typed. Kathy opens FareHarbor, checks the real date and calls. No guest ever receives anything written by a model. Six texts a day maximum, across both phones.

05    THE EDGES

What this deliberately does not do

Each of these was considered and cut. A demonstration that quietly performs more than the study proposes is a demonstration that lies.

  • No phone, no voicemail, no call answering. This build never touches your line. Being reachable while a captain is on the water is a phone company purchase, not software.
  • No reading or writing in FareHarbor. A person opens it. Ten seconds, and the date that governs real money is checked by somebody who can see the actual booking.
  • No working out how close the trip is. Days to departure can't be derived from an email, so nothing here is stamped against your fourteen day or twenty four hour lines. A date on this page is a word a guest typed.
  • No reply written by a model, ever. The only thing a guest receives is the one fixed sentence you approved.
  • No marking read, no filing, no archiving, no hiding. The build is strictly additive.
  • No accuracy figure. No labelled set of your own messages exists yet, so any number printed here would be invented. The rate we assume, ninety in a hundred, is printed as an assumed row in the study's cost table and tested at gate two before anything larger is funded.
  • No claim about your volume, or about how much of it is cancellations. Six messages on a scripted Tuesday is a number we chose to make this page work, and so is the share of them that read as cancellations. A real Tuesday may produce none at all. What actually arrives is unknown to us, and finding out is the thing being sold.
