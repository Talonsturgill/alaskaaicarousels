How it sits in your operation

Three steps, and a person is on the trigger in two of them.

01

GovEagle stays the source of what a data call needs

The compliance matrix comes out of GovEagle. No page we read says it exports as a file, which is one of the three questions for your rep, and if it gives you only a screen this first step is a paste and nothing else changes. The tracker holds status against it and nothing else. It never edits the matrix, never copies an answer out of it, and never writes into a submitted volume.

02

Your proposal manager approves every list before anything sends

Rules propose the owners, a person approves the recipients, a person sets the chase cadence. The tracker has no way to mail somebody who isn't on an approved list.

03

The named owner still decides whether the answer is right

The board records who submitted what and when. There's no verified state in it, because whether a returned answer is correct is a judgement with somebody's name on it.

What this build deliberately doesn't do

  • No model runs in this path. Routing is a written rule you can read, test and repeat. Chaining the same routing through four judgement steps instead, each right nine times in ten, would hand you several silently wrong rows on every pursuit with nobody assigned to catch them. It doesn't draft prose, rewrite an answer, score a response or fill a gap. It counts what is outstanding and it says who is holding it.
  • There is no verified state. Submitted means a named person pressed submit. It never means the answer is correct or complete.
  • Nothing sends on its own. The recipient list is approved by a person and the chase clock is set by a person.
