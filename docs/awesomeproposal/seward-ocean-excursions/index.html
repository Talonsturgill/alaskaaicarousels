    01

    Staff paste the question in and get a draft with the matching published trip, price, group limit and conditions wording.

    Unknown today: Questions about which trip fits, what it costs and whether rain cancels arrive by email, phone or form and are answered by hand from the tour pages (volume unknown).

    Staff read and edit every draft before it is sent; nothing is sent automatically.
    02

    The draft quotes only those published prices and trip lengths.

    Observed workflow: The published tours list $209 half day, $419 seven hour, $2,866 private full day and $1,254 private evening trips.[2]

    The owner refreshes prices and policies before each season.
    03

    Drafts use that wording and never promise or cancel a sailing.

    The captain alone decides go or no-go on the day.
    04

    Those drafts are marked held, ask for date and party size, and wait for the owner.

    Unknown today: Private charters and winter questions need a call or a date check.

    The owner confirms date, price and availability.
    Build plan, alternatives, and dependencies

    Where AI earns its place

    Reads the pasted guest message, matches it to the published trips and drafts a reply from approved wording, flagging private and winter questions for the owner.

    The systems it needs

    First slice is a standalone workspace: paste a message, review the draft, copy the approved reply. It has no inbox or booking connection; live availability would be a separate scope.

    The human decision stays here

    Staff approve every reply. Private, winter and unclear questions are held, and the captain keeps every go or no-go call.

    Buying, configuring, or building

    Try FareHarbor Agent first, since the tour pages book through FareHarbor. Build only if the owner wants staff-approved wording in their own voice before anything is sent, which is what this adds.

    FareHarbor Agent

    Vendor-launched AI that answers guest questions, checks live availability and sends booking links using FareHarbor data. The tour pages book through FareHarbor, so Seward may already have access. Its price, access and whether answers can be held for approval in the owner's wording are unknown. Ask FareHarbor first; choose this build only if its answers can't be held for approval in the owner's words.

    Saved replies and a website FAQ

    Costs almost nothing and reuses the wording already published. Every message still needs staff to choose and adapt the right reply. Fine at low volume; the planner adds value when questions are varied.

    Research

    Why Seward Ocean Excursions

    Staff paste a guest message into one workspace and get a draft reply naming the matching published trip, price, six guest limit and rain versus unsafe seas wording. Private and winter questions wait for the owner.

    Boats are capped at six guests, trips run year round, routes follow conditions and private trips run $1,254 to $2,866, so many guests need a tailored answer before they book. Boat tour operators such as Paradise Watersports and Body Glove Hawaii use chat for that first answer.

    verifiedThe company is locally owned by Captains Bixler and Krystin McClure, a husband and wife team who settled in Seward.[4]

    verifiedPublished tours include a 3.5 hour Kenai Fjords and Resurrection Bay half day tour at $209 per person, a 7 hour Aialik Bay tour at $419 per person, an 8 hour private full-day trip at $2,866 for up to 6 people, and private evening tours at $1,254 for up to 6 people.[2]

    verifiedIts FAQ-style profile says rain alone is not grounds for cancellation, but trips may be called off if ocean conditions are genuinely unsafe, and private charters are booked by call or email.[4]

    verifiedGuests contact the company by email at info@sewardoceanexcursions.com or by phone at 1-907-599-0499, and the company says the route changes with conditions and wildlife sightings.[3][2]

    vendor reportedTOMIS reports that Paradise Watersports, a boat tour and charter operator, used an AI chatbot from Jan 1 to Sep 30, 2025; only 2.08% of visitors needed a human handoff and chatbot users converted 155% higher than the rest of the site.[5]
