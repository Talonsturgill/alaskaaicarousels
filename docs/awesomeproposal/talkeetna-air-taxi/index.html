Field study prepared for Talkeetna Air Taxi

Every booked guest is told to phone in on the day, and the page they would look at instead is gone.

Talkeetna, AlaskaAugust 8th, 2026

In short

We spent a day on Talkeetna Air Taxi from the outside, reading only what you publish, before asking you for anything.

Your flightseeing FAQ tells every customer the same thing, "please call us the day you are flying to inquire about weather". That is a published policy and it works, and it also means a season's worth of guests are pointed at the one number you publish. Your careers page describes who picks it up, one role checking in scenic passengers and climbers, taking payments, answering the phone and working the inbox.

Two things make that call harder than it needs to be. Three of your pages publish three different staffed-hours answers, and your flightseeing index shows "From $304 per Adult" for the Southside Explorer while the tour's own page shows $275. Separately, talkeetnaair.com/denali-webcam/ returns a redirect to your homepage and serves no camera, so a guest has nowhere on your site to look. K2 links a live camera at the top of its FAQ.

What we would build is deliberately small and has no AI in it. One record holding every fact a customer asks about, with every page rendering from it. One mobile page at that dead webcam address showing public FAA camera pictures, the current observation and the forecast, next to one current status your counter can flip that expires by itself back to your own sentence. No verdict and no prediction. That decision stays at your counter, with a person, where you put it.

On cost and return. Phase one is 24,000 dollars fixed, staged so you can stop after 9,500. Across five years the conservative case recovers 44 percent of total cost and never pays back. The middle case recovers 133 percent and pays back in month 45. Break-even sits at roughly one retained seat every other staffed day. We would rather print that than something friendlier.

Nobody has measured how many of those calls you take, so every volume figure below is an assumption we have labelled. We also could not confirm which FAA cameras see the routes you fly.

The ask is a week with a notepad before September 15th, counting those calls. If the number is small, do not buy the page.

01

Your own policy sends every guest to the phone, and the page that would answer them is dead

We read your published pages the way a customer would, then read your competitors' the same way.

Your flightseeing FAQ carries the instruction plainly. "please call us the day you are flying to inquire about weather". Read as a policy it is defensible, because Alaska Range weather moves faster than any page can and your briefing at check-in is where the judgement happens. You are also one of only four operators awarded a Denali glacier landing concession for 2020 to 2029, so demand is not the problem here. Read as a workload it is a season of guests pointed at the one number you publish.

The person who answers it is described on your own careers page as "Greeting and checking in scenic passengers and climbers, collecting payments, answering phone calls, taking reservations as well as responding to email inquiries, processing online reservations". The same page says "Customer Service is our #1 priority." Both are true at once.

04

What we would build has no AI in it, and that is the finding

We checked every place a model could have earned its keep here, and none of them needed one.

Two things. A record holding every fact a customer asks about, hours, fares, the glacier add-on, the park fee, the gear allowance, the base camp fee, the two taxes, the passenger minimums and the check-in window. A page at your dead webcam address showing camera pictures, the current observation, the forecast, and one status your counter sets.

Every page renders its facts from the record, so a change happens once, and the build fails if a fact goes six months without a review.

The page pulls public FAA camera frames and Weather Service data every few minutes and stamps the age on every picture. A frame that stops arriving shows its real age, then says the camera is unavailable. Whether a picture is any good is left to the person looking at it, because deciding that in software would need exactly the guessing this build refuses to do.

Eight pieces, no model in any of them. The collector copies pictures and timestamps and makes no judgement about either.

A working demonstration, built on your own facts. It is a demonstration and it is honest about that, and it does nothing this study did not scope. Open it full screen.

You do not need AI for this, and we would be selling you something worse if we said otherwise. Reconciling three published hours is a decision only you can make, and a model asked to do it would guess. Every fee on your price list is a published rule, so the arithmetic belongs in code where it is testable. Camera staleness is subtraction on two timestamps.

The go/no-go call is the one we most deliberately left alone. A 2025 benchmark asked models to estimate how far you can see, in miles, from a camera picture. Across 147,182 images from 444 FAA sites none reached the accuracy bar the paper measures against, the best averaging about 1.8 statute miles of error. MIT Lincoln Laboratory's system answers a narrower question, whether visibility is low or not, and on that question it was "more than 90 percent accurate" against the instruments beside the cameras. It is still shown "alongside the camera imagery itself" rather than instead of it. Neither one is a flyability verdict and nobody should sell you one.

There is one seat where AI would genuinely earn its place later, and it is not facing your guests. An assistant for the person at your counter, looking answers up in your own material and showing the page each came from. The best measurement we found, across 5,179 support agents, was 14 percent more issues resolved per hour and 34 percent for the newest workers, and a counter that re-staffs every April is exactly that population. It is blocked until the facts record has held through a winter and until real counter questions have been captured from a live season. We are not selling it now.

Hosting, scheduling, the sign-in for your counter's status control and the weather data are all bought or free, and we generate none of it.

We would also tell you the cheaper option out loud. K2 links out to a third-party camera from its FAQ and that costs a few minutes. What you give up is the labelling, the frame age and the attribution, which is most of what makes the page honest. If you do not want to fund the page, do that instead.

What would make us wrong

Four things, and we would rather raise them than have you find them.

The cameras might not see what matters. Nothing we read tells us which FAA sites sit near the Ruth, the Kahiltna, Tokositna West, the Root Canal or the Kichatna Spires, and a clear picture of the valley is not a clear picture of a mountain tens of miles away. That is one day of checking inside the first stage. If the honest answer is valley only, we say so and you take the record alone at a lower price.

The volume might not be there. Nobody has counted the calls this exists to reduce, so the whole return could be smaller than the build. The counted week happens before we build the page for exactly that reason, and if it comes back small the right answer is that you should not buy it.

The status field might go unused. It is one more thing to do for the person this build exists to unload, and it will get flipped on quiet mornings and skipped on busy ones. That is why the money rests on the pictures and why the field expires back to your own sentence.

The expensive mistake runs opposite to instinct. Your unconditional refund is more generous than either competitor policy we could read, and it is an asset that also sets the direction here. A page saying flying when it is marginal costs goodwill and some counter time. A page saying holding at 9am on a day that clears by 11 turns a paid seat into a refund plus a seat you can no longer sell. That is why the field carries the current hour only, and why it times out fast.
