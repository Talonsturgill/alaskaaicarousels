Every camera is labelled twice

The site it sits on, and where that site sits relative to the route you fly. That second label is the whole point of the page. A clear picture of the valley is not a clear picture of a mountain tens of miles away, and a guest who reads a clear valley camera as a green light for a Summit Tour has been handed exactly the promise this build exists to avoid.

Camera health here is subtraction on two timestamps, which is a thing code can be tested on exhaustively.

The 30 minute cutoff is a number you set. It is not a number we decided for you.

Watch it clear itself

Set a status and the card stamps the clock time and starts a countdown. In this demonstration the clock runs at one minute a second and the status clears after 30 minutes, so you can watch it happen in half a minute. The real timeout is longer and it is a number we set with you.

When it clears, the page falls back to your own sentence about calling on the day, with your own phone number under it. That is the safety property, and it is the reason this is worth shipping. The worst this page can ever do, on the worst morning, with every feed dead and nobody having touched anything, is exactly what your website does today.

Careful with that one. It is the expensive direction, and it runs against instinct. Your refund is unconditional at 100 percent, so a page reading Flying on a marginal morning costs goodwill and some counter time. A guest who reads Holding at 9 AM and stands down on a day that clears by 11 turns a paid seat into a refund plus a seat you can no longer sell, on an aircraft that seats ten.

That asymmetry is why this field carries the current hour only, never a later tour block, and why it times out fast rather than sitting there going stale.

We would rather tell you the weak part

This field gets flipped on calm mornings and goes untouched on the busy marginal ones, which are the mornings guests look hardest. Its usefulness runs opposite to the need for it. That is why none of the money in the study rests on it, and why the case rests on the pictures, which need nobody to do anything and work hardest on the worst days.

Press Nobody touches it all morning to see the state this page will spend most of its life in. That state is the design rather than the failure.

HOW IT SITS IN YOUR OPERATION

Three steps, and a person stays on the trigger in all three

01

Nobody has to do anything

The pictures, the observation and the forecast arrive on their own, and every one of them carries the time it arrived. If your counter never opens the status control for a whole season, the page still works and still stamps everything.

02

A person sets the one word

When someone does set the status, the page publishes what that person set and the clock time they set it. No prediction, no later tour block, no opinion about a picture. It is a person reporting, with their timestamp attached.

03

It expires back to your sentence

Left alone it clears itself, and the page returns to "please call us the day you are flying to inquire about weather" with your number under it. The go, no go call stays at your counter, after the briefing, where you put it.

WHAT THIS BUILD REFUSES TO DO

The refusals are as much of the product as the page is

  • No verdict on whether a flight will go. Not on the page, not in a notification, not anywhere. An independent 2025 benchmark ran 147,182 images from 444 FAA camera sites and found no model reaching the FAA's own accuracy threshold, with the best averaging about 1.8 statute miles of error.
  • No forecast wearing a status field's clothes. Current hour only. A status set on a 2 PM block at 10 AM is a prediction published on your domain under your name.
  • No opinion about a picture. A stale camera is stale by the clock. Deciding in software whether an image looks flyable is the exact place a model sneaks into a build described as model free.
  • No visibility number presented as yours. Anything derived stays attributed to whoever derived it, with its error attached, or it gets dropped.
  • No advance cancellation notice. Your published policy is that you do not notify in advance. Automating one turns a policy into a promise and manufactures refunds when it is wrong.
  • No price, fee, tax or duration figure produced by a model. Every one of them is a published rule and every one is checkable by the customer.
  • No chatbot, no assistant, no voice agent, on the site or on the phone. The highest frequency guest question is whether it is flying today, which is the one question you deliberately route to a person after a briefing.
