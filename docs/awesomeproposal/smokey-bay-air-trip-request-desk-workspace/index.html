    Build plan, alternatives, and dependencies

    Where AI earns its place

    Reads a guest's request, picks the matching published trip information and writes a reply draft, listing what is missing. It does not price, schedule, accept payment or decide weather.

    The systems it needs

    Proposed first slice: a private web workspace where staff paste a request and approve the draft. No link to Smokey Bay Air's booking or scheduling systems is assumed because they are not public.

    The human decision stays here

    Staff review and send every reply from the company mailbox. The desk never sends anything.

    Buying, configuring, or building

    Tourism chatbots exist and the retained cases show them answering enquiries. Those cases do not state whether they draft staff-approved replies or list missing weights and dates, so that difference is unconfirmed. This slice is built around a staff-approved draft in the company's own words.

    Saved email replies

    Free and fast to start. Staff still pick the right text and spot what the guest left out. Good baseline, so the pilot compares against it.

    A tourism chatbot

    Answers guests instantly, as in the retained cases. The retained cases show enquiry answering; they do not state drafting for staff approval or listing missing weights and dates, so that difference is unconfirmed. Consider later, once the desk's drafts prove which questions repeat.

    Dependencies

    Staff share 30 recent anonymised guest requests and the replies they sent.

    Owner approves the reply wording and the weather terms.

    Research

    Why Smokey Bay Air

    Guests describe the trip they want. The desk drafts the reply your reservations team would write: what the trip involves, what Smokey Bay Air still needs from them, and how weather changes are handled. In this sample, the two menus stand in for the desk reading a guest's free text. A person approves every send.

    Smokey Bay Air takes bear viewing and village flight requests by form and phone, and each needs passenger names, weights and dates before a confirmation. That is a repeatable drafting job built on pages the company already publishes.

    What we verified and what we are proposing

    verifiedThe company flies multiple times daily year-round from Homer to Seldovia, Port Graham and Nanwalek, which are off the road system, and offers village pick-up and drop-off in Homer for $14 per trip.[2][3]

    verifiedBear viewing trips to Katmai or Lake Clark take about five hours and depend on tides and weather; if the company cancels for weather guests can take a full refund or reschedule on a space available basis.[4]

    verifiedReservation requests come by form or phone and need passenger names, weights, travel dates and desired activity, with a follow-up confirmation email.[1]

    vendor reportedBlack Cat Cruises, a New Zealand cruise operator, deployed a Yonder chatbot named Kiri to give instant answers about trips during peak departures, steering guests to the right cruise, and staff also used it for internal questions.[5]

    vendor reportedThe International Antarctic Centre deployed a Yonder chatbot; within 90 days it handled 40 times more conversations than Facebook Messenger alone, about 10% of chats clicked through to booking, and staff time was freed from manual replies.[6]
