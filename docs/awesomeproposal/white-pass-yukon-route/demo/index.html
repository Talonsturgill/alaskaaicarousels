Why the wording is locked. Your cancellation terms carry two clauses that are opposites separated by a preposition, up to 24 hours prior and within 24 hours. Anything that paraphrases those can swap them, and that is a money misstatement on your own site. In Moffatt v. Air Canada the tribunal held the airline liable for what the chatbot on its own site said about a refund policy. So this receipt quotes your governed policy file word for word or says nothing at all, and it never reaches a conclusion about money.

The two clocks. The guest's local time comes from their own device, which on a cruise is often still set to the port they flew into. Alaska Time is what the server wrote, and the server write is the record. Nobody can edit it afterwards, including us.

The sort is arithmetic on two timestamps. Nothing here scores a request, ranks it by importance or predicts anything. The cutoff is the departure time minus 24 hours, the arrival time is what the server wrote, and the difference is the sort key. The same inputs give the same order every time, which is exactly why this part earns no model.

Your decision, not ours. Whether a request stamped before the cutoff is honoured at that stamp is a policy call only White Pass can make. The five day spike puts that answer in writing before any code is written, and if the answer is no, the build does not start.

How it sits in your operation

Three steps, and a person is on every one that matters

01

The guest writes it down, at any hour

Your form, an out of hours greeting on 1-800-343-7373 and a timestamped copy of anything sent to info@wpyr.com all write one queue. The server stamps the arrival time before anything else runs, so the record that they tried exists whether or not anybody was at a desk.

02

It waits, and nothing acts on it

Nothing is answered, nothing is decided, nothing is written to your ticketing system. The queue holds the request and its stamp, in the order that matters. Your ticketing vendor is absent from this design on purpose.

03

Your agent opens it Monday at 7:00 AM

They read the request, see where it sat against that guest's 24 hour cutoff, and apply your cancellation policy the way they do today. The desk records what they decided, and that record is the only thing this software ever writes.

Deciding whether a guest gets their money back stays with your agent and your policy. We're not automating that, in this phase or any later one.

The one model this roadmap ever proposes is a single call on one node, sorting unstructured inbound email, built off season and gated on a season of your own labelled examples. No corpus, no phase two, nothing billed.
