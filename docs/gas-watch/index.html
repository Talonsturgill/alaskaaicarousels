What you are looking at

The reserve

Southcentral Alaska keeps gas in an underground field near Kenai, run by CINGSA. Utilities fill it in summer and draw on it in winter. The level is published once a day and never archived, so this page reads it and keeps the history.

The demand

How much gas the region burns is not published at all. It tracks how cold it is, so we model it from the Anchorage forecast and show the formula and its errors rather than asking anyone to take it on faith.

The gap

Subtract what came out of storage from what the region likely burned, and what is left came from somewhere nobody reports daily. That residual is the number no other source publishes.

This page will never tell you whether the lights stay on

It publishes what is measured, what is modeled, and what is missing. It does not publish a verdict. A compressor failure or a sanded well can produce curtailment on a day these numbers looked comfortable, and supply side deliverability is not public, so no adequacy conclusion can honestly be drawn from what is here. Anyone using this data to say the region is fine, or that it is not, is using it wrong.

What is not measured by anyone

Demand equals field production plus storage withdrawal. Withdrawal is measured, demand is modeled, and everything not CINGSA falls out by subtraction. On September 1st, 2026 that residual was 155.1 MMcf per day against modeled demand of 143, or 108.5 percent, all from sources no public feed reports daily. It exceeds demand because the field was filling, so some went to storage rather than a burner. That ratio is the hole in the public record, and why this page draws no adequacy conclusion.

Strictly it is production plus any Hilcorp storage movement, which public data can't separate, so the field is named non_cingsa_supply and never production.

The model, in full

Regional demand in MMcf per day is 80.868 plus 3.439 times heating degree days, base 65 Fahrenheit. Version 2.0. Fitted by least squares to 131 months of observed Alaska deliveries, refit on each new one, and off by 6.82 percent on average.

It sits below the published planning figures, as expected. A design day of 390 MMcf per day is a margin to build against, not a prediction, and the gap is published rather than tuned away.

Every figure below is recomputed from 4,599 days of observed Anchorage degree days, January 1st, 2014 to August 4th, 2026. Nothing here is a number somebody typed.

  1. The model evaluated at the temperature behind the published design day, for comparison against that planning figure. The model returns 338.8 MMcf/d.

  2. The coldest day in the committed Anchorage record, and what the model asks of the system on that day. Recomputed from the HDD history, never typed. The model returns 345.7 MMcf/d.

  3. The 2025 to 2026 gas year integrated day by day from the committed record. Season bounds are July 1st through June 30th. The model returns 65.8 Bcf.

  4. Mean modeled demand across every day in the committed record, against the published average day figure. The model returns 169.8 MMcf/d.

Across that record the model averages 169.8 MMcf per day against the published average day of 190, which is 10.6 percent light. The coldest day in the record is February 2nd, 2024 at 77 degree days, which models to 345.7 MMcf per day. Of the 4,599 days on file, none model at or above the published design day. That is a fact about the weather, not a statement about whether the system coped.

Fitted to what Alaska burned

EIA publishes Alaska gas deliveries monthly. The model is fitted to 131 months of them and refits on each new one, so the estimate answers to measured consumption, not a design document. The figures are statewide and two months behind, so they correct the model, not the record here.

Through May 2026 Alaska held 30.0 Bcf of working gas across 5 storage fields, against 69.9 Bcf of capacity. Only CINGSA reports daily.

What is not reported daily

These are the things no public feed reports daily, and their absence is why this page publishes numbers rather than conclusions. Two of them do have monthly statewide figures, which is what the model above is fitted to. What no source gives is a daily regional number, and that is the gap that matters here.
