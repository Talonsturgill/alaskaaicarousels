# Alaska's SNAP Bill Reads a Year That Opened Thursday

## The story

Alaska's SNAP bill reads a year that opened Thursday.

Fiscal 2027 is read either way.

From fiscal 2029, a state's share uses its error rate from three fiscal years before. Fiscal 2030 reads fiscal 2027, opened October 1st, whichever start applies.

The first share moved on Thursday.

Washington's share of SNAP administration fell from 50 to 25 percent, so Alaska now pays 75. Legislative Finance estimates about $6.6 million more a year. The Anchorage Daily News reported nearly $11 million.

The second share is a staircase.

Under 6 percent, a state pays none of the benefit cost. At 10 or more, it pays 15 percent.

The record, against the statute's lines.

Alaska's 2025 rate, times 1.5, cleared 20 percent, which moved its start to fiscal 2029. Clear it again in 2026 and the start moves to 2030.

"COBOL on a mainframe."

How the state describes EIS, its legacy eligibility system. ARIES, the newer one, holds only MAGI Medicaid.

218 of 332 positions filled.

The yearly average left 34.3 percent vacant. "During periods when Alaska had significant workload backlogs, that had a larger impact on the state's error rate," the Department of Health wrote.

The fix is scheduled for 2028.

Milestone 2, estimated at $20 million, began in October 2025. Milestone 2.5, estimated at $5 million, would move SNAP data off the mainframe. The Department says the biggest gains wait for full modernization.

Save this for the day fiscal 2026's rate is published.

## What we verified

- Public Law 119-21 sets the federal share of state SNAP administrative costs at 50 percent through fiscal year 2026 and 25 percent from fiscal year 2027 onward. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10106](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- Alaska's Legislative Finance Division says federal fiscal year 2027 begins October 1st, 2026, when SNAP administrative cost sharing changes to 25 percent federal and 75 percent state. [Alaska Legislative Finance Division, Informational Paper 25-1](https://www.legfin.akleg.gov/InformationalPapers/25-1DPAEligibilityTechnicianHistory.pdf), primary document.
- Under Public Law 119-21, a state with a SNAP payment error rate under 6 percent pays none of the benefit cost once the state cost share applies, starting in fiscal year 2028. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- Under Public Law 119-21, a state with a payment error rate of at least 6 percent but under 8 percent pays 5 percent of SNAP benefit costs. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- Under Public Law 119-21, a state with a payment error rate of at least 8 percent but under 10 percent pays 10 percent of SNAP benefit costs. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- Under Public Law 119-21, a state with a payment error rate of 10 percent or more pays 15 percent of SNAP benefit costs. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- From fiscal year 2029 on, the law sets each state's SNAP benefit cost share using its payment error rate from the third fiscal year before. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- The law delays the benefit cost share to fiscal year 2029 for any state whose fiscal year 2025 payment error rate, multiplied by 1.5, is 20 percent or more. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
- The law moves the start to fiscal year 2030 for any state whose fiscal year 2026 payment error rate, multiplied by 1.5, is also 20 percent or more. [U.S. Congress, Public Law 119-21, sec. 10105](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.htm), primary document, 2025-07-04.
