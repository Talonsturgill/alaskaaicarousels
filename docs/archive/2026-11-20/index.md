# Two Land Rights Decided. One Still Open.

> "It's hard to look at that and not call this rushed," Sen. Cathy Giessel said of DNR's Alaska LNG lease decision.

Published 2026-11-20 by Alaska AI. 10 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-20/

## The story

Two land rights decided. One still open.

Two are pressed. One is still a circle.

DNR approved a 50-year North Slope data center lease and, on November 10th, published its decision conveying about 6,800 acres near Houston to AIDEA. Its proposed decision on Alaska LNG's 800-mile corridor still takes comment.

"It's hard to look at that and not call this rushed."

DNR is "doing our best to advance (the decision) as expeditiously as we can."

A full lease is not a permit to build.

DNR proposes turning the line's conditional leases into full ones. Its own text says conversion "does not grant AGDC permission to start construction," and every condition stays a gate at a later step.

Part of the case is in a sealed envelope.

DNR cites planned capitalization of more than $50 billion and letters of intent for more than 60 percent of capacity, which it calls non-binding. The Department of Revenue's economics analysis is confidential. Per ADN, Crowther acknowledged departing from DNR's normal process.

Little Troublesome Creek Campground. One of the existing uses the gas line lease must still address.

6,800 acres to AIDEA. The borough wants it elsewhere.

DNR's decision conveys about 6,800 acres, down from almost 20,000 first proposed. On November 13th the Mat-Su Borough asked DNR to reconsider and named Point MacKenzie.

Both decided over heavy opposition.

North Slope, a 50-year lease on about 715 acres for a 1 to 3 gigawatt data center, drew more than 1,200 responses, most opposed. Houston drew more than 2,300 comments, almost all opposed. Each decision now has a reconsideration window.

The windows, on one page.

Should DNR's decision on the gas line leases wait for the next governor?
