# A Better Ear Is Not a Recovery

> Cook Inlet has about 331 belugas left, and AI can finally hear them through the ship noise.

Published 2026-07-21 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-07-21/

## The story

Now a machine can hear them through the ship noise.

Two gates. One white whale.

NOAA Fisheries, Microsoft AI for Good and Alaska Fish and Game built a deep learning listener in two stages. Stage one flags a sound. Stage two names the species behind it.

Down almost 80 percent.

Cook Inlet's belugas are a genetically distinct population, cut off from every other Alaska stock. They fell from about 1,300 whales in 1979 to 279 by 2018, and were listed endangered in 2008.

A whale in a wall of noise.

Ocean noise is one of the biggest threats NOAA lists for these belugas. In a crowded inlet a single call can vanish under a passing ship. Hearing them at all is the first problem.

It learns the calls it used to miss.

The model uses contrastive audio language models and active learning. It sorts a sound into beluga, killer whale or humpback, flags the calls it is unsure of for a person to label, and firms up a daily read of where belugas are.

Ears below. Eyes above.

A separate NOAA led effort, with the U.S. Geological Survey, Microsoft AI for Good and the Naval Research Laboratory, is teaching a cloud model to spot the same whales in satellite images.

331 today. Still far below 1979.

The machine measures the whale. It cannot move it.

A sharper ear tells managers where and when the belugas are. It does not quiet the ships, refill the prey, or clear the inlet. The hunt has been closed since 2007, and the whales still have not recovered.

A better ear is not a recovery.

AI can finally hear Alaska's most endangered whale. What would it take to bring it back?

## What we verified

- A 2026 study titled "Adaptive Acoustic Monitoring for Endangered Cook Inlet Beluga Whales in Complex Soundscapes" was published in the journal Marine Mammal Science. [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref record)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- The Cook Inlet beluga population declined by nearly 80 percent between 1979 and 2018, from about 1,300 whales to an estimated 279. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- In 2008, the Cook Inlet beluga population was listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- In 2011, NOAA Fisheries designated two areas of Cook Inlet as critical habitat. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- NOAA Fisheries identifies five beluga whale stocks in Alaskan waters, and the Cook Inlet stock is geographically isolated from the others. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- NOAA lists threats to Cook Inlet belugas including ocean noise, prey limitations, habitat impacts, contaminants, predation, strandings, disease and changing climate conditions. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- Although the subsistence hunt has been suspended since 2007, the Cook Inlet beluga population has not recovered as expected. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- The Cook Inlet beluga whale is managed as a Distinct Population Segment (DPS). [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
- The paper was published online first on June 7, 2026, with a print issue dated July 2026. [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref record)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- The study was a NOAA Fisheries, Microsoft AI for Good, and Alaska Department of Fish and Game collaboration; authors include Verena Gill (NOAA Fisheries, Anchorage), Manuel Castellote and Rahul Dodhia and Juan M. Lavista Ferres (Microsoft AI for Good Research Lab), and Lori Polasek (Alaska Department of Fish and Game). [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref record)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- The framework is a deep-learning dual-stage model that separates signal detection from species classification. [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref abstract)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- The model expands beyond belugas to a multi-species context that also includes killer whales and humpback whales. [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref abstract)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- Using contrastive audio-language models and active learning, the framework improved detection of rare species occurrences and strengthened confidence in daily beluga presence estimates across Cook Inlet regions. [Marine Mammal Science (Crossref abstract)](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/mms.70213), report, 2026-06-07.
- NOAA leads a separate collaborative effort with the U.S. Geological Survey, Microsoft AI for Good, the Naval Research Laboratory and other partners to detect Cook Inlet belugas in satellite imagery. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/alaska/science-data/satellite-monitoring-cook-inlet-beluga-whales), report, background.
- The satellite initiative aims to build a secure cloud-based application that uses machine learning to detect whales and other marine mammals in very high-resolution (VHR) satellite imagery. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/alaska/science-data/satellite-monitoring-cook-inlet-beluga-whales), report, background.
- The best current abundance estimate for the Cook Inlet beluga population is 331 whales, suggesting the population may be stabilizing and possibly increasing. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale), report, background.
