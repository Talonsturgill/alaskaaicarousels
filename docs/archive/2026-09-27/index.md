# A Bigger Tank, Not a Bigger Engine. Bradley Lake and 2030

> Fairbanks is burning 250,000 gallons of diesel a day, AEA's Curtis Thayer says.

Published 2026-09-27 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-09-27/

## The story

About 38 percent more electricity. Its dam is slated for 2030.

A bigger tank, not a bigger engine.

A new diversion dam would send Dixon Glacier meltwater through a 4.6-mile tunnel into Bradley Lake and raise its pool about 16 feet. Capacity barely moves. The plant gets more water to spend.

4.6 miles of tunnel to carry glacier water.

AEA estimates the cost at about $400 million and expects construction to start in 2027 if federal approvals come through. FERC has the application under project P-8221-124.

It buys energy, not megawatts.

Today, Dixon's meltwater runs down the Martin River.

"There's nothing for tomorrow. There's nothing for next year."

Natural gas fuels about 80 percent of Southcentral's power generation, per KTUU. Fairbanks is burning 250,000 gallons of diesel a day, Thayer said. "They never did that last year."

The AI pitch runs on the same calendar.

The Air Force offered twelve parcels, about 4,700 acres, at JBER, Clear and Eielson for AI data centers. Its solicitation said the JBER sites had insufficient power and needed a new Chugach connection.

What exists, and what is only filed.

Until the glacier water arrives, should Railbelt AI data centers bring their own power?

## What we verified

- Bradley Lake has 120 MW of installed capacity. [Alaska Energy Authority](https://www.akenergyauthority.org/What-We-Do/Railbelt-Energy/Bradley-Lake-Hydroelectric-Project), primary document, 2026-09-27.
- AEA filed its Bradley Lake expansion amendment application with FERC on June 30th under project number P-8221-124. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- The expansion would build a new diversion dam to send Dixon Glacier meltwater and runoff into Bradley Lake. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- The expansion would raise Bradley Lake's normal maximum operating pool by about 16 feet. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- The expansion would add a 0.3 MW micro-hydro turbine in one of Bradley Lake Dam's fishwater release valves. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- The expansion would raise Bradley Lake's installed capacity from 119.7 MW to 120.0 MW. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- FERC's notice says the expansion would raise Bradley Lake's average annual power generation by approximately 38 percent. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- FERC set the comment deadline on the amendment application for August 28th at 5 p.m. Eastern. [Federal Register (FERC notice, FR Doc 2026-15691)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-03/html/2026-15691.htm), primary document, 2026-08-03.
- The Dixon diversion's estimated construction cost is about $400 million. [Alaska Energy Authority](https://www.akenergyauthority.org/What-We-Do/Railbelt-Energy/Bradley-Lake-Hydroelectric-Project/Dixon-Diversion-Project), primary document, 2026-09-27.
- AEA estimates three years of study and permitting followed by three years of construction. [Alaska Energy Authority](https://www.akenergyauthority.org/What-We-Do/Railbelt-Energy/Bradley-Lake-Hydroelectric-Project/Dixon-Diversion-Project), primary document, 2026-09-27.
- The Dixon diversion would be located five miles southwest of Bradley Lake. [Alaska Energy Authority](https://www.akenergyauthority.org/What-We-Do/Railbelt-Energy/Bradley-Lake-Hydroelectric-Project/Dixon-Diversion-Project), primary document, 2026-09-27.
