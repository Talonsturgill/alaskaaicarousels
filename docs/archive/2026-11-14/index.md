# Who watches Alaska's fishing boats in 2027

> In 2027 camera hardware moves into Alaska's fisheries monitoring budget while the AI that could review the video waits on staff.

Published 2026-11-14 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-14/

## The story

This camera watches an Alaska fishing boat. Who watches the footage?

One review provider, priced by the hard drive.

NMFS prices camera review by the hard drive, a year of review spending divided by drives reviewed. The program currently has one EM review provider.

Congress paid for the cameras. That money is expected to end this year.

Since 2023, Congressional Directed Spending covered new EM systems and replacements. With it spent by the end of 2026, the 2027 plan carries that cost itself, about $6,002.96 a year per fixed-gear camera boat.

A preliminary $5.45M, with cameras off the top.

Trawl cameras come off the top at $732K. About $4.72M is left for observers and fixed-gear cameras, subject to change.

Nobody is chosen.

Boats under 40 feet and jig boats have no chance of carrying an observer or a camera in 2027. The draft expects 1,427 such trips.

Some trips still carry a person.

In the draft, an observer rides 79 of 253 expected fixed-gear trips in the Bering Sea and Aleutians, 31.35 percent. In the Gulf it is 174 of 1,868, or 9.31 percent.

Who gets watched in 2027.

The AI that could review the video is on a list.

NMFS intends to support it in 2027, "pending availability of staff resources." The title is in the draft. The staffing is pending.

Should the AI review line get staff before the fleet pays for more cameras?

The final plan goes to the Council, meeting November 30th through December 9th.
