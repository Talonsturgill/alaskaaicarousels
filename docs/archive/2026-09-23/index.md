# What Mat-Su's Plate Reader Ban Actually Reaches

> Five agencies police the Matanuska-Susitna Borough. The borough is not one of them.

Published 2026-09-23 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-09-23/

## The story

Mat-Su banned plate readers the night it was heard.

ADOPTED UNANIMOUSLY. IN FORCE ON ADOPTION.

The borough wrote down what the machine is.

The chapter defines it as one or more high-speed camera units, alone or combined with algorithms and optical character recognition, turning images of plates, makes, models, colors and unique vehicle identifiers into computer-readable data.

The subject never changes.

No Borough department, division, employee, or agent

It can't collect the data. It can't be handed it.

"The Borough and its departments are prohibited from accessing, querying, uploading data to, or receiving data from any ALPR operator, network, database, or private surveillance network."

"The borough does not have police powers."

That is the borough's own sentence, on its own site. Some of its cities do have them. The state troopers patrol here.

Five agencies police this boundary.

ABOUT 117,400 PEOPLE LIVE INSIDE THIS LINE, PER STATE LABOR ESTIMATES FOR 2025

The operator it names is the borough itself.

An ALPR Operator, in the chapter's own words, is any Borough department, division, employee, or third-party contractor acting under contract with the Borough. Eight borough officials signed the routing block between August 19th and August 21st, weeks before it reached the floor. On September 15th, another assembly took up the same question.

One body wrote the limit. The other kept drafting.

Anchorage Assembly members voted unanimously to postpone their own police surveillance ordinance, AO 2026-108(S), to their regular meeting on October 20th, the Anchorage Daily News reported. The paper quoted one member asking for stronger language and stronger protections.

A limit written before the power exists.

Mat-Su bound its own departments, its own money and its own contracts, unanimously, for a machine it has no police powers to run.
