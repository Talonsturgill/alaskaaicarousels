# UPDATE. Bristol Bay's newest salmon counter listens

> On the Wood River a person on a tower clicks each passing salmon by hand, and on the Naknek near King Salmon a sonar will listen.

Published 2026-11-17 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-17/

## The story

On the Naknek, the newest salmon counter listens.

A federal grant puts an automated sonar near King Salmon.

BBRSDA announced the NFWF award of $188,268 on October 29th, National Fisherman reports. The sonar is meant to count returning salmon automatically and feed an online dashboard. The Naknek is its first site. The grant runs June 1st to May 31st, 2028.

It listens across a wide stretch of the river.

"Sonar lets us count fish across a wide stretch of the Naknek," says Lilani Dunn of BBRSDA. SeaSignal Technologies, working with Coastal Measures and BioSonics, was picked to build and run it, per National Fisherman.

From the riverbed to a screen, during the season.

Across the bay, most counts are still made by hand.

Since 1955, a person and a hand clicker.

Most Bristol Bay escapement counts come from nine Fish and Game tower sites. Crews rotate through shifts around the clock and click each passing salmon in 10 minute intervals.

45.7 million sockeye came back to Bristol Bay this year. 15.6 million made it upriver to spawn.

One fishery, three ways to count.

NOAA and NFWF's 2026 slate funded 13 grants, $3.4 million, with $4.2 million matched. As of July, Fish and Game wasn't directly involved in the drone pilot.

Wood River's tower counted 2,586,432 sockeye this year.

Fish and Game's goal there is 700,000 to 2,800,000. The Kvichak, Naknek, Alagnak, Egegik and Wood were all inside their goals. The drone pilot is set to be compared against tower counts like this one.

Should the Naknek's machine count go live in season, or be checked against a tower first?
