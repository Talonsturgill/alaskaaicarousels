Who Sets the Clock on a Plate Read

In the Anchorage mayor's version of the surveillance ordinance, a plate data "limit" is a reason, not a number.

The story

The city's plate reader policy must limit storage "based on articulable operational needs." The sponsors' S-1 text names no number either.

Flock says it began installing plate readers for a private customer the week before October 5th. A crowdsourced map showed eight at two Anchorage Lowe's stores.

In the August 18th original, "limit" means 14 days and then deletion.

APD says it has no access to plate readers at privately owned locations.

Whatever "limit" means after October 20th, it will govern city readers, not store readers.

The three texts' clocks, the September definition built on AI, APD's 14-day policy and the store readers none reach.

Should Anchorage's plate data rules ever reach a reader on private property?
