# Update. The Plan Got 250,000. The Aircraft Got More

> Alaska's rural health program awarded $250,000 for a drone plan on August 14th. On September 4th it awarded $4,171,115 for aircraft.

Published 2026-09-14 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-09-14/

## The story

THE AWARD SAYS IMPLEMENTATION NOT PLANNING

Ryan Air was awarded $4,171,115 to deploy "autonomous cargo drones and electric vertical take-off and landing aircraft" carrying pharmaceuticals and moving patients across roadless communities. IA means aircraft, not a study.

A FAMILY FREIGHT LINE WITH A GRANT

Ryan Air started in 1953 in the village of Unalakleet and is managed by the third generation of the Ryan family. It serves 80 villages throughout Western Alaska and operates 24 aircraft out of eight hubs.

AIRCRAFT TAKE THE PLACE OF AMBULANCES

402 Alaskan communities depend on aviation for year round access, where aircraft often take the place of cars, school buses, semitrucks, and ambulances. That is 82 percent of the state's communities, served by 235 airports. Ryan Air's own proposal calls the gap "life-threatening".

ONE PROGRAM. TWO LOADS.

THE INTERIOR GOT A YEAR TO PLAN

Tanana Chiefs Conference took $250,000 for a planning year on the same idea, a drone pharmaceutical route from a Fairbanks hub to 42 Interior villages across 94,000 square miles. Tanana Chiefs says winter medication delays of 5 to 14 days are common.

FIRST THE WAIVER THEN THE AIRCRAFT

Tanana Chiefs names the University of Alaska Fairbanks Alaska Center for Unmanned Aircraft Systems Integration as its partner on FAA beyond visual line of sight waivers. ACUASI was selected as one of the first seven FAA unmanned aircraft test sites.

TEN ROWS NAME A MACHINE

THE ROUNDS ARE NOT FINISHED

Roughly $65 million is still expected to be awarded ahead of a federal deadline of October 30th. The next Friday is the next row.
