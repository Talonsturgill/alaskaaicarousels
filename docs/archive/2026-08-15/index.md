# A Plan, a Study, and No Published Due Date

> At 10:00 a.m. on August 5th, a Senate committee in room SR-253 took up five bills. Three carry AI or a chatbot in the title.

Published 2026-08-15 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-08-15/

## The story

The first federal answer to talking toys is a plan and a study.

Two agencies owe a plan. One academy owes a study.

Per Senator Duckworth's release, the Federal Trade Commission and the Consumer Product Safety Commission must give Congress a coordinated, actionable plan on AI-enabled toy products. The National Academies must run a comprehensive study.

The due date is not on the record.

The text of S. 5171 returned a 403 on August 15th, so no deadline for the plan or for the study could be read. The committee's own meeting page carries the agenda and no tally for any of the five bills.

Five bills that morning. Three of them AI.

The committee met at 10:00 a.m. on August 5th in room SR-253, entering through SR-254. Three Coast Guard nomination lists were on the same agenda. Senator Duckworth's office announced that day that S. 5171 passed.

Knives, pills and matches.

"discussing sexually explicit content and explaining where to find knives, pills and matches in the home"

The Alaskan on the bill is buying knowledge.

Murkowski is the Republican cosponsor named in the announcement. Read fairly, the bill buys knowledge and writes no rule, which is the normal opening move for a technology nobody has measured yet. Whether that is enough is the argument.

A plan is not a rule. A study is not a standard.

Alaska's own record, as one column tells it.

House Bill 47 provisions criminalizing AI-generated child sexual abuse material and deepfake harassment became law in June.

Five places the record was asked. One answered.

Save this for the next time a talking toy shows up in your house.
