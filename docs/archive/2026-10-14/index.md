# Software Can Read the Tape. The Camera Is the Cost.

> "Should not have to carry one less crew member," the North Pacific Council's Advisory Panel said of boats sent from cameras to observers.

Published 2026-10-14 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-10-14/

## The story

Software can read the tape. 76 boats would still lose the camera.

172 camera boats. 76 would move.

39 took no trips in three years. 37 more averaged under 3 trips a year. The other boats stay on camera.

Under 3 trips a year, the camera goes to a boat that fishes.

NMFS's draft starts the yearly check with the 2028 plan, at least 9 trips over 3 years. In 2027, 32 boats that filed no monitoring plan since 2024 go to observers first.

Monitored trips get cheaper.

About $96,000 shifts to boats that fish.

96 of 97

Pacific sleeper sharks, found on longline camera video by a machine-learning model.

Reading the tape is the cheap part.

NMFS calls the camera gear a high buy-in, about $6,003 a boat a year, while reviewing a selected trip adds little. It intends to support scaling the AI tools in 2027, if staff are available.

The Gulf's fixed-gear catch is the least watched.

NMFS expects 1,010 Gulf fixed-gear camera trips in 2027, against 92 in the Bering Sea, and plans to review 154 of the Gulf's.

A camera takes no bunk. An observer does.

The panel said a boat moved to observers shouldn't have to leave a crew member ashore. The Council asked NMFS to consider active boats that can prove they have no bunk for one.

3 trips a year to keep a camera. Fair line, or wrong test?

Final rates are expected in December.
