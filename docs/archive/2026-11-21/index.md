# The Senate bill names Alaska once.

> One subsection. That is the only place the Senate's energy bill names Alaska.

Published 2026-11-21 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-21/

## The story

The Senate bill names Alaska once.

Washington needs the RCA's yes.

New Federal Power Act section 224(d) has the Energy Secretary weigh congestion inside Alaska with the State and affected Tribes. It may name an Alaska area only "subject to the approval of the Regulatory Commission of Alaska."

Everywhere else, comments. In Alaska, a yes.

For every other area the Secretary may name congestion after taking comments from affected States and Tribes. No state commission approves. The approval step appears only in the Alaska subsection.

Support thinned. It still passed.

On passage, 47 Republicans, 14 Democrats and 1 independent voted yea. Murkowski and Sullivan both voted yea.

"No subsidy of data centers."

The section's own heading, further down the same text.

A data center's added cost stays on its own bill.

No part of the incremental cost of serving one may be charged to another customer. Recovery continues if it quits early, and the utility collects financial assurance first.

As introduced, 20 megawatts. As passed, no line at all.

S. 5653, introduced September 30th, counted load at 20 megawatts or more. The Senate-passed section 229 defines a data center by what it houses, artificial intelligence included, with no megawatt floor.

Three tools, if a State picks them up.

They apply to sites that connect on or after enactment. "State" includes a State commission, and the section expands no one's jurisdiction.

It heads to the House.

Should the RCA, not Washington, decide where Alaska's grid is congested?
