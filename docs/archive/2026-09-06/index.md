# Run Both Instruments, or Lose the Record

> When a machine takes a ship's place on a fish survey, how do you tell a change in the fish from a change in the instrument?

Published 2026-09-06 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-09-06/

## The story

Two instruments on the same line, for years. That's the bill.

The new instrument is allowed to be different.

The framework calls for "incremental incorporation and calibration of new technologies" rather than "a wholesale replacement of existing methods that would disrupt the historical baseline of a time series" (C05). The most common way to do it is to run the old method and the new one in parallel for a fixed period (C07).

Ground the net can't cross.

The camera can see it. It can't hold it.

The framework's own figure for the share of total Alaska seabed area that bottom trawl survey gear contacts, offered against calls to ban extractive sampling (C08).

The framework lists funding, vessel availability, new sampling objectives and new technology as human forces that break a time series (C09). NOAA says fish populations have shifted into deeper water or farther north where surveys have not traditionally been conducted (C12).

On September 15th, 2026 the North Pacific Council's Partial Coverage Fishery Monitoring Advisory Committee reviews the draft 2027 Annual Deployment Plan (C22, C23). The 2026 plan defines a no selection pool, vessels with no probability of carrying an observer or an electronic monitoring system on any trip that season (C28).

The draft 2027 Annual Deployment Plan had not yet been posted on the meeting page as of September 6th, 2026 (C25). The Federal Register notice says public comment letters will be accepted and should be submitted electronically through the meeting page (C24).

## What we verified

- NOAA Fisheries published a feature story on the future of fisheries-independent surveys on September 3rd, 2026. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/charting-future-fisheries-independent-surveys), primary document, 2026-09-03.
- Stan Kotwicki of the Alaska Fisheries Science Center is the lead author of the new fisheries-independent survey framework. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/charting-future-fisheries-independent-surveys), primary document, 2026-09-03.
- The framework paper carries 23 named authors. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework was published in the ICES Journal of Marine Science, Volume 83 Issue 7, on July 16th, 2026. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework's stated conclusion is that survey modernization works best through incremental incorporation and calibration rather than wholesale replacement. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework states that consistency in the information the survey data products provide matters more than consistency in survey methods. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework says the most common way to implement incremental survey change is to run old and new methods in parallel for a fixed period. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework reports that Alaska bottom trawl fisheries-independent surveys contact less than 0.006 percent of total seabed area. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- The framework lists funding, vessel availability, new sampling objectives and new technology among the anthropogenic forces that can break the continuity of a survey time series. [ICES Journal of Marine Science, Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/icesjms/article/83/7/fsag134/8735857), primary document, 2026-07-16.
- Kotwicki said the new tools have the potential to alleviate some challenges facing the survey enterprise and that maintaining data consistency and continuity is critical. - Kotwicki said the research recommends prioritizing calibration and comparison when implementing new methods. [NOAA Fisheries](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/charting-future-fisheries-independent-surveys), primary document, 2026-09-03.
