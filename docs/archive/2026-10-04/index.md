# Ten Minutes of Every Hour, The Count an AI Drone Must Beat

> "The results have exceeded my expectations," Norm Van Vactor said in July of his Wood River AI drone pilot.

Published 2026-10-04 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-10-04/

## The story

Togiak missed by 702 fish. Its tower counted 10 minutes an hour.

Ten minutes, times six.

Bristol Bay's towers count each bank for 10 minutes of every hour, then expand. Crews work around the clock with hand clickers. At six to one, the Togiak's 119,298 rests on roughly 19,883 fish someone saw.

The error bar runs three times the miss.

ADF&G put the average sampling CV across nine Bristol Bay tower projects at 0.02 in 2004 and 2005. On the Togiak's 119,298 that is about 2,400 fish either way. It assumes no miscounts inside the ten minutes.

The rule was tested on 12 to 80 hours.

In 1965 and 1966, crews counted whole hours and compared them with the first 10 minutes, expanded. Errors ran generally under 10 percent, with no significant bias.

Eight towers counted 12.3 million of 15.6 million.

A box opens. A drone takes off.

A computer vision model is learning to pick out the salmon in its footage.

A key press for every fish.

Technicians press a key each time a fish crosses a line drawn on the footage. The first phase logged 256 flights and 17,552 images, stitched into 117 orthomosaics. The project lead says it spots fish 12 to 18 hours before the tower.

Matching the tower can't be the bar.

The pilot plans to test its model against Fish and Game tower counts next. Tower sampling error averaged about 2 percent in 2004 and 2005. The team's goal is a count with a stated confidence. Write the margin first.

Before the Wood River test runs, who should set the passing margin?

[Salmonid Field Protocols Handbook (American Fisheries Society), Tower Counts chapter by Carol Ann Woody](https://wildsalmoncenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/SFPH-Chapter-11-Tower-Counts.pdf), report, 2007-01-01.

- The most common counting tower design in Alaska is nonreplicated systematic sampling of 10 minutes per hour. [Salmonid Field Protocols Handbook (American Fisheries Society), Tower Counts chapter by Carol Ann Woody](https://wildsalmoncenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/SFPH-Chapter-11-Tower-Counts.pdf), report, 2007-01-01.
- Before the season a random number from 1 to 6 is drawn to pick which 10-minute interval of each hour gets counted for the rest of the season. [Salmonid Field Protocols Handbook (American Fisheries Society), Tower Counts chapter by Carol Ann Woody](https://wildsalmoncenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/SFPH-Chapter-11-Tower-Counts.pdf), report, 2007-01-01.
- In the handbook's example a counter who draws interval 2 counts at ten minutes past each hour, starting at ten past noon and repeating every hour. - The standard 10-minute-per-hour tower design does not allow unbiased estimation of the variance of the total escapement estimate. [Salmonid Field Protocols Handbook (American Fisheries Society), Tower Counts chapter by Carol Ann Woody](https://wildsalmoncenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/SFPH-Chapter-11-Tower-Counts.pdf), report, 2007-01-01.
- Each paired-observer test pairing an inexperienced and an experienced counter consisted of eighteen 10-minute counts split across daylight, dusk and night.
