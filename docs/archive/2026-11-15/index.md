# UA regents moved the AI vote. Campus policies start first.

> In May, UA regents asked for a system AI statement. In November, they put campus AI policies ahead of the vote on it.

Published 2026-11-15 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-15/

## The story

Campus policies start first.

Regents pushed the AI vote to February.

The vote moved. The work didn't.

Meeting at UAF, regents postponed AI Engaged to their February meeting and told the AI Coordinating Council to start campus-specific AI policies in the interim, ADN reports.

A benchmark, not a law.

AI Engaged is a four-page statement of intent, ADN reports. It doesn't establish policy.

About 20 percent survived, Shier says.

Ben Shier, UA's chief information technology officer, wrote the first draft by prompting Claude with human ideas, "all fed to Claude." He says about 20 percent of that two-page draft survived human edits.

Nearly 200 students and staff asked for a brief statement written by humans.

UAA's Faculty Senate paused too.

Senators postponed their vote on a resolution to reject and replace AI Engaged until next month, ADN reports. Senate chair Andrea Dewees said, "I think it's probably going to be messy."

Campus policies start before the statement.

The statement would require each of the three universities to write its own AI policy. That work now starts first. A one-year clock for a system-wide policy report starts only at adoption.

The rules get drawn campus by campus.

UA says the statement keeps curriculum, academic integrity and faculty instructional judgment with UAA, UAF and UAS. Cooper says UA is "never going to have everyone on the same page with AI."
