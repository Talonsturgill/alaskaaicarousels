# Anchorage Kept the 14 Days in Policy, Not Code

> If you drive in Anchorage, a patrol car's plate reader can log your plate, and APD's own policy deletes it after 14 days.

Published 2026-11-05 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-11-05/

## The story

Notice over numbers.

The 14 days stays police policy, not code.

The surveillance rules passed 11 to 1.

An amendment to write a 14-day deletion deadline into city code was rejected. APD already deletes plate reader data after 14 days, under its own department policy.

"articulable operational needs"

Municipal Attorney Eva Gardner argued retention must stay adaptable on that standard, per Alaska's News Source. The substitute as filed September 15th used the same words and set no fixed number of days.

Thirty days of warning, now before the change.

APD is required to give the Assembly 30 days of advance notice by memo before a material policy change, and a change to data storage limits counts.

A number in code takes an ordinance to change. A number in APD policy takes a memo and 30 days' notice.

November 4th, in five votes

A committee to watch the policies, monthly, for a year.

At least four Assembly members, led by Kameron Perez-Verdia, meeting in public at least once a month, per the Anchorage Daily News. It can recommend changes, not discipline.

"machine learning, artificial intelligence, or similar technology"

That is how the substitute as filed defined surveillance technology. The Anchorage Daily News reports APD now relies more on tools including artificial intelligence used to aid investigations.

Should the 14 days live in code, or is 30 days' notice enough?
