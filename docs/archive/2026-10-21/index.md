# The State Flew the Kuskokwim. An AI Prospector Arrived.

> On June 23rd, 2024, four fixed-wing aircraft began flying the Kuskokwim for the state's McGrath survey.

Published 2026-10-21 by Alaska AI. 9 slides.
Canonical: https://alaskaaihq.com/archive/2026-10-21/

## The story

86,319 km of public survey lines. Now an AI prospector.

On October 20th, an AI prospect generator set up in Alaska.

Geomorphic AI formed Golden Dyke Exploration LLC, a wholly owned Alaska subsidiary. Its release says new state airborne surveys extend decades of USGS and state mapping. It names no Alaska property yet.

It packages the target, not the drill.

Geomorphic says its role is to generate and package targets, then advance them through joint ventures and earn-ins while keeping an interest. It doesn't fund or operate drilling. It hasn't said whether it uses these surveys.

Lines 400 meters apart, about 100 meters up.

Four fixed-wing aircraft flew McGrath over two summers, 86,319 line kilometers in all. A cesium sensor read the rock's magnetism. Two gamma-ray spectrometers read its radiation.

The McGrath block, read every 400 meters.

Five surveys, one scale.

Paid for by Earth MRI. Free to download.

Federal Earth MRI grants paid for the flying. The state posts the data under an open end-user license. DGGS says it will promote resource exploration of the central Kuskokwim.

Open input. Private output.

The flying, the grids and the license are public. A packaged target moves through private deals, with an interest kept. Geomorphic has named no Alaska property yet.

Should a target built on public surveys say which ones it used?
