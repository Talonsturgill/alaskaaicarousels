## The story

One award per state. Alaska is 552 Rhode Islands.

BY LAND AREA. U.S. CENSUS BUREAU. C23 C24

Fifty states. Fifty identical marks.

Every state is drawn at its true relative area through one projection. Every mark is the same size, because the rule is the same size. NSF anticipates ten awards per cycle.

Only one award per state or multi-state region will be made.

An institution may appear as a participant in at most one proposal.

Ten awards. One cycle.

NSF announced the program as a $100 million initiative. The solicitation puts anticipated funding at $40 million to $100 million. It expects a proposal to request $4 million to $12 million over five years.

Today, access to this infrastructure is highly uneven across the nation.

570,641

Alaska's land area. The largest of any state. About 552 times Rhode Island's 1,034 square miles.

Spreading it is the point.

Spreading a national program across the whole country is a defensible thing to want, and it is the reason the cap exists. It also makes the largest state and the smallest one the same unit of need.

82 days to the deadline.

Subsequent deadlines fall on the first Wednesday in November. A state that misses this cycle is not shut out, it waits a year. That is the difference between a ceiling and a cliff.

Save this. The deadline is November 4th.
